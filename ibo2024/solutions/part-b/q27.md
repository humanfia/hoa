# Part B — Task 27

*Using PCR to identify translocations, inversions, and duplications*

**Official answer: A — True; B — True; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

A PCR product is formed only when both primer-binding sites are on the same DNA molecule and their 3′ ends face toward the intervening region. In the wild type, these conditions are met for primer pairs (a+b) in gene H and (c+e) in gene K. The pair (c+d) cannot amplify because its primers point in the same direction, while (a+e) and (b+c) cannot amplify because their primers are on different chromosomes.

**A — True.** ID-1 has the two wild-type products, (a+b) and (c+e), but it also has products from (a+e) and (b+c). The latter pairs normally span chromosomes 1 and 5, so each product shows that a segment of gene H has become physically joined to a segment of gene K. Detection of both hybrid junctions is the pattern expected after reciprocal exchange between the two chromosomes, establishing a chromosome 1–5 translocation.

**B — True.** ID-2 retains the wild-type (a+b) and (c+e) bands and gains a (c+d) band. Primers (c) and (d) point in the same direction in the normal gene K and therefore cannot produce a wild-type amplicon. An inversion that reverses one of their binding regions makes the primers face one another, explaining the new band. The rearrangement involves the exon–intron boundary marked by primer (d). Because the correct junction of the depicted exons contributes to the enzyme's active site, disturbing this boundary changes the encoded product and makes that copy of the protein non-functional.

The embedded official explanation says at the end of B that **gene H** becomes non-functional. Figure 1 places both (c) and (d), and hence this inversion, in **gene K**; “gene H” is therefore an apparent labeling error. It does not affect the official truth value.

**C — False.** ID-3 shows a second (a+b) band above the wild-type (a+b) band. Larger DNA fragments migrate less far through a gel, so the higher band represents an amplicon longer than the wild-type interval. A deletion between (a) and (b) would instead shorten the product and produce a band that migrated farther down the gel. The observed increase in length is consistent with a duplication within that interval, not a deletion.

**D — False.** ID-1 has rearrangement-specific (a+e) and (b+c) products while retaining the wild-type (a+b) and (c+e) products. Thus it carries both intact and translocated homologous copies and is heterozygous for the translocation, not homozygous. ID-3 likewise has both a wild-type-sized (a+b) product and a larger rearranged (a+b) product, so ID-3 is heterozygous as stated. Because the claim incorrectly calls ID-1 homozygous, the whole statement is false.
