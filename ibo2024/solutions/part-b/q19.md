# Part B — Task 19

*Heat-ball and sting defenses of honeybees against hornets*

**Official answer: A — False; B — False; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** Figure 1B gives a *Vespa velutina* body mass of approximately $280\ \mathrm{mg}$ and a venom mass of approximately $5.2\ \mathrm{mg}$. Venom therefore accounts for

\[
\frac{5.2\ \mathrm{mg}}{280\ \mathrm{mg}}\times 100\% \approx 1.86\%,
\]

or about $1.9\%$ of body mass. This is well below the claimed $2.6\text{–}2.8\%$. There is a minor unit error in the official explanation: it reports the calculated result as “1.9 mg,” whereas the ratio is about **1.9%**. This does not affect the official False answer.

**B — False.** The mutant *A. mellifera* bees can still produce the $44^\circ\mathrm{C}$ heat ball; only their ability to sting is lost. Because they are otherwise described as equivalent to wild-type bees, their effect is represented by the no-sting, $44^\circ\mathrm{C}$ treatment. In that treatment, hornet survival falls from $1.0$ to about $0.75$, whereas survival at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$ remains near $1.0$; the temperature effect is significant ($p=0.004$). Thus heat alone kills roughly $25\%$ of the hornets in this experiment. *Am*def bees cannot kill as many hornets as bees that also sting, but the absolute claim that they cannot kill **any** is contradicted by the data.

**C — False.** The relevant comparison is between heat without a sting, which models *Am*def, and the *A. cerana* single-sting treatment, both at $44^\circ\mathrm{C}$. After three hours, survival is about $0.75$ with heat alone, corresponding to about $25\%$ mortality. With an *A. cerana* sting plus the same heat, survival is only about $0.10$, corresponding to about $90\%$ mortality. The ability to sting therefore makes *A. cerana* far more lethal under these conditions, so *Am*def does not kill hornets as efficiently.

**D — False.** At $44^\circ\mathrm{C}$, the single-sting survival curves for *A. cerana* and *A. mellifera* both fall to approximately $0.10$ and remain there through $180$ minutes. Hence each treatment kills about $90\%$ of the hornets over the stated three hours. The curves may differ slightly in their early time course, but their three-hour endpoints do not show that the same number of *A. cerana* bees kills more hornets than *A. mellifera* bees.
