# Part B — Task 14

*Aral Sea restoration, salinity tolerance, and species interactions*

**Official answer: A — False; B — True; C — True; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** Restoring a food chain requires organisms at complementary trophic levels, but an introduced fish need not consume phytoplankton directly. For example, phytoplankton can support zooplankton or benthic invertebrates, which can then be eaten by fish; some fish may instead eat other fish. Requiring every introduced fish species to graze on the primary producers would omit these intermediate trophic links and could unnecessarily reduce the food base that supports them. The word “must” therefore makes the statement false.

**B — True.** The graph associates the return of adult Z, beginning around 1990, with a salinity of roughly 30 mg/mL and subsequently with the strongly increasing, high-salinity trajectory. Adult W does not begin to return until around 2000, when the lower-salinity trajectory is only about 15–20 mg/mL. The presence and recovery of Z under saltier conditions than those in which W recovers indicate that Z has the greater tolerance of high salt concentrations.

**C — True.** The Northern basin receives freshwater from the Syr Darya, whereas little Amu Darya water reaches the Southern basin because much of it is diverted for agriculture. Greater freshwater input dilutes the Northern basin, while weak inflow and continued evaporation leave the Southern basin more saline; these contrasting outcomes correspond to the falling and rising salinity branches after the sea divided. Because W is the less salt-tolerant species, suitable conditions would be reached sooner in the less saline Northern Aral Sea, making an earlier return there more likely.

**D — False.** In the intended interpretation of the figure, the red introduction marker is followed almost immediately by a collapse of Z, while W initially remains abundant. An introduced organism that merely served as prey for Z, without also preying on Z, would add a food resource and does not explain that sharp decline. The observed response instead points to a harmful interaction with the introduced community, such as predation on Z or competition with it, so the official key rejects the proposed prey-only relationship.

There is an evidential limitation in D: the graph shows only a temporal association and the net response of Z. It cannot strictly rule out a mixed introduced community containing some prey of Z alongside predators or competitors whose harmful effects were stronger. Thus D is not logically falsified by the plotted data alone; the false designation above preserves the official answer and its intended inference.
