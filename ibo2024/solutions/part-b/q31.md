# Part B — Task 31

*Founder haplotypes, linkage disequilibrium, and recombination mapping*

**Official answer: A — True; B — True; C — False; D — True.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** For the two alleles in the statement, linkage disequilibrium can be written as

\[
D_{AB}=p(AB)-p(A)p(B),
\]

where \(p(AB)\) is the frequency of gametes carrying both alleles. The stated equality makes \(D_{AB}=0\), so the alleles are associated no more often than expected from their separate frequencies: they are in linkage equilibrium. Whether the alleles are phenotypically dominant is irrelevant to this population-genetic calculation.

**B — True.** Among the 200 present-day chromosomes carrying \(M^*\),

\[
p(R2\mid M^*)=\frac{188}{200}=0.94,
\qquad
p(Q1\mid M^*)=\frac{164}{200}=0.82.
\]

These are much larger than the corresponding frequencies of \(R1\!-\!M^*\) and \(Q2\!-\!M^*\). Indeed, even though the table gives only marginal pair counts, at least \(188+164-200=152\) of the 200 \(M^*\)-bearing chromosomes must carry both R2 and Q1. Under the intended founder-haplotype interpretation, the mutation therefore arose most likely on an R2–\(M^*\)–Q1 chromosome, while the less frequent combinations arose later through recombination. Because ID-1 was homozygous at both marker loci, an R2/Q1 founder haplotype implies the genotype **R2R2/Q1Q1**.

**C — False.** The proportions in which \(M^*\) is now paired with the non-founder alleles are

\[
p(R1\mid M^*)=\frac{12}{200}=0.06,
\qquad
p(Q2\mid M^*)=\frac{36}{200}=0.18.
\]

On the interpretation intended by the task, these pairings record recombination away from the original R2–\(M^*\)–Q1 background. The smaller change at R indicates less recombination between R and M than between M and Q. Since recombination frequency generally increases with map distance, M is inferred to be closer to **R**, not to Q.

**D — True.** Recombination progressively erodes an association inherited from a founder. In the standard random-mating model, disequilibrium between M and either marker follows

\[
D_{t+n}=(1-r)^nD_t,
\]

where \(r\) is the recombination fraction. The present R1–\(M^*\) and Q2–\(M^*\) chromosomes show that recombination with the founder background has already occurred, so \(r>0\) for both intervals. With no selection and, in the infinite-population idealization, no genetic drift, further generations are therefore expected to reduce the magnitude of LD toward zero.

**Qualification on B and C.** The official reasoning treats the 6% and 18% non-founder associations as direct indicators of recombination. Strictly, the table does not give the background population frequencies of R1 and Q2, which are also needed. In a simple neutral two-locus model, the frequency of a non-founder marker allele \(X\) among \(M^*\) chromosomes after \(t\) generations is

\[
p(X\mid M^*)=p(X)\left[1-(1-r)^t\right].
\]

Thus different background frequencies \(p(R1)\) and \(p(Q2)\) could also contribute to the observed 6% versus 18%, and the founder alleles and relative distances are not uniquely identifiable from the supplied marginals alone. B and C follow under the problem's intended simplifying assumption that the greater retention of an association primarily reflects tighter linkage; the official **T/T/F/T** key is retained.
