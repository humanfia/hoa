# Part B — Task 9

*Checkpoint-kinase-dependent changes in DNA content and polyploidy*

**Official answer: A — False; B — True; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** In the wild-type control histogram, the largest peak is at 2n DNA content. Because the figure defines 2n as the DNA content of a non-dividing diploid cell, this peak represents cells in G1, before DNA replication. Cells passing through S phase have intermediate DNA contents between 2n and 4n, while cells in G2 or M have already replicated their DNA and therefore lie at 4n. Thus the control population is mainly in G1, not G2.

**B — True.** Compound X shifts the wild-type distribution toward larger genome contents: the major peak moves from 2n to 4n, and a distinct population appears at about 8n. An 8n cell contains more complete genome equivalents than an ordinary diploid cell-cycle population, whose DNA content ranges from 2n in G1 to 4n after S phase. The appearance of this higher integer-multiple peak therefore indicates an additional round of genome replication without the normal intervening reduction of DNA content by cell division, which is the evidence for compound-X-induced polyploidy.

**C — False.** The response to compound X differs sharply according to kinase status. Wild-type cells acquire the large 4n peak and an appreciable 8n population after treatment, whereas cells deficient in both kinases retain a distribution broadly similar to their untreated control and do not show the corresponding accumulation at high DNA content. The compound's effect therefore requires, or is at least mediated by, checkpoint kinases A and B; it is not kinase-independent.

**D — False.** The comparison points in the opposite direction. Following compound X treatment, cells with intact kinases A and B reach 8n DNA content, consistent with another replication cycle occurring without a normal intervening division. Kinase-deficient cells fail to make this treatment-induced shift. Thus, under the conditions tested, the checkpoint kinases promote or permit the extra genome replication associated with the response rather than preventing it.

The official explanation calls the 8n population “tetraploid.” This uses the figure's flow-cytometric DNA-content labels: a tetraploid cell that has replicated its DNA can contain 8n-equivalent DNA. In strict cytogenetic notation, ploidy (chromosome-set number) and DNA content after replication are distinct, so an 8n DNA-content label should not by itself be read as eight chromosome sets.
