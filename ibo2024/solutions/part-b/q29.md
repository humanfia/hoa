# Part B — Task 29

*CRISPR–Cas9 target sites, donor design, and coding consequences*

**Official answer: A — False; B — True; C — False; D — True.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** Potential PAMs must be sought on both DNA strands. On the displayed top strand, `AGG` occurs immediately after the highlighted 20-nucleotide target and a later `CGG` provides a second PAM. A bottom-strand `NGG` appears in the top-strand sequence as its reverse complement, `CCN`; the `CCA` near the left end therefore corresponds to a bottom-strand `TGG` PAM. The fragment consequently contains three Cas9-targetable sites, not two.

**B — True.** Both donors contain the desired blue `GAG`-to-`CCA` change. DN-1 also changes `CAC` to the synonymous codon `CAT`, but this is downstream of the recognition site: the guide-matching sequence and its `AGG` PAM remain intact, so Cas9 can still attack DN-1 or the edited product made from it. In DN-2, the orange silent change is `AGG` to `AGA`. Both codons specify arginine, but `AGA` is not an `NGG` PAM. DN-2 therefore retains the required homology and desired edit while preventing further cleavage at that site, making it the better donor.

**C — False.** Starting at the first `ATG`, the relevant wild-type reading frame is

`ATG CGA TTC GAC ACT TAC ...`

Mut-2 changes the fifth codon from `ACT` to `ACA`. Both codons encode threonine, so this third-position substitution is synonymous. Mut-3 instead changes `ACT` to `TCT`, a first-position substitution that changes threonine to serine. Mut-3 is therefore potentially more disruptive than Mut-2, contrary to the statement.

**D — True.** Mut-1 removes the complete `AGG` codon, deleting one arginine while leaving all later codons in their original reading frame. Mut-4 removes `CAG` across the boundary between two codons:

`ACT TA[CAG]G TCA → ACT TAG TCA`.

The resulting `TAG` is a premature stop codon, so Mut-4 can truncate almost the entire protein and is potentially much more deleterious than the single-amino-acid deletion in Mut-1. The official explanation calls Mut-4 a frameshift, but the figure shows a three-nucleotide deletion, which does **not** shift the frame. This is an error in the official explanation rather than in its True verdict: the newly formed stop codon independently supports the verdict.
