# Part B — Task 47

*Cancer immunotherapy, immune checkpoints, and mismatch-repair deficiency*

**Official answer: A — False; B — True; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** Figure 1A shows a CD8-positive cytotoxic T cell recognizing a tumor neoantigen presented by MHC, most likely class I, through its T-cell receptor. Binding of tumor-cell PD-L1 to T-cell PD-1 sends an inhibitory signal that opposes this activating recognition. A checkpoint inhibitor blocks that inhibitory interaction and thereby restores or strengthens the T cell's response to the tumor. Its purpose is not to make the patient generate antibodies against tumor cells; antibody production is a B-cell function. Although many checkpoint-inhibitor drugs are themselves manufactured monoclonal antibodies, that does not make antibody generation their mechanism of antitumor action.

**B — True.** An antibody that binds tumor-cell PD-L1 can prevent PD-L1 from engaging PD-1 on the T cell. The inhibitory branch in Figure 1A is then removed while neoantigen–MHC recognition can still deliver the activating signal. The cytotoxic T cell can consequently remain active and attack the tumor, so a suitably blocking anti-PD-L1 antibody can be an effective immunotherapy.

**C — False.** The fluorescent secondary antibody in Figure 1B detects patient immunoglobulins that remained bound to fixed, healthy human epithelial cells. Patient Z's sample produces strong staining in many cells, whereas patient R's produces little staining. Z therefore has substantially more circulating antibodies reactive with normal human cellular antigens, suggesting an autoimmune tendency. Because PD-1/PD-L1 signaling is also a physiological restraint on self-reactive immune responses, checkpoint blockade could aggravate such a response. Of these two patients, Z is therefore the one for whom immunotherapy should be avoided, making R—not Z—the more likely recipient.

**D — False.** After checkpoint-inhibitor treatment, cohort A retains a much higher overall-survival probability than cohort B: by about 12 months, the curves are roughly 0.70 for A and 0.33 for B. Loss of DNA mismatch repair permits replication errors to accumulate, increasing the tumor mutation burden. More mutations can produce more altered proteins and hence more neoantigen peptides for presentation to T cells. Such a tumor is more immunologically visible and is expected to respond better when checkpoint inhibition releases the T-cell brake. Under the question's condition that mismatch-repair status is the only important cohort difference, the better-surviving cohort A is therefore the likely mismatch-repair-deficient cohort; cohort B is more likely mismatch-repair proficient.

**Qualification.** The epithelial-cell staining in Figure 1B demonstrates serum antibodies that bind healthy-cell antigens, but this assay alone would not establish a clinical autoimmune diagnosis or an absolute contraindication in real medical practice. The conclusion in C follows the problem's explicit instruction that treatment must be avoided for one of the two patients. Likewise, the survival curves support D only under the stipulated assumption that mismatch-repair status is the sole meaningful difference between the cohorts. These limitations do not change the official truth pattern.
