# Part B — Task 25

*X-linked recessive inheritance and population allele frequencies*

**Official answer: A — False; B — True; C — False; D — True.**

## Reasoning

Let $X^A$ carry the dominant non-performant allele and $X^a$ carry the recessive performant allele. The pedigree is consistent with X-linked recessive inheritance: unaffected parents can have affected sons, and the affected female shown has an affected father and a mother who must be a carrier.

The embedded answer page presents A as an unlettered bullet but labels B–D explicitly; its printed truth values nevertheless give the unambiguous pattern **F/T/F/T**. It also writes a malformed $q^2=q$ in the male calculation. An X-linked male is hemizygous, so his affected frequency is $q$; $q^2$ is the affected-female frequency. Neither editorial issue changes the official key.

**A — False.** The two source groups contain equal numbers of mice and have the same sex ratio, so they contribute equal numbers of X chromosomes. The recessive-allele frequency after mixing is therefore

\[
q'=\frac{0.3+0.2}{2}=0.25,
\qquad p'=0.75.
\]

For an X-linked recessive trait, the affected fraction is $q'=0.25$ among males and $(q')^2=0.0625$ among females. Because the final population contains equal numbers of males and females, the expected affected-male to affected-female ratio is

\[
\frac{1000(0.25)}{1000(0.25)^2}=\frac{0.25}{0.0625}=4:1,
\]

not $5:1$.

**B — True.** A son receives his Y chromosome from Mouse 3 and his only X chromosome from the randomly selected female. In Scientist A's population, the maternal genotype frequencies are

\[
P(X^AX^A)=0.7^2=0.49,\quad
P(X^AX^a)=2(0.7)(0.3)=0.42,\quad
P(X^aX^a)=0.3^2=0.09.
\]

A son is non-performant with probability $1$ if his mother is $X^AX^A$, $1/2$ if she is $X^AX^a$, and $0$ if she is $X^aX^a$. Thus, conditional on the offspring being male,

\[
P(\text{non-performant son})=0.49+\frac12(0.42)=0.70.
\]

Equivalently, the maternal gamete carries $X^a$ with frequency $q=0.30$, so a son is unaffected with probability $p=0.70$.

**C — False.** Mouse 1 must be $X^AX^a$: she is an unaffected daughter of an affected father, so she necessarily received $X^a$ from him and $X^A$ from her mother. Mouse 2 is an affected male, $X^aY$. In their cross, one birth is a performant male only if it is male ($1/2$) and receives $X^a$ from Mouse 1 ($1/2$), giving probability $1/4$. Assuming independent births, the probability that all four offspring are performant males is

\[
\left(\frac14\right)^4=\frac1{256}\approx0.003906=0.3906\%.
\]

This is greater than $0.2\%$, so the stated inequality is false.

**D — True.** The father of mice A and B is affected, so he is $X^aY$; their mother is phenotypically unaffected. If the mother is a carrier, $X^AX^a$, mouse A (a daughter) is affected when she receives maternal $X^a$, with probability $1/2$. Mouse B (a son) is affected under exactly the same maternal event, also with probability $1/2$. If the mother is instead $X^AX^A$, both probabilities are $0$. Therefore, even though the mother's carrier status is not specified, mice A and B have the same probability of the performant phenotype.
