# Part B — Task 23

*ACE2, CTS1, and multicellular clusters in baker's yeast*

**Official answer: A — False; B — True; C — True; D — True.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** Wild-type cells separate after budding and therefore remain mostly as individual cells, as in Figure 1A (G). By contrast, the *ACE2* mutant cells fail to separate and form the much larger clusters shown in Figure 1A (H). A cluster has a larger effective size and greater gravitational settling tendency than a single dispersed cell, so the mutant culture should settle faster under the same conditions. The statement reverses the expected comparison.

**B — True.** The sequence of strain 8 is missing one adenine from the run of adenines present in wild-type strain 1. A one-nucleotide deletion is not a multiple of three, so it changes the grouping of all subsequent codons and produces a frameshift. Figure 1B also supports recessive inheritance: crosses of mutant strain 8 with each tested wild-type row strain (1, 3, 4, or 6) produce unicellular offspring, whereas its crosses with mutant strains 2 and 5 produce multicellular offspring. Thus one wild-type allele complements the mutant allele in a hybrid, and the multicellular phenotype appears when both parents contribute mutant *ACE2* alleles. The embedded official explanation misnumbers these panels: the sequencing is in Figure 2 and the crosses are in Figure 1B, not Figure 3 and Figure 2A; this editorial error does not affect the answer.

**C — True.** Before the deletion site, the unknown chromatogram has a single clean set of peaks. After that site, it contains overlapping peaks, as expected when both strain-1 and strain-8 templates are sequenced together: because the strain-8 allele is one base shorter, the two sequences become offset and their downstream peaks are superimposed. A diploid product of the strain 1 × strain 8 cross could carry one allele of each length, so it could generate the unknown trace. The wording says “may be,” and the trace establishes compatibility rather than a unique parentage assignment.

**D — True.** *ACE2* mutants express *CTS1* at only a small fraction of the wild-type level in Figure 1C, and those mutants complete repeated budding but retain mother and daughter cells in chitin-stained clusters. This links the *CTS1* product to removal of the chitin-containing partition or cell-wall connection at the bud neck, which releases the daughter after cytokinesis. Because cell formation and division have already occurred when this separation step is needed, the evidence places the principal role of CTS1 late in cell division rather than at its initiation.
