# Part B — Task 30

*Three-point linkage, an enzyme-regulation pathway, and redundant developmental genes*

**Official answer: A — True; B — False; C — False; D — True.**

## Reasoning

The official answer section omits the bullet and the letter `D` before its final **TRUE** paragraph. In statement order, however, the intended key is unambiguously **T/F/F/T**.

There is also a numerical issue in the official treatment of A. Table 1 gives both rarest classes a count of 1, but the printed answer substitutes 3 for the last count. Moreover, the strict two-point recombinant fraction for the outer genes A and B is $96/498\approx19.3\%$, because double crossovers restore the parental combination of those two outer markers. The official **True** verdict is supported if "between A and B" is intended as the genetic map length across the two intervals: that calculation gives $100/498\approx20.1\%$, or about 20%. The reasoning below uses that intended map-distance interpretation while preserving the official key.

**A — True.** In the `EE` column, where the developmental lethal interaction cannot remove classes, the two most frequent gametes are the parental types `ABD` and `abd` (200 each). The two least frequent types, `ABd` and `abD` (1 each), are the double-crossover classes. Each differs from its corresponding parental type only at D, so D must be the middle gene and the order is A–D–B (or the reverse).

For the A–D interval, the single-crossover classes are `Abd` and `aBD`; for the D–B interval, they are `AbD` and `aBd`. A double crossover contributes once to each interval. Therefore,

\[
r_{A-D}=\frac{16+16+1+1}{498},
\qquad
r_{D-B}=\frac{33+31+1+1}{498}.
\]

Adding the two interval distances gives

\[
\frac{34+66}{498}\times100\%=20.08\%\approx20\%.
\]

Thus the intended genetic distance between A and B is about 20 map units. For comparison, counting only offspring recombinant for the outer A/B markers gives $(33+31+16+16)/498=19.28\%$; the difference is the double-crossover correction noted above.

**B — False.** A dominant allele produces the functional product, whereas a homozygous recessive locus does not. Among genotypes with only one functional product, `Abd/abd` has orange wings, but `aBd/abd` and `abD/abd` have yellow wings. A alone must therefore encode the enzyme that converts yellow pigment to orange; neither B alone nor D alone can make orange pigment without that enzyme.

The remaining orange/yellow comparisons identify the regulators. With enzyme A present, `ABd/abd` is yellow: functional B blocks the enzyme when D is absent, so B is the inhibitor. `ABD/abd` is orange because functional D suppresses that inhibitor, making D the activator. Hence A encodes **ENZYME**, B encodes **INHIBITOR**, and D encodes **ACTIVATOR**, so the claim about B is false.

**C — False.** Every child of `AAbbDdEE × aaBbddEE` is `Aa` and `EE`, so every child has functional enzyme A and the developmental interaction is irrelevant. At the linked B/D loci, the first parent can make only `bD` and `bd` gametes, while the second can make only `Bd` and `bd` gametes, each pair in a 1:1 ratio. Recombination cannot create any additional allele combinations because each parent is heterozygous at only one of these loci.

The four equally likely B/D genotypes are therefore `BbDd`, `bbDd`, `Bbdd`, and `bbdd`. With enzyme A present, only `Bbdd` is yellow: it has the B inhibitor but lacks the D activator that would inhibit B. The other three genotypes have active enzyme and are orange. Thus

\[
P(\text{orange})=\frac34=75\%,
\]

not 50%.

**D — True.** In the `ee` testcross column, every surviving class carries a dominant D allele, whereas every `dd` class has zero offspring. Thus D is the gene whose developmental role is redundant with E: larvae die only when they are `dd ee`.

In `AaBbDDEe × AABbddEe`, the first parent always contributes D and the second always contributes d, so every offspring is `Dd`. Even the one-quarter expected to be `ee` still has functional D and therefore does not have the lethal `dd ee` combination. All offspring should consequently be viable.
