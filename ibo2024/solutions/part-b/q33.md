# Part B — Task 33

*Regulation of vascular cambium proliferation and secondary vascular development*

**Official answer: A — True; B — True; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

The embedded official explanation refers to “Figure 2” and “Figure 3,” but the exam page presents these data as panels B and C of Figure 1. This panel-numbering error does not affect the answer pattern.

**A — True.** Region C is the vascular cambium. It produces secondary phloem toward the outside of the stem, where region A lies, and secondary xylem toward the inside, where region B lies. The red arrows identify vessel elements in region B, confirming this orientation. As secondary xylem accumulates, the stem expands in circumference. Mature phloem cells, including sieve-tube elements, cannot continue dividing or stretch indefinitely, so older phloem is compressed and eventually crushed or ruptured. The vessel elements in region B have strong lignified secondary walls. Cells in region A are therefore more prone to rupture than those in region B.

**B — True.** In Figure 1A, `PtrVCS2` overexpression produces a thinner region C with fewer cambial cell layers than in the adjacent wild type, so PtrVCS2 restricts cambial proliferation. In contrast, `PtrWOX4a` overexpression enlarges region C and increases the number of cambial layers, showing that PtrWOX4a promotes proliferation. The two genes consequently have opposing regulatory effects on the vascular cambium.

**C — False.** Removing the proliferation inhibitor PtrVCS2 produces a wider stem cross-section in Figure 1C, consistent with PtrVCS2 placing an upper limit on secondary, radial growth. However, the wild-type plant and both `PtrVCS2` loss-of-function plants in Figure 1B have comparable heights. Stem elongation is primary growth, so the data do not support an upper-limit role for PtrVCS2 in primary growth. Because the statement claims such a role in both primary and secondary growth, it is false.

**D — False.** Cambial-cell proliferation and differentiation into mature secondary xylem are distinct processes. Loss of PtrVCS2 removes a brake on cambial proliferation, yet the mutant in Figure 1C shows weaker red/pink safranin O staining in the secondary vascular tissue than the wild type. Safranin O marks lignified walls, a defining feature of differentiated secondary xylem. Thus increased cambial proliferation can occur without faster xylem differentiation—and in this mutant it accompanies reduced xylem maturation—so enhanced proliferation does not necessarily accelerate secondary xylem development.
