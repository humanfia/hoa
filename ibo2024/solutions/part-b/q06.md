# Part B — Task 6

*ProQC toehold-switch control of full-length mRNA translation*

**Official answer: A — True; B — True; C — True; D — True.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** When the toehold-switch sequence is unbound, it folds into a hairpin that hides the ribosome-binding site (RBS), so a ribosome cannot efficiently initiate translation of the gene of interest (GOI). A complete ProQC transcript also contains the reverse-complementary cis-trigger at its $3'$ end. Binding of that cis-trigger to the switch replaces the inhibitory switch hairpin with a switch–trigger duplex, exposes the RBS, and turns translation on. Because a prematurely terminated transcript lacks the downstream cis-trigger, its RBS normally remains hidden. This is the intended meaning of “only” in A: translation from the standard cassette is conditional on formation of the switch–trigger duplex.

**B — True.** Ordinary expression from incomplete mRNAs can consume amino acids, energy, and ribosome time while producing shortened, usually nonfunctional proteins. A transcript that has lost its stop codon can also leave a ribosome stalled at its end and make ribosome recycling more difficult. ProQC suppresses initiation on such truncated transcripts because they lack the $3'$ cis-trigger needed to expose the $5'$ RBS. Full-length transcripts contain both the trigger and the stop codon, so they can be translated to completion and release the ribosome normally. ProQC therefore directs a larger share of the cell's expression resources toward complete, functional product and reduces waste.

**C — True (official answer, with a qualification).** The intended comparison is with prokaryotic cells, where transcription and translation occur in the same compartment and can be coupled. A ribosome can therefore encounter an RNA while it is still being made, making a device that withholds the RBS until the $3'$ end has been transcribed especially useful. In eukaryotes, transcription and pre-mRNA processing occur in the nucleus, whereas translation occurs in the cytoplasm. The $5'$ cap, splicing, $3'$ cleavage and polyadenylation, nuclear retention, and export surveillance generally keep unfinished pre-mRNAs away from cytoplasmic ribosomes, so the exam treats ProQC as unnecessary there. The absolute wording is biologically stronger than the evidence warrants: eukaryotic quality control is not perfect, and aberrant or damaged cytoplasmic mRNAs can still be translated. Thus the official **True** is best understood as the simplified, intended contrast between coupled prokaryotic expression and compartmentalized eukaryotic expression, not as a claim that ProQC could never benefit a eukaryotic synthetic system.

**D — True.** Opening the switch depends on complementary base pairing, not on the trigger being covalently attached to the same RNA molecule. A separately transcribed trigger RNA with the appropriate complementary sequence can bind the toehold switch in trans, disrupt the RBS-sequestering hairpin, and expose the RBS for translation initiation. This also clarifies the scope of A: a trans-trigger can physically substitute for the built-in cis-trigger, so A's word “only” should not be read as excluding every separately supplied complementary trigger.
