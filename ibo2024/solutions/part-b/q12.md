# Part B — Task 12

*Förster resonance energy transfer in protease and inhibitor assays*

**Official answer: A — False; B — False; C — True; D — True.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** FRET requires an excited donor to come within roughly 10 nm of a spectrally compatible acceptor. Increasing the concentrations of both freely dispersed chromophores raises their number densities and reduces the typical distance from a donor to a nearby acceptor. Close donor–acceptor encounters therefore become more, not less, probable, assuming the other requirements for FRET are unchanged. Thus the proposed decrease in FRET probability has the wrong direction.

**B — False.** A donor's emission range must overlap the acceptor's excitation range. Brim43, when used as the acceptor, is excited from 510 to 570 nm, whereas G8 emits from 670 to 710 nm; these intervals do not overlap. In fact, the reverse assignment is spectrally plausible: Brim43 emits from 600 to 660 nm, which overlaps much of G8's 590–650 nm excitation range, so Brim43 could be the donor and G8 the acceptor. Among the listed fluorophores, YFP is a much better donor for a Brim43 acceptor because its 515–560 nm emission lies within Brim43's excitation interval.

**C — True.** Excitation at 405 nm directly excites CFP. When the CFP–YFP fusion is intact, nearby YFP accepts energy from CFP, producing relatively little CFP emission near 480 nm and strong YFP emission near 530 nm. Cleavage of the intervening protease-target sequence separates the fluorophores, reduces FRET, restores the 480 nm donor signal, and lowers the 530 nm acceptor signal. Of the three protease-treated spectra, P3 produces the largest restoration of the 480 nm peak and the strongest reduction of the 530 nm peak, making its spectrum most similar to that of separate CFP and YFP. It therefore cleaves the most fusion substrate and has the highest activity of the tested proteases.

**D — True.** The plotted quantity is the emission ratio at 480 nm to 530 nm. Because cleavage raises CFP emission and lowers FRET-dependent YFP emission, a larger ratio indicates more cleavage by P3. With 5 nM inhibitor Z, raising CFP–YFP substrate from 10 to 20 to 30 nM increases the ratio from about 0.25 to 0.40 to 0.75: excess substrate progressively overcomes inhibition, as expected for a competitive inhibitor. With inhibitor Q, the ratio remains low at about 0.25 across the same substrate concentrations, so extra substrate does not restore appreciable activity, as expected for non-competitive inhibition. Z and Q are therefore competitive and non-competitive inhibitors, respectively.
