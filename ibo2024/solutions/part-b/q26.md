# Part B — Task 26

*Intratumor heterogeneity, clonal evolution, and copy-number alterations*

**Official answer: A — True; B — False; C — True; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** Different subclones within one tumor can carry different mutations and therefore differ in their sensitivity to a drug. A treatment directed against a mutation present in only some subclones can eliminate those cells while sparing subclones that lack the target or already possess resistance-conferring changes. The surviving cells are then selected to repopulate the tumor, so intratumor heterogeneity commonly contributes to incomplete responses, relapse, and drug resistance.

**B — False.** In the evolutionary tree in Figure 1B, mutation A lies on the trunk before any of the four sampled regions diverge, so it is present in R1, R2, R3, and R4. Mutation D arose later on the branch leading only to R2. All else being equal, targeting A could affect every represented tumor subclone, whereas targeting D would leave R1, R3, and R4 untreated. Mutation A is therefore the broader therapeutic target indicated by this tree.

**C — True.** Mutation C arose after the R1 lineage had separated, but before the common ancestor of R2, R3, and R4 divided further. Thus R4 carries mutation C, whereas R1 does not. A drug specifically effective against C-mutant cells would suppress R4 and the other C-bearing regions but would not directly affect R1. This selective advantage, together with reduced competition from sensitive cells, makes the C-negative R1 lineage more likely to expand than R4 after treatment.

**D — False.** The normal diploid baseline in Figure 1C is two copies. The segment containing gene E has fallen to one copy, indicating a deletion, while the segment containing gene F has risen to four copies, indicating amplification. Copy-number loss is the pattern expected for a potential tumor-suppressor gene, whereas increased dosage is the pattern expected for a potential oncogene. The proposed assignments are reversed: the plot supports E as the tumor-suppressor candidate and F as the oncogene candidate.
