# Part B — Task 45

*Lung hyperinflation and operating lung volumes in COPD*

**Official answer: A — True; B — False; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** Figure 1C places person 1's end-expiratory lung volume (EELV) at about 60% of vital capacity, compared with about 40% for person 2. Person 1 is therefore breathing from a more inflated resting position, on the positive-pressure part of the pressure–volume curve. This is the pattern expected when expiratory flow limitation causes air trapping and intrinsic positive end-expiratory pressure.

At this high operating volume, the diaphragm is flatter and its muscle fibres and sarcomeres are shortened. Because skeletal muscle produces less maximal active force when it is appreciably shorter than its optimal length, the hyperinflated inspiratory muscles have poorer force-generating capacity. They must also overcome the positive end-expiratory pressure before new inspiratory flow can begin. Thus, in the intended sense of maximal or effective inspiratory force, person 1 generates less force than person 2.

**B — False.** Total lung capacity is the sum of all four component volumes,

$$
\mathrm{TLC}=\mathrm{RV}+\mathrm{ERV}+V_T+\mathrm{IRV}.
$$

In Figure 1B, the top of the severe-COPD bar is higher than the top of the moderate-COPD bar, so TLC itself increases. The proportions also change: residual volume (RV), and hence the trapped volume remaining after expiration, becomes much larger, while inspiratory reserve volume (IRV) becomes smaller. Therefore the change from moderate to severe disease is not merely a repartitioning of an unchanged TLC.

**C — False.** In each Figure 2 panel, the lower dashed trace is EELV and the upper solid trace is end-inspiratory lung volume (EILV). Consequently,

$$
V_T=\mathrm{EILV}-\mathrm{EELV},\qquad
\mathrm{IRV}=\mathrm{TLC}-\mathrm{EILV},\qquad
\mathrm{IC}=\mathrm{TLC}-\mathrm{EELV}.
$$

Worsening hyperinflation raises EELV and reduces inspiratory capacity (IC), leaving less room to inhale before TLC is reached. Panel E has the lowest EELV and largest IC, so it represents mild COPD. Panel T has the highest, most strongly rising EELV and the smallest IC, so it represents severe COPD. Panel Z is intermediate and represents moderate COPD. The correct order is therefore **T — severe, Z — moderate, E — mild**, not the order stated.

**D — False.** Figure 1C reports position on the pressure–volume curve as a percentage of vital capacity. Vital capacity is

$$
\mathrm{VC}=\mathrm{TLC}-\mathrm{RV}=\mathrm{IRV}+V_T+\mathrm{ERV},
$$

so this coordinate describes the usable volume excursion above residual volume; it does not provide the absolute RV offset. The lower plotted EELV of person 2 therefore shows a lower operating lung volume relative to VC, not a higher RV. Absolute RV values for the two people cannot be recovered from Figure 1C, so the claimed comparison is unsupported.

**Interpretive qualification.** The official verdict for A treats “generate lower force” as lower *force-generating capacity* caused by hyperinflation and muscle shortening. The pressure–volume curve does not directly measure inspiratory-muscle force, and the actual activation or force used during a breath may be greater in a COPD patient because intrinsic positive end-expiratory pressure imposes an extra threshold load. Read literally as instantaneous force rather than maximal capacity, A would therefore be underdetermined; the official answer is preserved under its intended physiological interpretation.
