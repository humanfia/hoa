# Part B — Task 28

*Epistatic control of hair pigmentation and pedigree probabilities*

**Official answer: A — True; B — False; C — True; D — False.**

## Reasoning

The embedded answer section leaves its first three verdicts unlettered and labels only the final one as D. In statement order, however, the printed verdicts unambiguously give **T/F/T/F**. It also calls the three probability pathways used for C “independent”; they are actually mutually exclusive cases that should be added. This wording issue does not change its result or the official key.

**A — True.** The pedigree shows that an `Aabb` individual is white-haired whereas an `aabb` individual is black-haired. It also shows that individuals with `B_` are black-haired even when they carry `A`. Thus white hair occurs only with `A_bb`; a dominant `B` allele masks the effect of gene A and produces black hair regardless of the A genotype. Gene B is therefore epistatic to gene A.

**B — False.** In an `AaBb × AaBb` cross, unlinked assortment gives

\[
P(A_)=\frac34,
\qquad
P(bb)=\frac14.
\]

Only `A_bb` children are white-haired, so

\[
P(\text{white hair})=P(A_)P(bb)=\frac34\cdot\frac14=\frac{3}{16},
\]

not \(7/16\).

**C — True.** The pedigree fixes V-1's mother as `Aabb`. Her generation-III mother must be `aabb`: both of that woman's parents are `bb`, and her black phenotype therefore requires `aa`. V-1's white-haired mother consequently inherits `a` from this parent but must also possess a dominant `A`, making her `Aabb`. IV-8 is `Bb`, while his A genotype is unknown. With \(p(A)=0.4\) and \(q(a)=0.6\), Hardy–Weinberg frequencies for IV-8 are

\[
P(AA)=0.16,\qquad P(Aa)=0.48,\qquad P(aa)=0.36.
\]

His probability of passing an `A` allele is consequently

\[
0.16+\frac12(0.48)=0.40.
\]

Because the genes are unlinked, IV-8's known B genotype does not alter these A-genotype frequencies; moreover, `B_` already guarantees black hair, so his phenotype supplies no additional information about his A genotype. V-1 receives `b` from the mother with certainty and receives `b` from IV-8 with probability \(1/2\), giving \(P(bb)=1/2\). At gene A, V-1 is `A_` either when the mother transmits `A`, or when she transmits `a` and IV-8 transmits `A`:

\[
P(A_)=\frac12+\frac12(0.40)=0.70.
\]

Because the loci are unlinked,

\[
P(\text{white-haired V-1})=P(A_)P(bb)=0.70\cdot0.50=0.35.
\]

**D — False.** Consider the generation-III couple at the left: the mother is the black-haired `aabb` female, and the father is white-haired. If A were X-linked, the mother would be \(X^aX^a\,bb\), while a white-haired father would have to be \(X^AY\,bb\). Every daughter would receive \(X^A\) from him and `b` from both parents, so every daughter would be \(X^AX^a\,bb\) and therefore white-haired. The pedigree instead shows this couple with both a black-haired daughter and a white-haired daughter. Gene A therefore cannot be X-linked; the pedigree does not permit its location on every chromosome other than Y.
