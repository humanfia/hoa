# Part B — Task 41

*Post-feeding cardiac growth in the Burmese python*

**Official answer: A — True; B — True; C — False; D — True.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** Three days after feeding, python heart mass is about 40% greater than in the fasting state (Figure 1A), whereas the ventricular DNA concentration falls from about 3.0 to 2.6 ng per mg of tissue (Figure 1B). Because most DNA is nuclear, less DNA per unit tissue means that there is more ventricular mass per nucleus. This is consistent with cardiac hypertrophy—growth of existing cells through the addition of cytoplasm and contractile material—rather than growth caused solely by producing proportionally more cells. The DNA measurement is a concentration, not a direct cell count or total-heart DNA measurement, so it cannot prove that no cell division occurred; nevertheless, the statement only says that increased cell size is *likely*, which the data support.

**B — True.** From Figure 1C, the absolute increases in heart mass are P: $26-20=6$ g, Q: $40.5-30=10.5$ g, R: $60-40=20$ g, and S: $70-50=20$ g. P therefore has the smallest absolute increase. It also has the smallest proportional increase: P increases by $6/20=30\%$, compared with $10.5/30=35\%$ for Q, $20/40=50\%$ for R, and $20/50=40\%$ for S. Thus the conclusion is the same under either interpretation of “heart mass increase.”

**C — False.** Figure 1A shows that a fed Burmese python has an approximately 40% increase in heart mass. In Figure 1C, animal R increases from 40 g to 60 g, which is a 50% increase. Animal S increases from 50 g to 70 g, or $20/50=40\%$, so S is the animal intended to represent the Burmese python. This identification relies on matching the proportional changes; Figure 1C does not independently provide species labels.

**D — True.** Infusing a fasting python with plasma from a fed python produces about a 40% heart-mass increase, whereas plasma from a fasting python produces only about a 19% increase. Likewise, BSA plus substance W produces a significant increase of roughly 37%, while BSA alone produces only about an 18% increase. Because W reproduces much of the fed-plasma effect above the corresponding controls, W could be one of the circulating post-feeding signals present in fed-python plasma. The similar responses do not establish that W is actually present—another plasma factor could act through the same pathway—but the cautious wording “might contain” is justified.
