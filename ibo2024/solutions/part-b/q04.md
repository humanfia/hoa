# Part B — Task 4

*Calcium-channel control of mitochondrial calcium in pancreatic beta cells*

**Official answer: A — True; B — False; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** Raising the extracellular K⁺ concentration from 5 mM to 30 mM reduces the K⁺ concentration gradient across the beta-cell membrane. Less K⁺ leaves the cell, so the membrane potential becomes less negative: the cell depolarizes. The mitochondrial Ca²⁺ transients that begin when the 30 mM K⁺ treatment is applied are consistent with depolarization initiating the calcium response.

**B — False.** The experiment activates the response by depolarizing the cells with high K⁺, not by adding a ligand for Channel A. Blocking Channel A strongly reduces the K⁺-induced mitochondrial Ca²⁺ peak, which is consistent with Channel A being a voltage-gated calcium channel that opens upon depolarization. The data therefore do not support the claim that it is ligand-gated.

**C — False.** High glucose is expected to raise calcium in a pancreatic beta cell. Glucose metabolism increases the ATP-to-ADP ratio, causing ATP-sensitive K⁺ channels to close and the plasma membrane to depolarize. Depolarization opens voltage-gated Channel A, Ca²⁺ enters the cytosol, and this influx can promote further release from the ER and transfer of Ca²⁺ into mitochondria. Thus replacing the high-K⁺ stimulus with high glucose should still produce an increase in mitochondrial Ca²⁺ rather than no increase.

**D — False.** Removing extracellular Ca²⁺ is not the same experimental manipulation as selectively blocking Channel A while leaving 2 mM Ca²⁺ in the medium. In Ca²⁺-free medium, extracellular Ca²⁺ is unavailable to every possible influx route, and the altered influx also changes activation and refilling of intracellular calcium stores. By contrast, inhibitor Y targets only Channel A, while ER release and any other calcium routes can still contribute to the mitochondrial signal. Because mitochondrial Ca²⁺ integrates these several sources, the control trace in 0 mM extracellular Ca²⁺ cannot be expected to reproduce the inhibitor-Y trace.
