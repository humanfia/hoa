# Part B — Task 4

*Honeybee heat-ball and sting defenses against hornets*

**Official answer: A — True; B — False; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — True (official key).** The embedded official answer key marks this statement as true, so that keyed value is preserved here.

**Discrepancy note.** The plotted data do not appear to support the keyed answer. Figure 1B gives a *Vespa velutina* body mass of about 280 mg and a venom mass of about 5.2 mg. The venom fraction is therefore approximately

$$
\frac{5.2\ \mathrm{mg}}{280\ \mathrm{mg}}\times 100\%\approx 1.9\%,
$$

which is well below 2.6–2.8% and therefore supports **False** for statement A. The official explanation reaches the same numerical value but labels it as 1.9 mg; because this calculation is a mass divided by a mass and multiplied by 100%, the correct unit is percent. Thus the figure-derived calculation and the accompanying explanation conflict with the official **True** key.

**B — False.** The mutant *A. mellifera* bees can still form a 44°C heat ball even though they cannot sting. The no-sting control at 44°C isolates the effect of heat: hornet survival falls from 1.0 to roughly 0.75, meaning that heating alone kills about one quarter of the hornets under these conditions. Thus the mutant colony would be less lethal than heat plus stinging, but it is not incapable of killing any attacker.

**C — False.** Amdef bees should behave like the 44°C no-sting treatment, in which about 75% of hornets remain alive after three hours. By contrast, a single *A. cerana* sting combined with 44°C reduces survival to about 10%. The mutant bees can kill by heating, but not nearly as efficiently as wild-type *A. cerana* using both heat and venom.

**D — False.** At 44°C, the *A. cerana* and *A. mellifera* single-sting curves both fall to approximately 10% survival and remain there through 180 minutes. Hence both treatments kill roughly 90% of the hornets over three hours; the graph does not show that the same number of *A. cerana* bees kills more hornets than *A. mellifera* bees.
