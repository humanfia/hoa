# Part B — Task 34

*GAPLESS proteins and the root endodermal apoplastic barrier*

**Official answer: A — False; B — False; C — True; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** In the micrographs, the red dye outlines cell walls and extracellular spaces, but the cell interiors remain dark. A small non-polar molecule would dissolve in the lipid bilayer and diffuse readily across plasma membranes, so it would not remain restricted to the apoplast in this way. The observed exclusion from cells is instead consistent with a membrane-impermeant, likely polar or charged, tracer.

**B — False.** At position A, red fluorescence extends across the yellow-arrowed endodermal layer and into the vascular cylinder. At position B, it reaches the endodermis but the tissue inside that layer remains dark, showing that an effective apoplastic barrier has formed there. The Casparian-strip barrier develops as endodermal cells differentiate, so it is incomplete in younger tissue near the root apex and functional in older tissue farther from the apex. Position A, not position B, must therefore be closer to the root tip.

**C — True.** Figure 2 compares the two genotypes at the barrier-forming position B. The wild-type vascular cylinder excludes the red dye, whereas the `GAPLESS` loss-of-function mutant shows red fluorescence inside the endodermis. Loss of GAPLESS therefore makes the endodermal apoplastic barrier leaky. This supports the conclusion that wild-type GAPLESS proteins are required to prevent uncontrolled apoplastic movement from the cortex into the vascular system.

**D — False.** The root has many xylem poles arranged around a central pith rather than the central, star-shaped xylem pattern typical of a eudicot root; this is characteristic of a monocot. Typical monocot vascular bundles lack a vascular cambium, so these plants generally do not undergo the conventional year-by-year secondary thickening of stems seen in woody eudicots and gymnosperms. The anatomy therefore does not support the proposed annual increase in stem diameter.
