# Part B — Task 37

*Polar auxin transport, grafting, and tissue-specific AXR1 rescue in apical dominance*

**Official answer: A — True; B — True; C — False; D — True.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** Auxin produced near a shoot apex is transported mainly basipetally, from the apical end toward the base of the stem. Here, auxin supplied at A produces 2221.0 ± 232.3 counts per minute at the opposite end, B, whereas auxin supplied at B produces only 13.4 ± 0.5 counts per minute at the opposite end, A. Transport from A to B is therefore about 2221.0 / 13.4 ≈ 166 times as great as transport in the reverse direction. NPA also lowers the A-to-B value from 2221.0 to 31.5, a reduction of about 98.6%, confirming that the large signal reflects polar auxin transport. Thus A is the apical end, closer to the shoot apical meristem, and B is the more basal end.

**B — True.** When auxin moves efficiently through the stem after application at A, the bud contains only 14.2 ± 0.9 counts per minute, about 1/156 of the 2221.0 ± 232.3 measured at the far end of the stem. Moreover, all four bud measurements remain in the narrow range 11.9–15.8 counts per minute, whether auxin is applied at A or B and whether NPA is absent or present. The very large, NPA-sensitive change in stem transport therefore has no corresponding accumulation in the bud. The intended conclusion is that stem auxin can maintain apical dominance indirectly, without being transported into the axillary bud as the local inhibitory signal.

**C — False.** Figure 2 identifies each graft as shoot/root. A wild-type shoot has similarly low branching on either a wild-type root or an auxin-insensitive `axr1-mut` root (about 2.5 branches in both cases). Conversely, an `axr1-mut` shoot remains highly branched on a wild-type root (about 4.5), nearly like an `axr1-mut` shoot on an `axr1-mut` root (about 5.1). Branching therefore follows the shoot genotype, not the root genotype. If inhibition of cytokinin synthesis in the root were the essential auxin response, changing root auxin sensitivity should have changed the branching phenotype. These grafts instead locate the required auxin response in the shoot and do not support the proposed root mechanism.

**D — True.** The untransformed `axr1-mut` has 26 ± 2.0 units of branching, compared with 16 ± 0.5 in wild type. Restoring AXR1 only in the epidermis or only in the phloem leaves branching at 25 ± 1.0, essentially the mutant phenotype. In contrast, restoring AXR1 only in the xylem reduces branching to 16 ± 0.5, exactly the reported wild-type value and comparable to the 18 ± 1.0 obtained when AXR1 is restored in all listed tissues. Thus auxin perception in xylem cells is sufficient to restore auxin-dependent suppression of bud outgrowth, supporting the stated critical role of the xylem.

**Interpretive and source notes.** The bud counts in Table 1 are not literally zero, and no untreated-background value or significance test is supplied. Therefore B is most precisely read as “auxin need not appreciably accumulate in the bud to inhibit its growth,” rather than proof that no auxin molecule enters it. Likewise, the xylem-only rescue in D demonstrates sufficiency; proving strict necessity would require an additional treatment lacking AXR1 only in the xylem. Neither qualification changes the official truth pattern. The embedded official explanation also writes “ARX1” and refers to Table 2 in its discussion of D; the question and Table 3 show that the intended gene and data source are **AXR1** and **Table 3**.
