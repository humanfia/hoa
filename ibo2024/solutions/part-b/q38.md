# Part B — Task 38

*Chemical A, wheat germination, gibberellin signalling, and α-amylase zymography*

**Official answer: A — False; B — True; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** Chemical A inhibits rather than accelerates early wheat development. In Figure 1A, both an intact seed and an isolated embryo produce a much shorter shoot after treatment with chemical A than their respective controls. Slower germination and seedling growth would tend to lengthen, not shorten, the time needed to establish the crop. Thus the figure gives no basis for claiming that adding the chemical would reduce cultivation time.

**B — True.** The inhibition is proportionally stronger when chemical A is applied directly to an isolated embryo. From the photograph, the treated-to-control shoot-length ratio is roughly $0.4$–$0.5$ for intact seeds, but only about $0.15$–$0.2$ for isolated embryos. In other words, treatment reduces shoot length by approximately 50–60% in the intact seed comparison and by about 80–85% in the isolated-embryo comparison. The most direct interpretation is that tissues surrounding the embryo in an intact seed impede penetration of chemical A, so the embryo receives a smaller effective dose. This supports the intended conclusion that the seed covering acts as a partial barrier.

**C — False.** The gel is an activity stain. Active α-amylase hydrolyses starch in the region where it migrated; because iodine stains remaining starch dark but not the resulting small soluble sugars, greater α-amylase activity appears as brighter bands. The Phyt-X lane has several much stronger bright bands than the control lane, so phytohormone X promotes α-amylase activity. During cereal germination, gibberellin induces the production and secretion of α-amylase, which mobilizes stored starch for the growing embryo, whereas abscisic acid generally maintains dormancy and antagonizes this response. X is therefore more consistent with gibberellin than with abscisic acid. The weak bands when chemical A is added together with X also show that chemical A suppresses this induced response.

**D — False.** Detecting bands by starch digestion requires α-amylase to retain a catalytically functional three-dimensional structure. Standard denaturation and linearization with SDS, reducing agent, and heat would unfold the enzyme and normally abolish the activity needed to create the clear zones. The intended method is therefore activity-preserving, native PAGE rather than ordinary denaturing SDS-PAGE.

**Qualifications.** Figure 1A directly measures only early growth in the experimental setup, not the complete time from sowing to harvest after soil application; statement A and the official explanation extrapolate beyond the measured endpoint. Likewise, the intact-seed versus isolated-embryo comparison supports a barrier somewhere in the tissues surrounding the embryo but does not by itself identify the seed coat, rather than the pericarp, endosperm, or another whole-seed difference, as the sole cause. For D, some zymography protocols can use partially denaturing electrophoresis followed by enzyme renaturation, but no renaturation step is given here, so the official false verdict follows from the stated procedure.
