# Part B — Task 35

*Phytochrome B photoconversion, thermosensing, hypocotyl growth, and nuclear bodies*

**Official answer: A — True; B — True; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** Figure 1A shows that red light drives phyB from the red-light-absorbing $P_r$ form to the far-red-light-absorbing $P_{fr}$ form. Figure 1B then provides the temperature comparison. Because every treatment contained the same total amount of phyB, the lower absorbance at 725 nm at higher temperatures indicates a smaller fraction of phyB in the $P_{fr}$ state. For example, the fitted plateau falls from about 0.011 at 10°C to about 0.003 at 30°C. Increasing temperature therefore promotes net thermal reversion from $P_{fr}$ to $P_r$, whereas red light promotes $P_r$ to $P_{fr}$. Their effects on the balance between the two states are opposite.

**B — True.** In Figure 1C, the phyB-null mutant elongates much faster than the wild type at the lower two temperatures. At about 27°C, growth is roughly $2.1\ \mathrm{mm}/9\mathrm{h}$ in the mutant but only $0.55\ \mathrm{mm}/9\mathrm{h}$ in the wild type; a similarly large difference remains near 32°C. Removing phyB therefore releases an inhibition of hypocotyl elongation, showing that phyB normally affects hypocotyl growth negatively. The much smaller difference near 40°C does not overturn the clear inhibitory effect at the other tested temperatures.

**C — False.** Figure 1D uses temperature-insensitive phyB specifically so that light is the relevant cause of interconversion. As the red-to-far-red ratio rises, more phyB is driven into $P_{fr}$, and nuclear-body size rises with it: both logarithmic axes show a positive relationship. This is the opposite of what would be expected if the nuclear bodies were predominantly the $P_r$ form, whose relative abundance falls under red-rich light. The data instead associate larger nuclear bodies with the $P_{fr}$ state. Strictly, the graph measures nuclear-body size rather than their molecular composition, but it does not support the statement's claim of predominant $P_r$.

**D — False.** Leaves above a shaded plant preferentially absorb red light for photosynthesis while allowing proportionally more far-red light to reach the plant below. Canopy shade consequently gives a low red-to-far-red ratio, shifts phyB toward $P_r$, and, by Figure 1D, should produce smaller nuclear bodies. Figure 1E shows smaller nuclear bodies in plant H than in plant K. Thus H, not K, is the plant more consistent with canopy shade.

**Qualification.** Figure 1D establishes the light-dependent relationship using temperature-insensitive phyB, whereas Figure 1E shows wild-type plants grown under otherwise unspecified conditions. Nuclear-body appearance alone therefore cannot prove which plant experienced shade if the plants also differed in temperature or another factor. Statement D says “more likely,” however, and under the intended comparison based on light quality, its direction is still reversed and the official verdict remains false.
