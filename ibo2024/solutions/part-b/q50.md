# Part B — Task 50

*Maternal-effect patterning of the Drosophila body axes*

**Official answer: A — False; B — False; C — True; D — True.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** Bicoid is a maternal-effect determinant: during oogenesis, the mother loads bicoid mRNA into the oocyte and localizes it at the anterior pole. After fertilization, translation of that maternal mRNA produces the anterior-to-posterior Bicoid protein gradient that specifies anterior structures, with high Bicoid concentrations required for head and acron fates and lower concentrations contributing to more posterior fates such as thorax. Depleting bicoid in the father's testes does not remove the bicoid mRNA already supplied by the mother to the egg. A paternal depletion therefore would not produce the stated headless, thorax-deficient phenotype; bicoid would have to be depleted from the female germ line or oocyte to disrupt this early axis.

**B — False.** Exuperantia normally holds bicoid mRNA at the anterior of the oocyte. In a loss-of-function mutant, the mRNA can spread instead of remaining concentrated at that pole, so the embryo cannot form the normal steep Bicoid protein gradient. Figure 1B indicates that the acron occupies the extreme, highest-Bicoid end of the concentration range. Dispersing the maternal mRNA lowers that anterior peak below the threshold needed for acron and head specification; it does not extend the acron. The expected change is loss of the acron and head, with expansion of lower-threshold, more posterior identities such as thorax.

**C — True.** The reciprocal tissue combinations distinguish where Torpedo is needed. A torpedo-deficient oocyte surrounded by wild-type follicle cells develops a normal body plan, whereas a wild-type oocyte surrounded by torpedo-deficient follicle cells produces an entirely ventralized embryo. Thus the decisive Torpedo activity is in the somatic follicle cells that receive the oocyte's Gurken signal, not in the oocyte itself. Torpedo expression in the oocyte is therefore unnecessary for establishment of the normal dorsal–ventral axis under the conditions described.

**D — True.** Loss of Torpedo from all follicle cells eliminates Gurken–Torpedo signaling and gives an entirely ventral embryo. The side with strong Gurken signaling must therefore be the side that acquires or induces dorsal identity, while the weak-signal side corresponds to ventral identity. In Figure 1C, Gurken concentration is highest in the dark region at the bottom and decreases toward the light region at the top; the arrow points upward from high to low concentration. It consequently points from the future dorsal side toward the future ventral side.

**Figure-reference note.** The official explanation for B refers to “Figure 2,” but this task contains only Figure 1; the relevant panel is Figure 1B. This apparent numbering typo does not affect the official truth pattern.
