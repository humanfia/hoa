# Part B — Task 36

*Photoperiod, CONSTANS, flowering time, and seed development*

**Official answer: A — False; B — True; C — True; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — False.** Days from germination to flowering (DGF) are a measure of flowering time: more days mean later, not enhanced, flowering. For *Vigna umbellata*, Figure 1A shows roughly 65 DGF under the long-day 16:8 regime, compared with about 35 DGF under the short-day 12:12 regime. Long days therefore delay flowering. They also lower seed mass, from approximately 13–14 g under 12:12 to about 9–10 g under 16:8. Both parts of the statement are opposite to the data.

**B — True.** *Lotus japonicus* has a strong long-day response in Figure 1A: it flowers after only about 50–60 days under 16:8, but after roughly 190 days under 12:12. Plants measure photoperiod largely by the duration of uninterrupted darkness. A red-light pulse in the middle of a 12-hour night produces a phytochrome-mediated “night break,” so the plant perceives two shorter dark periods rather than one long one. This mimics the short-night signal that promotes flowering in a long-day plant and should therefore reduce DGF, meaning that flowering is accelerated.

**C — True.** In the CO-transgenic seeds, Dex makes CO functional. Activating CO with Dex at 2, 4, or 6 days after pollination produces a significant increase in final seed size relative to the corresponding control, whereas activation at 8 or 10 days has no significant effect. Thus CO can alter the developmental outcome when active early, but has little detectable influence when activated later. This supports the conclusion that CO activity is more important during the early stages of embryo/seed development.

**D — False.** A mature ovule represents the pre-fertilization condition, and its integuments later contribute to the seed coat. Figure 1C shows no meaningful difference in mature ovule size between wild type and the CO loss-of-function mutant, so the data do not support a role for CO in setting size before double fertilization. By contrast, Figure 1B shows that Dex-induced CO activity during the early post-pollination seed-development period can increase seed size. The supported conclusion is therefore a post-fertilization role, not regulation both before and after double fertilization.

**Notes on the official explanation.** Its verdicts are internally consistent with the figures, but it has minor wording errors: for A it says long days reduce seed **size**, although Figure 1A and the statement concern seed **mass**; for D it refers to “Figure 3,” which should be Figure 1C. It also treats “after pollination” as automatically meaning “after double fertilization.” Strictly, pollination occurs before fertilization, although the 2–10-day post-pollination seed stages in Figure 1B are plainly intended as post-fertilization development.
