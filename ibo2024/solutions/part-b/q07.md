# Part B — Task 7

*DNA fiber assays: replication-fork progression, termination, and origin firing*

**Official answer: A — True; B — True; C — False; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** CldU was supplied first and appears red, whereas IdU was supplied second and appears blue. A fork that remains active through both pulses therefore leaves an older red tract followed, in its direction of travel, by a newer blue tract. Panel A has this sequence at only one end of the red tract, so it can be produced by one progressing fork. Panel B instead has a central red tract with blue extensions at both ends. This is the expected pattern when an origin launches two forks in opposite directions: both forks synthesize red-labeled DNA during the first pulse and then extend outward with blue-labeled DNA during the second pulse. (The official explanation calls IdU green, but the question and Figure 1 identify it as blue; this color-name mismatch does not affect the interpretation.)

**B — True.** In panel C, the first-pulse red tracts lie outside the second-pulse blue tracts. Thus a fork from the left moved rightward from red into blue, while a fork from the right moved leftward from red into blue. Their later blue tracts meet in the middle, the characteristic pattern of two converging forks completing replication of the intervening region. Panel E contains a red tract but no subsequent blue extension, so synthesis on that tract ceased before or at the change to the IdU pulse; the official key counts this as a termination pattern. There is, however, a genuine ambiguity in the official marking: a red-only tract can also result from a stalled or collapsed fork, as the official explanation itself acknowledges. Panel E therefore does not uniquely *demonstrate* normal replication termination. The answer here retains the required official **True**, but the word “demonstrate” is stronger than the evidence supports.

**C — False.** CldU is a thymidine analogue available to every DNA polymerase during the first pulse; it does not selectively label only one mode of strand synthesis. At each replication fork, one daughter strand is synthesized continuously as the leading strand and the other discontinuously as the lagging strand, and both incorporate CldU. Because panel B represents two forks moving away from an origin, first-pulse labeling occurs in both leading- and lagging-strand products, not exclusively in one or the other.

**D — False.** Panel F has the sequence blue–red–blue–red–blue along one uninterrupted DNA molecule. Each red first-pulse region with later blue extension on both sides is an origin-firing signature, so the panel records two origins on the same molecule. Under the standard chromosome-replication model used here, a bacterial chromosome initiates from one origin, whereas a eukaryotic chromosome uses many origins. Panel F is therefore inconsistent with the proposed bacterial origin of the DNA and supports the official False classification.
