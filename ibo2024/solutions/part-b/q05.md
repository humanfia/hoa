# Part B — Task 5

*Ketogenic-diet seizure protection, GABA production, and catecholamines*

**Official answer: A — True; B — False; C — True; D — False.**

## Reasoning

**A — True.** Ketolysis ultimately supplies acetyl-CoA, which combines with oxaloacetate to make citrate in reaction 2. Increased use of oxaloacetate in this reaction lowers its availability. The coupled transamination shown in Figure 1 can be written as

$$
\text{aspartate}+\alpha\text{-ketoglutarate}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{oxaloacetate}+\text{glutamate}.
$$

Removing oxaloacetate by citrate formation pulls this equilibrium to the right. A ketogenic diet therefore favors conversion of alpha-ketoglutarate into glutamate; that glutamate can subsequently be converted to the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA in reaction 3.

**B — False.** A lower $K_m$ generally means that the reaction-2 enzyme has greater effective affinity for its substrate and, at a substrate concentration below saturation with unchanged $V_{\max}$, a higher rate. Faster reaction 2 would consume more oxaloacetate, drive the transamination above toward glutamate, and make more substrate available for GABA production. Because GABA reduces neuronal excitability, the intended prediction is greater resistance to fluorothyl-induced seizures and hence a **longer**, not shorter, latency to the first seizure in ketogenic-diet $Dbh^{+/-}$ mice. The proposed decrease in latency therefore has the direction reversed.

**C — True.** L-DOPA is converted into dopamine, so it can replenish dopamine when endogenous dopamine production is inadequate, as in Parkinson's disease. In animals with functional DBH, dopamine can also be converted into noradrenaline and then adrenaline. Figure 2 shows that the ketogenic diet significantly lengthens seizure latency in $Dbh^{+/-}$ mice, which retain this downstream pathway, but not in $Dbh^{-/-}$ mice, which cannot make noradrenaline or adrenaline from dopamine. The intended inference is therefore that L-DOPA can supply precursor for a catecholamine-dependent anti-seizure effect as well as supply dopamine for Parkinson's treatment. This is the official basis for marking C true, although the experiment did not administer L-DOPA itself and thus does not, by itself, establish L-DOPA as a clinical anticonvulsant.

**D — False.** Removing DBH eliminates noradrenaline and also prevents production of adrenaline, because adrenaline lies downstream of noradrenaline. The loss of the ketogenic diet's significant effect in $Dbh^{-/-}$ mice therefore cannot identify which of these two catecholamines is required. Moreover, the experiment measures seizure latency, not GABA concentration or a direct interaction between noradrenaline and GABA signaling. It supports a dependence of ketogenic-diet protection on the DBH-dependent catecholamine branch, but it is not conclusive evidence that noradrenaline specifically enhances GABA's anti-seizure action.
