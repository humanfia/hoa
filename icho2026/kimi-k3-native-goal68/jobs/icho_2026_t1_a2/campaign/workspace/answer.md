## 1. Identifying A

The constraints on A from the shared context (page Q1-3) are:

1. A is a **well-known substance** and Z — a *blue* component of the elixir — is a derivative
   of it.
2. A has **two perpendicular planes of symmetry**.
3. On heating, A **isomerises** into an aromatic compound B with **three mutually perpendicular
   planes of symmetry**.

A is **azulene** (C₁₀H₈), the classic deep-blue hydrocarbon:

```
        8    9
       / \  ||
      7═══1═══2           five-membered ring 0-1-9-8-7 fused to the
       \  0    |          seven-membered ring 1-2-3-4-5-6-0 along the
        6      3          edge 0–1; one Kekulé form has 1=9, 3=4, 5=6, 7=8
         \\  //           (fusion bond 0-1 single)
          4═══5
```

Why azulene fits every constraint:

- **Well-known + colour chemical sense.** Azulene is the textbook blue conjugated hydrocarbon
  (the other compound families in the table are colourless terpene alcohols/ketones); blue
  substituted azulenes (the Chamomilla-derived guaiazulene family) are the classic "blue
  elixir" compounds.
- **Exactly two perpendicular planes.** Azulene is planar with the vertical C₂ᵥ-style symmetry
  of its framework: (i) the molecular plane of the planar π-system and (ii) the in-plane
  vertical plane through the midpoints of the fusion bond and the opposite side of the
  seven-membered ring (atom permutation `(0 1)(2 6)(3 5)(7 9)`, fixing the two axis atoms).
  In 3D these two planes are perpendicular to each other, and azulene has no third mirror
  plane: the 5-ring/7-ring asymmetry destroys the short-axis in-plane reflection that
  naphthalene has.
- **Thermal isomerisation to aromatic B.** Heating azulene (~350 °C) isomerises it to
  naphthalene — the famous azulene → naphthalene rearrangement of polycyclic hydrocarbon
  chemistry.

## 2. Identifying B

B is the aromatic thermal-isomerisation product of A; an isomerisation conserves the molecular
formula, so B is also C₁₀H₈. The well-known aromatic hydrocarbon C₁₀H₈ is **naphthalene**
(two fused benzene rings):

```
   0 ══ 1         rings 0-1-2-3-4-9 (left) and 4-5-6-7-8-9 (right),
  ||    \         fused along the edge 4–9; one Kekulé form
   9     2        0=1, 2=3, 4=9, 5=6, 7=8
  /       \
  4        3
  ||       ||
  5        8
   \\ - 7 /
     6====
```

Naphthalene has **three mutually perpendicular planes of symmetry** (D₂ₕ framework):
the molecular plane, the in-plane long-axis reflection (exchanging the two rings,
permutation `v ↦ v + 5` in the numbering above) and the in-plane short-axis reflection
(permutation `(0 3)(1 2)(4 9)(5 8)(6 7)`). The two in-plane reflections are distinct and
commute, hence perpendicular; the molecular plane is perpendicular to both. All requirements
(aromatic; same formula C₁₀H₈ as A; three mutually perpendicular planes) are met, and B is the
unique well-known answer.

## 3. Identifying Z

Z is the blue component of the elixir and a derivative of A = azulene. Scanning the plant
table (page Q1-2): only the *Chamomilla* row contains azulene-skeleton entries, and among them
only **compound 7 (C₁₄H₁₆)** is a hydrocarbon azulene derivative (compound 8, C₁₅H₂₄, is a
saturated guaiene-type tricyclic hydrocarbon, not an azulene, and compound 3 is a terpene
alcohol). Compound 7's skeletal drawing is a fused [5.7] azulene core bearing an **ethyl**
group on the seven-membered ring, a **methyl** on the seven-membered ring next to the fusion,
and a **methyl** on the five-membered ring: **4-ethyl-1,7-dimethylazulene**:

```
          8    9──CH₃        ethyl at ring atom 4, methyl at ring atom 7
         / \  ||             (seven-ring, adjacent to the fusion) and methyl at
        7═══1═══2            ring atom 1 (five-ring); azulene Kekulé pattern on
        |CH₃ 0   |           the core: 1=9, 3=4, 5=6, 7=8
        6        3
         \\    // \
          5═══4    \
                CH₂─CH₃
```

**Mass-spectrum confirmation.** Monochlorination of Z replaces one H by Cl, giving
C₁₄H₁₅Cl. The molecular ion then appears as the pair

- ³⁵Cl isotopologue: 12·14 + 1·15 + 35 = **218**,
- ³⁷Cl isotopologue: 12·14 + 1·15 + 37 = **220** (the M, M+2 pair),

with the ³⁵Cl:³⁷Cl natural abundance ratio ≈ 75.8 : 24.2 ≈ **3 : 1** — exactly the
observed pair at m/z 218 and 220 with intensity ratio 3:1, and the M+2 spacing of 2 is the
signature of chlorine. The molecular formula C₁₄H₁₆ (M = 184) is fully consistent with the
structure: the azulene core C₁₀H₈ loses three ring hydrogens at the three substituent
attachment points, and C₂H₅ + 2 CH₃ add 3 carbons and 11 hydrogens: carbons 10 + 4 = 14,
hydrogens (8 − 3) + 11 = 16.

Hence **Z is compound 7, 4-ethyl-1,7-dimethylazulene**.
