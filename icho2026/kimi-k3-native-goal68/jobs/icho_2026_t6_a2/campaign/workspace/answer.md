# IChO 2026 T6-A2 (question 6.2) — Structures B–D in the AFM synthesis of cyclo[14]carbon

## Source-grounding (what is read from the problem page itself)

       **C₁₄Cl₁₀** —(−2 Cl•)→ **A** —(−5 Cl•)→ **B** —(−2 Cl•)→ **C** —(−Cl•)→ **C₁₄**
       **A** —(−3 Cl•)→ **D**

    - **A** = C₁₄Cl₈;  **B** = C₁₄Cl₃;  **C** = C₁₄Cl₁;  **D** = C₁₄Cl₅;  C₁₄ = C₁₄.
    (10 − 2 − 5 − 2 − 1 = 0; the A→D branch removes 8 − 3 = 5.)

2.  The structure of **A itself is drawn** (both in the scheme and as the “example” in the answer sheet’s 6.2 box): the intact anthracene skeleton bearing **8 Cl** on the outer perimeter (two on the upper-left arc, three on the outer left, three on the outer right in the left/right rings), all four ring junctions unchlorinated, and **two radical dots** drawn on the two central-ring meso carbons (the “9,10-positions” of anthracene). So A is the anthracene-9,10-diyl diradical C₁₄Cl₈ with every other bond intact.

3.  The generic retro-Bergman step, “(1)”: a six-membered carbocycle carrying *unpaired electrons on the 1,4-related ring carbons* (a p-dehydrobenzene / cyclohexa-1,4-diyl-3-ene ring) opens by breaking the ring bond between those two radical-bearing carbons, producing an acyclic **hex-3-ene-1,5-diyne** unit (two terminal alkynes conjugated with a central C=C double bond). This is drawn explicitly: cyclohexa-ene 1,4-diradical → open-chain enediyne.

4.  The question prompt: “**Draw** structures B–D. Note they may contain one or more unpaired electrons.” (11.0 pt)

5.  The final AFM image labelled **C₁₄** shows a featureless single ring — the target cyclo[14]carbon.

## Derivation (using only the above + general mechanism)

Halogen abstraction from aryl chloride by the AFM tip takes place one C–Cl bond at a time; each abstraction leaves the carbon’s newly freed lobe as an *unpaired electron* sitting on the carbon where Cl used to be (the note in the problem says exactly this: “they may contain one or more unpaired electrons”). A drawn aromatic/alkene framework is only a bookkeeping device: what must be tracked per structure is **which carbons still carry Cl**, **where the unpaired electrons sit**, and **whether a retro-Bergman ring opening has already occurred** (the only bond-breaking/bond-making step on the page, shown by scheme (1) and required to turn three fused six-membered rings into a single fourteen-membered ring).

### B — C₁₄Cl₅ → C₁₄Cl₃, diradical (still tricyclic)

A → B removes **five** Cl• in one image-to-image step without any retro-Bergman opening (no new six-membered-ring cleavage is possible yet because the only 1,4-diradical arrangement that can open is the central meso pair of A — which would already have to open in B, losing its diradical character later needed for C).  Hence B has the **same intact anthracene framework as A** with five of the remaining eight peripheral chlorines gone.  Of the eight peripheral Cl of A the three leftmost/outermost remain in B because they are the sterically most exposed / first-imaged ones to have survived: leaving the chlorine pattern **Cl on {lt, lbl, rl}** and the two unpaired electrons still on the two central meso carbons **cu· and cl·** (the 9,10-diyl diradical).  So **B = 9,10-dehydroanthracene bearing three Cl (C₁₄Cl₃), all sixteen C–C skeleton bonds intact, exactly two unpaired electrons.**

### C — C₁₄Cl₁, retro-Bergman-opened diradical

B → C removes **two** more Cl• **and** the AFM image of C differs qualitatively — the molecule on the surface now appears as a *single, closed outer contour* rather than three fused rings (the AFM frame for C already looks like a ring with one residual bump). This is the fingerprint of the retro-Bergman step (1) having occurred.  As drawn in (1), ring opening happens between the two carbons carrying unpaired electrons when they are 1,4-related on a six-membered ring; in B they sit on the two meso carbons of the central ring, so **the central ring opens between its two halogen-free sides, cleaving exactly the two skeleton bonds incident on the lower meso carbon (cl): bonds jb–cl and cl–jrB are broken and the opened path cl–… is converted into the alkyne arm of an enediyne**.  Concretely C is still C-skeleton bonded everywhere except those two bonds; the now-unpaired electrons migrate to the two junction carbons **jb· and jrB·** at the ends of the opened arm (the two carbons the broken bonds used to connect to cl).  Of B's three chlorines the two adjacent to the opening ring are abstracted, so **C carries one remaining Cl (on lt), exactly two unpaired electrons (on jb and jrB), and exactly two opened skeleton bonds — the retro-Bergman ring opening of scheme (1)**.  C = C₁₄Cl₁.

### D — C₁₄Cl₅, triradical (still tricyclic), made from A by −3 Cl•

D branches off A before any ring opening.  The arrow removes **three** Cl• while the AFM frame of D still shows the familiar three-lobed (fused-ring) outline, so D keeps the **intact anthracene skeleton** with three of A's eight peripheral chlorines removed.  Each abstraction leaves its unpaired electron behind: A's two meso radicals persist, and the *third* radical sits on the carbon that just lost Cl on the right outer arc (the position adjacent to the central ring and thus part of the former dehydrobenzene-like unit).  **D = A minus Cl• at {rt, rr, rb} (right outer arc), i.e. C₁₄Cl₅, skeleton fully intact, unpaired electrons on cu, cl (meso pair, inherited from A) and rt — exactly three unpaired electrons overall.**  (−3 Cl• ⇒ 8 − 3 = 5 Cl, and 2 + 1 = 3 radicals.)
