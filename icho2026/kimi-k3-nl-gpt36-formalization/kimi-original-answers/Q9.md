## Detailed solution

### Conventions used throughout

- D-glucopyranose absolute stereochemistry (Fischer → Haworth, D-series): C1–OR down (α), C2–OH down, C3–OH up, C4–O(glycosidic) down, C5–CH₂OH up.
- Chair axial directions: **⁴C₁**: C1↓, C2↑, C3↓, C4↑, C5↓ (equatorial points the other way at each carbon). A ring flip to **¹C₄** keeps every up/down but swaps axial/equatorial everywhere: axial = C1↑, C2↓, C3↑, C4↓, C5↑.
- CD connectivity: units are numbered clockwise; the α-1,4-glycosidic bond is C1(unit *i*)–O–C4(unit *i*+1).
- Abbreviations: Bn = CH₂C₆H₅; TBS(=TBDMS) = SiMe₂ᵗBu; Ms = SO₂CH₃; Ts = SO₂C₆H₄CH₃; Boc = CO₂ᵗBu; DIBAL-H = (ⁱBu)₂AlH.
- Integer atomic masses for 9.7: C = 12, H = 1, O = 16, Na = 23.

### 9.1 Molar mass of β-CD

β-CD is a *cyclic* heptamer: 7 glucose units joined by 7 glycosidic bonds, each formed with loss of one H₂O (7 dehydrations, not 6):

\[
M(\beta\text{-CD}) = 7\,M(\text{glucose}) - 7\,M(\mathrm{H_2O})
= 7(180.16) - 7(18.02)
= 1261.12 - 126.14
= \boxed{1134.98\ \text{g mol}^{-1}}
\]

with M(H₂O) = 2(1.008) + 16.00 = 18.02 g mol⁻¹ from the relative-atomic-mass table. Check by composition: 7 × C₆H₁₂O₆ − 7 H₂O = C₄₂H₇₀O₃₅; 42(12.011) + 70(1.008) + 35(16.00) ≈ 1135.0 g mol⁻¹ ✓ (= 7 × 162.14 g mol⁻¹ per anhydroglucose residue ✓).

### 9.2 Compound K — tick ¹C₄

**Reading the reagents.** Seven equivalents of TsCl convert the seven *primary* C6–OH groups into tosylates (primary vs secondary selectivity). Hydroxide then generates the C3 alkoxide; in every residue O3⁻ displaces the primary tosylate intramolecularly at C6:

\[
\mathrm{C3\!-\!O^- \;+\; C6\!-\!OTs \;\longrightarrow\; C3\!-\!O\!-\!C6 \;+\; TsO^-}
\]

Net result per residue: loss of the elements of water, one new 3,6-anhydro ether bridge, and the C2–OH as the **only remaining OH**. Overall: C₄₂H₇₀O₃₅ − 7 H₂O = **C₄₂H₅₆O₂₈** (one free OH and one new ether bridge per residue — precisely the statement "one OH group remained free per glucopyranoside unit"). K is therefore **heptakis(3,6-anhydro)-β-CD**, not a conventionally protected β-CD with free 6-OH.

**Which chair to tick.** The new O–C6 tether connects the substituents at C3 and C5. These two carbons are 1,3-related; their substituents approach one another only when **both bonds are axial on the same face** (1,3-diaxial, both "up"). That geometry is the **¹C₄** chair: in ¹C₄ the axial directions at C3 and C5 are both up, so O3 (bond up at C3) and C6 (bond up at C5) are juxtaposed and the bridge can close. In ⁴C₁ the same two bonds are both *equatorial* and point apart — too far to be spanned by one oxygen — so ⁴C₁ is incompatible with the 3,6-bridge. **Tick ¹C₄.** Once closed, the fused bicyclic bridge locks this conformation; as a side benefit the α-anomeric OR becomes equatorial.

**Per-residue stereochemical description of K (each of the seven identical residues, ¹C₄):**

- C1–O(glycosidic): down, equatorial (α retained);
- C2–OH: down, axial — the only free OH;
- C3–O and C5–C6: both up, axial, joined to each other by the O3–C6 ether bridge (bridge on the upper face);
- C4–O(glycosidic): down, axial.

No stereocentre is altered: the S_N2 displacement occurs at the primary carbon C6, which is not a stereocentre; all sugar configurations (D-gluco) are retained, only the chair flips. Template filling: join the 3-O box and the 6 box by the anhydro O–CH₂ link (drawn above each ring), and mark the 2-O box as free OH, on all seven units.

### 9.3 Ring size and stereocentres of macrocycle X

The scheme leading to X (the demonstration that "CDs combine macrocyclic properties with glucose chemistry") oxidatively cleaves the C2–C3 bond of **every** glucose residue (Malaprade-type 2,3-seco cleavage of the vicinal-diol stage), reduces both new termini with borohydride, and O-acetylates them. Every C2 and C3 therefore ends up as a –CH₂O– (–CH₂OAc) tail.

**Ring size.** The macrocycle survives because its path never passes through C2 or C3: per residue the unique large-ring path is

\[
\mathrm{C1 - O_{glycosidic} - C4 - C5 - O5} \;=\; 5\ \text{atoms},
\qquad r_s = 7\times 5 = \boxed{35}.
\]

(The severed 2,3-tails hang off C1 and C4 and are not part of the loop; any 3,6-bridge as in K would likewise lie outside the loop.)

**Stereocentres.** Per residue, C1 (substituents H, O5, O_gly, CH₂OAc), C4 (H, O_gly from the previous C1, C5-side, CH₂OAc) and C5 (H, O5, C4-side, CH₂OAc) each still bear four different groups; C2 and C3 have become methylene carbons (two identical H), and C6 was a CH₂ already:

\[
s_c = 7\times 3 = \boxed{21}.
\]

Counting 35 would correspond to an *intact* β-CD pyranose skeleton (5 centres per unit), which is not the structure of X drawn in the scheme: the cleavage/reduction removes exactly 2 centres per residue, 35 − 14 = 21.

### 9.4 Structure of Y — the 6-O-TBS-2,3-anhydro epoxide

**Chemistry.** α-CD + ≈6 equiv TBSCl in pyridine silylates only the six primary 6-OH groups (primary/secondary discrimination, as in 9.2). Regioselective sulfonylation of the C2 hydroxy group (the most reactive secondary OH of glucose) is then followed by base: the neighbouring O3 alkoxide attacks C2 intramolecularly and expels the sulfonate. The S_N2 displacement **inverts C2 only** (its new C–O bond points up); the C3–O bond is not broken (retained, up). Each residue of Y therefore carries a 2,3-epoxide on the **upper face**.

Per residue of Y (identical for all six): C1–O(glycosidic) down (α, unchanged); C2–O(epoxide) up (inverted); C3–O(epoxide) up (retained); C4–O(glycosidic) down (unchanged); C5–CH₂OTBS up (never deprotected). Template filling: connect the 2-O and 3-O positions of every unit as one epoxide oxygen drawn above the ring; the 6 box = `CH2OTBS`.

**Formula check (vs the formula printed for Y in the scheme).**

\[
\underbrace{\mathrm{C_{36}H_{60}O_{30}}}_{\alpha\text{-CD}} + \underbrace{6\,\mathrm{C_6H_{14}Si}}_{6\times\mathrm{O\!-\!TBS\ replacing\ H}} - \underbrace{6\,\mathrm{H_2O}}_{6\times\text{epoxide formation}} = \mathrm{C_{72}H_{132}O_{24}Si_6}
\]

exactly the printed formula. This also rules out the alternative "altro-configured 2,3-diol" candidate (C₇₂H₁₄₄O₃₀Si₆ — heavier by 6 × H₂O): the altro geometry is produced only **later**, when the epoxide is hydrolysed at C3 (second inversion) after desilylation, on the way to α-cycloaltrin. Y must be drawn as the epoxide.

### 9.5 Structure of L

β-CD has 7 × 3 = 21 OH groups; NaH/BnCl perbenzylates all of them. DIBAL-H (2 equiv) then performs Sinay's directed double debenzylation: the first cleavage frees one primary OH — this defines unit 1 (6A–OH, protic); this protic group coordinates the Lewis-acidic aluminium and directs the *next* primary debenzylation to **unit 4 (6D)** of the same ring (or to unit 3 if 6D is unavailable), as stated in the problem. L is therefore the 6A,6D-diol shown in the table in the final answers: units 1 and 4 carry `CH2OH`, units 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 carry `CH2OBn`, and all fourteen secondary positions remain OBn. Count check: 21 OH sites − 2 free = **19 benzyl ethers** ✓; all seven units are ordinary ⁴C₁ α-D-glucopyranosides.

### 9.6 Number of isomers of the β-CD dimer

The site counting must use the two genuinely free positions of L — A = 6A (unit 1) and B = 6D (unit 4), which are constitutionally different on the oriented α-1,4 seven-membered ring. Mono-functionalisation (alkenyl tether) can occur at A or at B, and connecting two such monomers (metathesis, then hydrogenation of the tether) gives the unordered site pairs

\[
(A,A),\quad (A,B),\quad (B,B) \;\;=\;\; \text{1–1,\;1–4,\;4–4}.
\]

Interchanging the two identical β-CD halves adds nothing, and hydrogenating the tether changes no attachment site. Hence **3 constitutional linkage isomers** — the mixture the scheme reports. (Note these are the pairings of the two free sites of one L; they are not different debenzylation *patterns* of the diol.)

### 9.7 m/z of the two [M+Na]⁺ peaks from the hexo-5-enose degradation of L

**Which bonds are cut.** The two free 6-OH groups of L (units 1 and 4) become two 6-deoxy-hex-5-enose units; hydrolysis of these 5-enoses (the vinyl-ether-like O5–C5=C unit opens, and the glycosidic bond at that residue's C1 is cleaved) cuts the heptameric ring exactly at the two marked residues. The ring therefore splits into the two arcs between them:

\[
\{2,3\}\;(2\ \text{intact residues}) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \{5,6,7\}\;(3\ \text{intact residues}).
\]

Each of the two isolated products = the intact residues of one arc + one degraded hexo-5-enose-derived terminus on one side + the acetate (AcO) end group on the other, exactly as drawn in the degradation scheme.

**Additive composition (read from the drawn fragment structures).**

- intact per-O-benzyl glucopyranosyl residue (ring O5, one glycosidic O, three OBn): **R = C₂₇H₂₈O₅**, M(R) = 27(12) + 28 + 5(16) = 432;
- end block (degraded hexo-5-enose-derived terminus together with the AcO end group): **E = C₂₄H₂₈O₆**, M(E) = 24(12) + 28 + 6(16) = 412.

**Product masses (integer masses, z = 1).**

- 3-residue product = 2R + E = C₇₈H₈₄O₁₆:
  \(M = 78(12) + 84 + 16(16) = 936 + 84 + 256 = 1276\); [M+Na]⁺ = 1276 + 23 = **m/z 1299**;
- 4-residue product = 3R + E = C₁₀₅H₁₁₂O₂₁:
  \(M = 105(12) + 112 + 21(16) = 1260 + 112 + 336 = 1708\); [M+Na]⁺ = 1708 + 23 = **m/z 1731**.

Consistency checks: both formulae are closed-shell (even H counts); ΔM = 1731 − 1299 = 432 = exactly one intact perbenzylated residue, as required for chains differing by one unit.

**Diagnostic value.** Observing the 3-residue/4-residue pair (1299/1731) and *not* the 2-residue/5-residue pair (R₁ + E → 867, R₄ + E → 2163, by the same arithmetic) proves that L was cut at units 1 and 4, i.e. the 6A,6D (1,4-directed) substitution pattern — the purpose of the degradation. (Values near 1229/1661 would correspond to uncapped free-OH ends and unelaborated termini, which contradict the fragments drawn in the official scheme; they are rejected.)

### 9.8 Structures O–S (Sollogoub hexadifferentiation)

**Rules applied (from the problem and its hints).** Every DIBAL-H step removes one primary `CH2OBn` → `CH2OH`; the directing group already on the ring chooses the site: a **protic** group (free OH or N–H) at unit *i* directs to the 1,4-related (opposite) primary position *i*±3, and if that position is unavailable, to the 1,3-related position **counterclockwise** (*i*−2); an **alkene** at unit *i* directs to the 1,2 "ortho" position **clockwise** (*i*+1); **protic groups out-direct alkenes**. Azide, Boc-carbamate, the tertiary N(Bn) of a bridge, and ethers are **non-directing**. Units 1–6 run clockwise as printed. The twelve secondary positions stay `OBn` throughout, and no stereocentre is ever touched (all chemistry is at the primary rim), so every unit remains ⁴C₁ α-D-gluco.

**N (context; the scheme's starting point).** Mono-debenzylated perbenzyl-α-CD: `CH2OH` at unit 1, `CH2OBn` at units 2–6.

**N → O.** Swern oxidation of the unit-1 `CH2OH` gives the aldehyde; methylenation (Wittig) converts it into the terminal alkene. **O:** u1 = `CH=CH2`; u2–u6 = `CH2OBn`.

**O → P.** The vinyl group at u1 directs one DIBAL-H debenzylation to its clockwise 1,2 (ortho) neighbour u2; mesylation then primary S_N2 with NaN₃ installs the azide. **P:** u1 = `CH=CH2`, u2 = `CH2N3`, u3–u6 = `CH2OBn`.

**P → Q.** Two equivalents of DIBAL-H: the first reduces the u2 azide to `CH2NH2` — a *protic* director — which then directs the second equivalent to the opposite (1,4) unit **u5**, freeing its `CH2OH`. The two nucleophiles (u2 amine, u5 alkoxide) then each displace one chloride of ClCH₂C(=CH₂)CH₂Cl, closing the **single shared bridge** `J_H(2,5)` = (u2)–CH₂–NH–CH₂–C(=CH₂)–CH₂–O–CH₂–(u5); N keeps one H and is still protic. **Q:** u1 = `CH=CH2`; u2 = `J_H` N end; u3, u4, u6 = `CH2OBn`; u5 = `J_H` O end.

**Q → R.** The u2 –NH– bridge director would select the opposite unit u5, but the u5 primary position is now consumed by the bridge, so the fallback applies: 1,3-related, **counterclockwise** from u2 → **u6** is debenzylated. Boc protection (Boc₂O) then removes the directing N–H (`J_Boc`, non-directing), and MsCl then NaN₃ converts the new u6 `CH2OH` into `CH2N3`. (The order matters: the debenzylation must occur while the bridge nitrogen is still protic.) **R:** u1 = `CH=CH2`; u2 = `J_Boc` N end; u3, u4 = `CH2OBn`; u5 = `J_Boc` O end; u6 = `CH2N3`.

**R → S.** TFA removes Boc (bridge N–H restored), and NaH/BnI N-benzylates it to the tertiary amine `J_Bn` — non-protic and therefore non-directing. The final two-equivalent DIBAL-H treatment first reduces the u6 azide to `CH2NH2`; this fresh protic group is a stronger director than either alkene (vinyl at u1, bridge methylene) and directs debenzylation to its opposite (1,4) unit **u3**, freeing the last `CH2OH`; u4 retains the only remaining `CH2OBn`. **S:** u1 = `CH=CH2`; u2 = `J_Bn` N end; u3 = `CH2OH`; u4 = `CH2OBn`; u5 = `J_Bn` O end; u6 = `CH2NH2`.

**Outcome.** S carries six different primary-rim environments (vinyl; N-of-bridge; OH; OBn; O-of-bridge; NH₂) — a hexadifferentiated α-CD. The essential feature, signalled by the hint "You may draw structures outside the boxes", is the **shared N/O bridge first installed in Q**; the intermediates do not consist of six independent capping groups, and the bridge N/O "ends" must each be entered in the boxes of *both* bridged units (u2 and u5) with the tether drawn outside the template.

### 9.9 Number of arrangements on a hexadifferentiated α-CD

Six **different** substituents occupy the six primary positions of α-CD; the six ring units are otherwise constitutionally identical. Arrangements related by one of the six rotations of the macrocycle are the same molecule, so the free position count 6! is reduced by the six-fold rotational degeneracy:

\[
N = \frac{6!}{6} = (6-1)! = \boxed{120}.
\]

The clockwise and counterclockwise sequences are **not** identified: α-CD is built only from chiral D-glucopyranosides joined in a directed α-(1→4) sense, so the scaffold has rotational but no reflection symmetry (no operation reverses the sequence while preserving the structure). Hence there is no further factor of two, and the answer is 120.
