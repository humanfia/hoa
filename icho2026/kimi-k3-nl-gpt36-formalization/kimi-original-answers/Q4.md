## Detailed solution

### 4.1 Atomic abundance of ²³⁵U (2 pt)

Natural uranium's standard atomic mass (periodic table) is *M*(U) = 238.03 a.u. Let *x* be the atomic fraction of ²³⁵U; then ²³⁸U has fraction (1 − *x*).

- 235.04·*x* + 238.05·(1 − *x*) = 238.03
- 238.05 − 3.01·*x* = 238.03
- *x* = (238.05 − 238.03) / 3.01 = 0.02 / 3.01 = 6.6 × 10⁻³

**Atomic abundance of ²³⁵U ≈ 0.66 %**, consistent with the known natural value of about 0.7 %.

### 4.2 Nuclear equations for neutron production (3 pt)

Conservation of mass number (A) and atomic number (Z) is applied to each reaction.

- (a) Alpha-particle bombardment of boron-11, the classic (α,n) reaction:
  ¹¹₅B + ⁴₂α → ¹⁴₇N + ¹₀n
  Check: 11 + 4 = 14 + 1 = 15; 5 + 2 = 7 + 0 = 7 ✓
- (b) Photodisintegration (photoneutron) reaction of deuterium in reactor coolant water:
  γ + ²₁H → ¹₁H + ¹₀n
  Check: 2 = 1 + 1; 1 = 1 + 0 ✓
- (c) Alpha-particle bombardment of beryllium-9 in an external neutron source:
  ⁹₄Be + ⁴₂α → ¹²₆C + ¹₀n
  Check: 9 + 4 = 12 + 1 = 13; 4 + 2 = 6 + 0 = 6 ✓

### 4.3 Main fission reaction of ²³⁵U (3 pt)

The fission of ²³⁵U after neutron absorption gives two fission fragments plus three free neutrons:

²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ᴬ¹z₁X + ᴬ²z₂Y + 3¹₀n

Constraints:
- Mass balance: A₁ + A₂ = 236 − 3 = 233.
- Charge balance: Z₁ + Z₂ = 92.
- The two elements must belong to the **same group** of the periodic table.
- The fragment masses must lie at the two maxima of the fission-yield curve, which the graph places at A ≈ 93 and A ≈ 140.

The only same-group pair with Z₁ + Z₂ = 92 in the fission-fragment region is the group 1 pair Rb (Z = 37) and Cs (Z = 55), since 37 + 55 = 92. The yield-curve maxima at A ≈ 93 and A ≈ 140 sum exactly to 233, matching the mass balance:

**²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ⁹³₃₇Rb + ¹⁴⁰₅₅Cs + 3¹₀n**

Check: 235 + 1 = 93 + 140 + 3 = 236 ✓; 92 = 37 + 55 ✓.

### 4.4 Energy released per fission (2 pt)

Total binding energy increases on fission; the released energy is

ΔE = BE(products) − BE(reactants).

- Reactants: ²³⁵U plus a free neutron (zero binding). BE(reactants) = 235 × 7.59 MeV = 1783.65 MeV.
- Products: 93 + 140 = 233 bound nucleons in the fragments (the 3 free neutrons are neglected as instructed). BE(products) = 233 × 8.45 MeV = 1968.85 MeV.

ΔE = 1968.85 − 1783.65 = 185.2 MeV

**ΔE ≈ 185 MeV per fission (released).**

### 4.5 Slowing-down collisions in water (2 pt)

The logarithmic energy decrement per collision is ξ = ln(Eᵢₙᵢₜᵢₐₗ/Eբᵢₙₐₗ), constant for successive collisions, so after n_c collisions:

ln(E₀/E) = n_c·ξ ⟹ n_c = ln(E₀/E) / ξ

Substitution (E₀ = 2 MeV = 2 × 10⁶ eV; E = 0.012 eV; ξ = 0.948):

- E₀/E = 2 × 10⁶ / 0.012 = 1.667 × 10⁸
- ln(1.667 × 10⁸) = ln(1.667) + 8 ln 10 = 0.511 + 18.421 = 18.93
- n_c = 18.93 / 0.948 = 19.97

**n_c ≈ 20 collisions.**

### 4.6 Standard enthalpy of methane combustion at 298 K (2 pt)

Reaction (all gases): CH₄(g) + 2 O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2 H₂O(g)

ΔrH°₂₉₈ = [ΔfH°(CO₂) + 2 ΔfH°(H₂O,g)] − [ΔfH°(CH₄) + 2 ΔfH°(O₂)]

Substitution (ΔfH° in kJ mol⁻¹; ΔfH°(O₂) = 0):

ΔrH°₂₉₈ = [(−393.5) + 2(−241.8)] − [(−74.8) + 2(0)]
= (−393.5 − 483.6) − (−74.8)
= −877.1 + 74.8

**ΔrH°₂₉₈ = −802.3 kJ mol⁻¹ (per mol CH₄).** Atom check: 1 C, 4 H, 4 O on each side ✓.

### 4.7 Combustion enthalpy at 2000 K (2 pt)

Kirchhoff's law (constant heat capacities, from the general booklet):

ΔrH_T₂ = ΔrH_T₁ + ΔrCp·(T₂ − T₁)

Heat-capacity change (Cp in J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹):

ΔrCp = [Cp(CO₂) + 2 Cp(H₂O,g)] − [Cp(CH₄) + 2 Cp(O₂)]
= [37 + 2(34)] − [35 + 2(29)] = 105 − 93 = +12 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹

With T₂ − T₁ = 2000 − 298 = 1702 K:

ΔrH₂₀₀₀ = −802.3 kJ mol⁻¹ + (12 × 10⁻³ kJ mol⁻¹ K⁻¹)(1702 K)
= −802.3 + 20.4

**ΔrH₂₀₀₀ ≈ −781.9 kJ mol⁻¹ (≈ −782 kJ mol⁻¹).** The magnitude decreases at high temperature because the products have a larger heat capacity than the reactants.

### 4.8 Energy released per day (2 pt)

Methane volume flow at the wellhead: Q = 2.2 × 10⁵ m³ day⁻¹ at P = 101.325 kPa, T = 298 K. Molar flow from the ideal gas law:

n = P·Q / (R·T) = (101325 Pa × 2.2 × 10⁵ m³) / (8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ × 298 K)
= 2.229 × 10¹⁰ / 2477.6 ≈ 9.0 × 10⁶ mol day⁻¹

Energy released on complete isothermic combustion at 2000 K (using ΔrH₂₀₀₀ = −781.9 kJ mol⁻¹):

E = n·|ΔrH₂₀₀₀| = 9.0 × 10⁶ mol day⁻¹ × 7.819 × 10⁵ J mol⁻¹

**E ≈ 7.0 × 10¹² J day⁻¹ (unrounded ≈ 7.03 × 10¹² J day⁻¹).**

### 4.9 Number of fissions and mass of enriched uranium (4 pt)

Total explosion energy (30 kt TNT; 1 ton TNT = 4.184 GJ):

E_total = 30 × 10³ × 4.184 × 10⁹ J = 1.2552 × 10¹⁴ J

Energy per fission (from 4.4, ΔE = 185.2 MeV; 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J):

E₁ = 185.2 × 10⁶ eV × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J eV⁻¹ = 2.967 × 10⁻¹¹ J

**Total number of fissions:**

TN = E_total/E₁ = 1.2552 × 10¹⁴ / 2.967 × 10⁻¹¹ = **4.23 × 10²⁴ fissions**

**Mass of ²³⁵U that underwent fission:**

n(²³⁵U) = TN/N_A = 4.23 × 10²⁴ / 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ = 7.03 mol
m_fiss = 7.03 mol × 235 g mol⁻¹ = 1.65 × 10³ g = **1.65 kg**

**Total mass of enriched uranium:** only 33 % of the ²³⁵U fissioned, and ²³⁵U constitutes 90 % by mass of the enriched uranium:

m(²³⁵U, total) = 1.65 kg / 0.33 = 5.00 kg
m(enriched U) = 5.00 kg / 0.90 = **≈ 5.6 kg**

So the 30 kt explosion consumed about 1.65 kg of ²³⁵U from a charge of ≈ 5.6 kg of 90 %-enriched uranium.
