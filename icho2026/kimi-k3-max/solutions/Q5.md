## Detailed solution

### Fragment inventory and the assembly of PL1 (foundation for 5.1–5.6)

The fragments map onto the cardiolipin building blocks: the three c fragments (3 connections each) are the three glycerol units; the two b fragments (2 connections each) are the two –O–P(=O)(OH)–O– phosphate diester groups; the four d fragments (1 connection each) are the four fatty-acyl residues R–C(=O)–; and the a fragments (1 connection, `~H`) are hydrogen caps terminating any hydroxyl left unesterified. The condition "no peroxide bonds" guarantees that every oxygen stub pairs with a carbon or phosphorus stub (ester or phosphate ester), never O–O. PL1 is therefore 1,3-bis(phosphatidyl)glycerol: three glycerols linked by two phosphate diesters, carrying four identical fatty acids.

Non-ionised PL1 forms from

3 C₃H₈O₃ + 2 H₃PO₄ + 4 RCOOH → PL1 + 8 H₂O,

because 8 ester bonds are created (4 fatty-acid esters + 4 phosphate esters). With RCOOH = C_cH_hO₂:

**PL1 = C(4c+9)H(4h+14)O₁₇P₂.**

Stereoisomerism: the only stereocentres are the C2 carbons of the two terminal glycerols (each bears H, –O–C(=O)R, –CH₂–O–C(=O)R, –CH₂–O–P(···)); the central glycerol C2 carries two identical –CH₂–O–P arms and is not a stereocentre; P atoms are excluded per the problem. Hence one enantiomeric pair (R,R)/(S,S) — the form found in prokaryotes/eukaryotes and the form found in archaea — plus an achiral meso (R,S) diastereomer with a mirror plane, exactly as stated.

### 5.1 Parity of n

Total free valences in the stated collection:

n(1) + 2(2) + 3(3) + 4(1) = n + 17.

Each bond between fragments consumes two valences, so n + 17 must be even ⟹ **n is odd — answer (b)**.

Exact value: there are n + 2 + 3 + 4 = n + 9 fragments; a connected acyclic molecule built from them has n + 8 interfragment bonds. Pairing every stub gives (n + 17)/2 bonds, so

(n + 17)/2 = n + 8 ⟹ n = 1.

That single a-fragment hydrogen terminates the sole unesterified OH in the assembly — the 2-OH of the central glycerol (all other glycerol stubs are consumed by the four fatty-acid esters and the four phosphate-ester bonds).

### 5.2 Structures of PL1 and of the stabilised monoanion Y

PL1 connectivity (one enantiomer, both terminal centres (R)):

```
RC(=O)O–CH2–C*_L(H)(O–C(=O)R)–CH2–O–P(=O)(OH)–O–CH2–CH(OH)–CH2–O–P(=O)(OH)–O–CH2–C*_R(H)(O–C(=O)R)–CH2–O–C(=O)R
```

Here OCOR means –O–C(=O)R, not a direct C–R bond. Reflecting in a mirror gives the (S,S) enantiomer; the (R,S) form is superposable on its mirror image (plane through the central CH(OH)) and is achiral.

Y (after the first deprotonation): one phosphate is anionic, the other still bears its P–OH. The molecule folds so that the free central 2-OH (identified via the n = 1 result of 5.1) sits between the two phosphates:

```
P_L–O–H ··· O_c–H ··· O⁻–P_R
```

H-bonds: P_L–OH···O_c and O_c–H···O(–)⁻P_R; the –CH₂–CH(OH)–CH₂– linker closes two fused intramolecular H-bonded rings around O_c, which acts as a proton relay (the cardiolipin "proton trap"). The arrangement with the two phosphates exchanged is degenerate, so the surviving proton and the single charge are stabilised over two equivalent structures. A bare P–O⁻···H–O–P contact cannot account for the effect — the phosphates are separated by the three-carbon tether, and the model naturally requires the free central OH (which is absent in analogues such as PL2, destroying the effect). A second deprotonation both breaks the cooperative trap and places two mutually repelling negative charges on adjacent phosphates, so

pKₐ₂ ≫ pKₐ₁.

### 5.3 Molecular formula of the fatty acid (RCOOH)

Step 1 — bond-count equation. PL1 = C(4c+9)H(4h+14)O₁₇P₂ is connected and acyclic, so its number of σ bonds equals (atoms) − 1:

N(atoms) = (4c + 9) + (4h + 14) + 17 + 2 = 4c + 4h + 42 ⟹ N(σ) = 4c + 4h + 41.

π bonds: 4 fatty-ester C=O + 2 P=O + 4k fatty-chain C=C (k double bonds per identical residue) = 4k + 6. Given total 255:

(4c + 4h + 41) + (4k + 6) = 255 ⟹ c + h + k = 52.    (I)

Step 2 — number of double bonds. Reductive ozonolysis cleaves every C=C, so a straight chain with k double bonds gives k + 1 carbonyl fragments. The carboxyl-end and methyl-end fragments each occur exactly once and are necessarily different from each other and from any internal fragment. Exactly three different products form in **equimolar** (1 : 1 : 1) amounts, so each product occurs once and there are three fragments:

k + 1 = 3 ⟹ k = 2.

Step 3 — solve. An acyclic monocarboxylic acid (one C=O plus k C=C) has h = (2c + 2) − 2(1 + k) = 2c − 2k.    (II)

Substituting (II) with k = 2 into (I):

c + (2c − 4) + 2 = 52 ⟹ 3c = 54 ⟹ c = 18,  h = 32.

**RCOOH = C₁₈H₃₂O₂**, M = 18(12.01) + 32(1.008) + 2(16.00) = 280.44 g mol⁻¹. This is the formula of linoleic acid, CH₃(CH₂)₄CH=CHCH₂CH=CH(CH₂)₇COOH, the predominant fatty acid of mammalian heart cardiolipin; its reductive ozonolysis gives OHC(CH₂)₇COOH (9-oxononanoic acid) + OHCCH₂CHO (propanedial) + CH₃(CH₂)₄CHO (hexanal) — three different products, equimolar ✓.

Check: PL1 = C₈₁H₁₄₂O₁₇P₂ → 81 + 142 + 17 + 2 = 242 atoms → 241 σ; π = 4 C=O + 8 C=C + 2 P=O = 14; 241 + 14 = 255 ✓ (equivalently, total bond order = (4·81 + 142 + 2·17 + 5·2)/2 = 255 ✓).

### 5.4 Identification of X

Stoichiometry with iodine (A(I) = 126.90 g mol⁻¹, M(I₂) = 253.80 g mol⁻¹):

n(RCOOH) = 100 g / (280.44 g mol⁻¹) = 0.3566 mol
n(I₂) = 181.0 g / (253.80 g mol⁻¹) = 0.7132 mol
n(I₂) : n(RCOOH) = 0.7132 / 0.3566 = 2.00 : 1,

i.e. exactly two addition sites per acid molecule — independent confirmation of the two double bonds found in 5.3. "Reacts in a similar way with X" means X adds across both C=C bonds (2 X per acid), and since the adduct contains iodine, X must carry the iodine; taking one I atom per X (the I₂ analogue), the adduct contains 2 I:

M(adduct) = 2(126.90) / 0.3657 = 253.80 / 0.3657 = 694.01 g mol⁻¹
M(X) = (694.01 − 280.44) / 2 = 206.79 g mol⁻¹
206.79 − 126.90 = 79.89 g mol⁻¹ ≈ A(Br) = 79.90 g mol⁻¹.

**X = IBr** (the classical Hanus-type iodine monobromide reagent). Verification: w(I) = 253.80 / (280.44 + 2 × 206.79) = 253.80 / 694.02 = 0.36569 ≈ 36.57 % ✓.

Alternatives are excluded on the observed 2 : 1 basis: I₂ would give w(I) = 2(253.80) / (280.44 + 2 × 253.80) = 507.60 / 788.04 = 64.4 % ≠ 36.57 %; ICl (M = 126.90 + 35.45 = 162.35 g mol⁻¹, not 206.79) would give w(I) = 253.80 / (280.44 + 2 × 162.35) = 253.80 / 605.14 = 41.9 % ≠ 36.57 %.

### 5.5 Phase of fully protonated PL1

No structural detail of PL1 is needed. With both phosphate groups protonated, the headgroup is neutral: electrostatic headgroup repulsion and hydration vanish, so the effective headgroup area a₀ collapses, while the four C₁₈ tails keep their full volume v and length l_c. The packing parameter

P = v / (a₀ · l_c)

rises above 1: the molecule becomes an inverted cone and the aggregate favours negative interfacial curvature — headgroup-lined aqueous cylinders embedded in a continuous hydrocarbon matrix.

Answer: **(d) inverse hexagonal** (the H_II phase known for neutral/protonated or Ca²⁺-complexed cardiolipin). Conversely at physiological pH the dianion has a large, charged, hydrated headgroup, P ≈ 1, giving the stated lamellar phase.

### 5.6 Hydrolysis balances; structures of PL2 and PL3

Reference hydrolysis (PL1, from the assembly above):

C₈₁H₁₄₂O₁₇P₂ + 8 H₂O → 4 C₁₈H₃₂O₂ + 2 H₃PO₄ + 3 C₃H₈O₃
(H: 158 = 128 + 6 + 24 ✓; O: 25 = 8 + 8 + 9 ✓; C 81/81 ✓; P 2/2 ✓.)

**Z and PL2.** PL2 hydrolyses with the same 8 H₂O to the same 4 RCOOH and 2 H₃PO₄, but gives 2 glycerol + Z instead of 3 glycerol: the central glycerol bridge of PL1 is replaced by Z, which has one fewer backbone unit. Two independent determinations of Z's formula:

(i) Atom balance on PL2 hydrolysis (PL2 = C₈₁H₁₄₂O₁₆P₂, one O fewer than PL1): Z = (C₈₁H₁₄₂O₁₆P₂ + 8 H₂O) − (4 C₁₈H₃₂O₂ + 2 H₃PO₄ + 2 C₃H₈O₃): C: 81 − 72 − 6 = 3; H: 158 − 128 − 6 − 16 = 8; O: 24 − 8 − 8 − 6 = 2 ⟹ Z = C₃H₈O₂.

(ii) Bond-order counting: glycerol has B = (3·4 + 8·1 + 3·2)/2 = 13; PL2 has exactly one σ+π bond fewer than PL1 (254 vs 255), so B(Z) = 12. Hydrolysis must regenerate the two OH groups with which Z was esterified to the two phosphates, so Z is a diol; Z contains only C, H, O and is not hydrogenated by H₂/catalyst, hence no C=C, C=O or cleavable ring: Z = C_qH_(2q+2)O₂ (saturated acyclic diol). Then B(Z) = [4q + (2q + 2) + 4]/2 = 3q + 3 = 12 ⟹ q = 3.

Balance check for PL2 hydrolysis: C₈₁H₁₄₂O₁₆P₂ + 8 H₂O → 4 C₁₈H₃₂O₂ + 2 H₃PO₄ + 2 C₃H₈O₃ + C₃H₈O₂ (C 81/81 ✓; H 158/158 ✓; O 24/24 ✓; P 2/2 ✓).

Of the C₃H₈O₂ diols, only **propane-1,3-diol** has equivalent ends; inserting –CH₂–CH₂–CH₂– as the bridge keeps the two phosphate groups equivalent and makes the (R,R) molecule C₂-symmetric — chiral and more symmetrical than PL1, as required (propane-1,2-diol would differentiate the two phosphates). PL2:

```
RC(=O)O–CH2–C*_L(H)(O–C(=O)R)–CH2–O–P(=O)(OH)–O–CH2–CH2–CH2–O–P(=O)(OH)–O–CH2–C*_R(H)(O–C(=O)R)–CH2–O–C(=O)R
```

(R,R)/(S,S) is the chiral pair; (R,S) has a mirror plane and is achiral. Bond check: atoms = 81 + 142 + 16 + 2 = 241 → 240 σ; π = 14; 240 + 14 = **254 ✓** (equivalently B = (4·81 + 142 + 2·16 + 5·2)/2 = 254 ✓).

**PL3.** Its balanced hydrolysis gives 2 RCOOH + H₃PO₄ + glycerol + **ethanol**, so the phosphate is esterified by one glycerol residue and one ethyl group (a phosphatidyl-propane-1,3-diol or any other diol diester would release that diol on hydrolysis, never ethanol). Four ester bonds are cleaved (2 fatty-acid esters + 2 phosphate esters), so the water coefficient is 4:

C₄₁H₇₃O₈P + 4 H₂O → 2 C₁₈H₃₂O₂ + H₃PO₄ + C₃H₈O₃ + C₂H₅OH
balance: C 41/41 ✓; H 73 + 8 = 81 = 64 + 3 + 8 + 6 ✓; O 8 + 4 = 12 = 4 + 4 + 3 + 1 ✓; P 1/1 ✓ (m = 4).

Neutral formula from the hydrolysis products: 2 C₁₈H₃₂O₂ + H₃PO₄ + C₃H₈O₃ + C₂H₅OH − 4 H₂O = **C₄₁H₇₃O₈P** (C: 36 + 3 + 2 = 41; H: 64 + 3 + 8 + 6 − 8 = 73; O: 4 + 4 + 3 + 1 − 4 = 8 ✓). The two fatty acids must occupy adjacent glycerol positions, with the phosphate on the remaining terminal oxygen — placing the phosphate at C2 with identical acyls at C1 and C3 would make C2 non-stereogenic and the molecule achiral. Structure (drawn as the physiological monoanion):

```
RC(=O)O–CH2–C*(H)(O–C(=O)R)–CH2–O–P(=O)(O⁻)–O–CH2CH3
```

C* bears H, –O–C(=O)R, –CH₂–O–C(=O)R and –CH₂–O–P(···): exactly one stereocentre, giving the stated (R)/(S) pair of enantiomers ✓. The remaining phosphate-diester OH (pKₐ ≈ 2) is deprotonated at pH ≈ 7.4, giving C₄₁H₇₂O₈P⁻ — so PL3 is **charged** at physiological pH ✓.
