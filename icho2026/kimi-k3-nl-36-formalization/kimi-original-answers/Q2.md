## Detailed solution

Given / used throughout: [BrO₃⁻] = 0.06 M, [MA] = 0.1 M, [H⁺] = 0.8 M, [Ce⁴⁺]₀ = 0.001 M (all constant except Ce), and
k₁ = 1.0 × 10⁴ M⁻² s⁻¹, k₂ = 6.2 × 10⁴ M⁻² s⁻¹, k₃ = 4.0 × 10⁷ M⁻¹ s⁻¹, k₄ = 2.0 × 10⁹ M⁻² s⁻¹, k₅ = 2.1 M⁻³ s⁻¹, k₆ = 8.2 M⁻¹ s⁻¹, k₇ = 1.0 × 10² M⁻¹ s⁻¹.

### 2.1 Overall balanced equation of the BZ reaction (2.0 pt)

Half-reactions. In malonic acid CH₂(COOH)₂ the two carboxyl carbons are +3 each and the central carbon is −2 (sum = +4); in CO₂ each carbon is +4 (sum = +12), so oxidation of one MA releases 8 e⁻:

- Oxidation: CH₂(COOH)₂ + 2 H₂O → 3 CO₂ + 8 H⁺ + 8 e⁻ (C: 3 = 3; H: 8 = 8; O: 6 = 6 ✓)
- Reduction (Br: +5 → −1, 6 e⁻): BrO₃⁻ + 6 H⁺ + 6 e⁻ → Br⁻ + 3 H₂O ✓

LCM(8, 6) = 24: multiply oxidation by 3 and reduction by 4, add, and cancel 24 H⁺ and 6 H₂O:

4 BrO₃⁻ + 3 CH₂(COOH)₂ → 4 Br⁻ + 9 CO₂ + 6 H₂O

Check: Br 4 = 4; C 9 = 9; H 12 = 12; O 24 = 24; charge −4 = −4. Ce³⁺/Ce⁴⁺ interconvert in the Ce cycle and cancel (catalyst), so they do not appear.

### 2.2 Stationary concentrations [HBrO₂]A and [HBrO₂]B (11.0 pt)

**Process A (steps 1–3).** Steady state for the BrO₂• radical: it is formed in step (1) (2 per event) and consumed in step (2), 2r₁ = r₂. Then

d[HBrO₂]/d𝑡 = −r₁ + r₂ − 2r₃ = −r₁ + 2r₁ − 2r₃ = r₁ − 2r₃ = 0

(net autocatalytic growth of HBrO₂ via (1) + 2×(2) balanced by disproportionation (3)):

k₁[BrO₃⁻][H⁺][HBrO₂]A = 2k₃[HBrO₂]A²
[HBrO₂]A = k₁[BrO₃⁻][H⁺] / (2k₃) = (1.0 × 10⁴ M⁻² s⁻¹ × 0.06 M × 0.8 M) / (2 × 4.0 × 10⁷ M⁻¹ s⁻¹)

[HBrO₂]A = 480 s⁻¹ / (8.0 × 10⁷ M⁻¹ s⁻¹) = **6.0 × 10⁻⁶ M** (consistent with the fallback value 1 × 10⁻⁵ M). Note k₂ and the Ce concentrations drop out; step (2) only needs to be fast enough to keep BrO₂• at steady state. Units check: s⁻¹ / (M⁻¹ s⁻¹) = M ✓.

**Process B (steps 4–6).** HBrO₂ is formed only in step (5) and consumed only in step (4) (step (6) involves HBrO, not HBrO₂):

d[HBrO₂]/d𝑡 = r₅ − r₄ = k₅[BrO₃⁻][Br⁻][H⁺]² − k₄[HBrO₂][Br⁻][H⁺] = 0
[HBrO₂]B = k₅[BrO₃⁻][H⁺] / k₄ = (2.1 M⁻³ s⁻¹ × 0.06 M × 0.8 M) / (2.0 × 10⁹ M⁻² s⁻¹)

[HBrO₂]B = 0.1008 M⁻¹ s⁻¹ / (2.0 × 10⁹ M⁻² s⁻¹) = 5.04 × 10⁻¹¹ M ≈ **5.0 × 10⁻¹¹ M** (consistent with the fallback 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ M; [Br⁻] cancels). Units check: M⁻¹ s⁻¹ / (M⁻² s⁻¹) = M ✓.

### 2.3 Critical bromide concentration [Br⁻]critical (4.0 pt)

The switch A ↔ B occurs when the rate of step (4) equals the rate of step (1):

r₄ = r₁ ⇒ k₄[HBrO₂][Br⁻]critical[H⁺] = k₁[HBrO₂][BrO₃⁻][H⁺]

[HBrO₂] and [H⁺] cancel:

[Br⁻]critical = k₁[BrO₃⁻] / k₄ = (1.0 × 10⁴ M⁻² s⁻¹ × 0.06 M) / (2.0 × 10⁹ M⁻² s⁻¹) = **3.0 × 10⁻⁷ M**

Interpretation: [Br⁻] > 3.0 × 10⁻⁷ M ⇒ r₄ > r₁ ⇒ Process B (HBrO₂ destroyed); [Br⁻] < 3.0 × 10⁻⁷ M ⇒ r₁ > r₄ ⇒ Process A (HBrO₂ autocatalysis). Consistency check with 2.2: k₁[HBrO₂]B/(k₅[H⁺]) = (1.0 × 10⁴ × 5.04 × 10⁻¹¹)/(2.1 × 0.8) = 3.0 × 10⁻⁷ M ✓.

### 2.4 Direction of motion in the [HBrO₂] vs [Br⁻] phase portrait (2.0 pt)

Trace one period (x-axis [Br⁻], y-axis [HBrO₂]):

1. **Process B, slow leg:** [Br⁻] > [Br⁻]critical, solution colourless. Steps (4) and (5) consume Br⁻, so [Br⁻] slowly falls from [Br⁻]max = 7.0 × 10⁻⁴ M to [Br⁻]critical = 3.0 × 10⁻⁷ M while [HBrO₂] stays low at [HBrO₂]B ≈ 5 × 10⁻¹¹ M — motion along the bottom edge, right → left.
2. **Switch to A:** at [Br⁻]critical, step (1) wins; autocatalysis raises [HBrO₂] to [HBrO₂]A ≈ 6 × 10⁻⁶ M and Ce³⁺ → Ce⁴⁺ (yellow) — fast motion up the left edge.
3. **Fast Br⁻ regeneration:** with Ce⁴⁺ abundant, Process C (Ce⁴⁺ + BMA → Ce³⁺ + Br⁻ + …) rapidly restores [Br⁻] from 3.0 × 10⁻⁷ M back to [Br⁻]max = 7.0 × 10⁻⁴ M while [HBrO₂] is still high — fast motion along the top edge, left → right.
4. **Switch back to B:** at high [Br⁻], step (4) again outruns step (1); [HBrO₂] crashes back to [HBrO₂]B — fast motion down the right edge.

Bottom leftward → left side upward → top rightward → right side downward = **clockwise**. Tick the box showing clockwise rotation.

### 2.5 Period of oscillations τ (9.0 pt)

The slow, rate-determining part is the decay of [Br⁻] during Process B; the return to [Br⁻]max is stated to occur "almost immediately" and contributes negligibly, so τ ≈ 𝑡(Process B).

During Process B, Br⁻ is consumed in steps (4) and (5):

−d[Br⁻]/d𝑡 = r₄ + r₅

At the stationary state found in 2.2, r₄ = r₅, so

−d[Br⁻]/d𝑡 = 2r₅ = 2k₅[BrO₃⁻][H⁺]²[Br⁻] = k′[Br⁻]

with the pseudo-first-order coefficient (constant [BrO₃⁻], [H⁺]):

k′ = 2 × 2.1 M⁻³ s⁻¹ × 0.06 M × (0.8 M)² = 2 × 2.1 × 0.06 × 0.64 = 0.1613 s⁻¹

(Same result from Process B stoichiometry: (4) + (5) consumes 2 Br⁻ per event, and r₄ = r₅.)

Exponential decay: [Br⁻](t) = [Br⁻]max e^(−k′t). The time to fall from [Br⁻]max = 7.0 × 10⁻⁴ M to [Br⁻]critical = 3.0 × 10⁻⁷ M:

τ = (1/k′) ln([Br⁻]max / [Br⁻]critical) = (1 / 0.1613 s⁻¹) × ln(7.0 × 10⁻⁴ / 3.0 × 10⁻⁷)
  = (1 / 0.1613 s⁻¹) × ln(2.333 × 10³) = 7.755 / 0.1613 s⁻¹

**τ ≈ 48 s** (48.1 s unrounded; ≈ 0.8 min). (If the fallback [Br⁻]critical = 1 × 10⁻⁷ M is used instead, τ = ln(7.0 × 10³)/0.1613 ≈ 55 s.)

### 2.6 Effect of perturbations on the open BZ system (4.0 pt)

- **1. Small amount of Ce⁴⁺ during Process A → (c) Process A switches to Process B.** Process C (Ce⁴⁺ + BMA → Ce³⁺ + Br⁻ + products) runs continuously; extra Ce⁴⁺ speeds up Br⁻ generation, pushing [Br⁻] back above [Br⁻]critical, where step (4) beats step (1) and the system flips to Process B.
- **2. Small amount of Ag⁺ during Process B → (d) Process B switches to Process A.** Ag⁺ precipitates bromide (Ag⁺ + Br⁻ → AgBr↓), driving [Br⁻] below [Br⁻]critical; step (1) then outruns step (4), HBrO₂ autocatalysis ignites and Process A begins.
- **3. Small amount of Br⁻ during Process B → (b) prolongs Process B.** Adding Br⁻ moves [Br⁻] up again, away from [Br⁻]critical; step (4) remains faster than step (1) for longer, so a longer time is needed before the switch to Process A.
- **4. Continuous addition of Br⁻ → (e) oscillations stop.** [Br⁻] is then held permanently above [Br⁻]critical, step (4) always exceeds step (1), Process B persists indefinitely and the A/B alternation (and hence the oscillation) ceases.

### 2.7 Graph of d𝐺/d𝑡 for a closed oscillating system (3.0 pt)

For a closed system at constant 𝑇 and 𝑝 the second law requires the Gibbs energy to decrease monotonically toward equilibrium: 𝐺(t) can never rise, so d𝐺/d𝑡 ≤ 0 at every instant — the concentration oscillations are swings between kinetic regimes (Processes A and B), not violations of thermodynamics. The instantaneous dissipation rate varies with the phase of the cycle (largest during the fast switch-over steps, smallest during the slow drift), so |d𝐺/d𝑡| shows periodic modulation, and as the reactants of the closed system are consumed the system approaches equilibrium and d𝐺/d𝑡 → 0.

Therefore tick the graph in which d𝐺/d𝑡 lies entirely below (or on) the time axis — negative throughout, with periodic structure tracking the oscillation, decaying toward zero — and never crosses into positive values. Any graph showing d𝐺/d𝑡 oscillating through zero or becoming positive is thermodynamically impossible and must not be ticked.
