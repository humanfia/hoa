## Detailed solution

### 8.1 Half equation (2 pts)

Carbon goes from +4 in CO₂ to +2 in CO — a two-electron reduction. Balance O with H₂O and H with H⁺:

CO₂ + 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻ → CO + H₂O

Check: C 1 = 1; O 2 = 2; H 2 = 2; charge LHS = 2(+1) + 2(−1) = 0 = RHS ✓.

### 8.2 Structures 3–7 (16 pts)

The scheme is the standard two-photon oxidative quenching cascade of triethanolamine (TEOA) in water:

1. 2 → 3: one-electron transfer from the amine lone pair to the photogenerated hole gives the radical cation **3 = [N(CH₂CH₂OH)₃]•⁺** (ionic, short-lived ✓).
2. 3 → 4: deprotonation of an α-C–H (α to nitrogen) gives the neutral α-aminoalkyl radical **4 = (HOCH₂CH₂)₂N–C(•)H–CH₂OH** (radical ✓).
3. 4 → 5: a second one-electron oxidation gives the iminium ion **5 = (HOCH₂CH₂)₂N⁺=CH–CH₂OH** (ionic ✓; the minimal Lewis structure has an N=C double bond).
4. 5 + H₂O → 6 + 7: water adds to the iminium carbon; proton transfer and C–N cleavage release **6 = diethanolamine, HN(CH₂CH₂OH)₂**, and **7 = glycolaldehyde, HOCH₂CHO**.

Step-4 balance: C₆H₁₄NO₃⁺ + H₂O → C₄H₁₁NO₂ + C₂H₄O₂ + H⁺ — C 6 = 6, H 16 = 16, N 1 = 1, O 4 = 4, charge +1 = +1 ✓.

Adding the two redox steps gives the overall oxidation half-reaction: N(CH₂CH₂OH)₃ + H₂O → HN(CH₂CH₂OH)₂ + HOCH₂CHO + 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻ — C₆H₁₇NO₄ and zero net charge on each side ✓. Thus the two-electron oxidation of one TEOA exactly supplies the two electrons of the 8.1 half-reaction.

Tollens check for 7: RCHO + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 3 OH⁻ → RCOO⁻ + 2 Ag↓ + 4 NH₃ + 2 H₂O — an aldehyde, so it gives the silver mirror ✓, matching the printed hint.

### 8.3 Geometry of 1 (2 pts)

Ligand 8 is tetradentate: its four donors lie in one plane (the printed cartoon). Two further ligands are needed to complete the Fe coordination sphere in the Fe-salt + 8 assembly, and they must sit on opposite sides of that plane — trans axial positions. Fe is therefore six-coordinate, and the geometry to tick is **octahedral** (chelate-distorted); tetrahedral and square-planar are excluded because they account for only four donors.

### 8.4 Structures and bookkeeping for 9–15 (29 pts)

Counting convention (ionic): Fe is group 8, so dⁿ with n = 8 − OS; neutral N₄ ligand 8 donates 8 e⁻; H₂O and CO are neutral 2-electron (L-type) donors; the C-bound fragments CO₂•⁻ and –C(=O)OH are anionic X-type 2-electron donors (charge −1 each); VE is the total valence-electron count of the complex at Fe — 18/17/16-type bookkeeping, not the bare d-electron count. Two bookkeeping features printed in the scheme fix the structures unambiguously: CO₂ enters **with loss of the last axial aqua ligand**, so 11 (and 12, which follows it without a ligand step) carry **no axial solvent** and are five-coordinate; and the electron added at 11→12 is consumed ligand-side (CO₂•⁻ → closed-shell COOH⁻), so the iron formal oxidation state stays **+2 in 12** rather than rising to Fe(III).

- **9**: resting state [FeᴵᴵL(OH₂)₂]²⁺; VE = 6 (d⁶) + 8 (L) + 2×2 = 18; CN = 4+2 = 6; Z = +2.
- **9 → 10 (+e⁻, −H₂O)**: a conduction-band electron from photoexcited C₃N₄ reduces Fe(II) to Fe(I) and one axial water dissociates: **10 = [FeᴵL(OH₂)]⁺**; VE = 7 + 8 + 2 = 17; CN 5; Z = +2 − 1 = +1 ✓.
- **10 → 11 (+CO₂, −H₂O)**: CO₂ substitutes the remaining axial water. The Fe(I) electron is transferred onto the bound substrate, so the resonance form used for the OS assignment is C-bound, bent CO₂•⁻ with Fe(II): **11 = [FeᴵᴵL–CO₂•⁻]⁺** (radical ligand-centred). Five-coordinate, no axial ligand; VE = 6 + 8 + 2 = 16; Z = +2 − 1 = +1.
- **11 → 12 (+e⁻, +H⁺)**: the second electron and the first proton arrive at this step (PCET), converting the radical anion into the closed-shell, C-bound hydroxycarbonyl ligand: **12 = [FeᴵᴵL–C(=O)OH]⁺**. Fe stays +2 (the added electron has gone to the ligand, which is confirmed by the charge: +1 − 1(e⁻) + 1(H⁺) = +1; ionic check +2 − 1(COOH⁻) = +1 ✓); VE = 6 + 8 + 2 = 16; CN 5.
- **12 → 13 (+H₂O)**: solvent re-coordinates axially: **13 = [FeᴵᴵL(OH₂)–C(=O)OH]⁺**; VE = 6 + 8 + 2 + 2 = 18; CN 6; Z +1.
- **13 → 14 (+H⁺, −H₂O)**: second protonation at the hydroxycarbonyl OH and C–O cleavage expel the distal oxygen as water while the co-bound aqua ligand stays: **14 = [FeᴵᴵL(CO)(OH₂)]²⁺**; VE = 6 + 8 + 2 + 2 = 18; CN 6; Z = +1 + 1 = +2 ✓.
- **14 → 15 (−CO)**: product CO dissociates: **15 = [FeᴵᴵL(OH₂)]²⁺**; VE = 6 + 8 + 2 = 16; CN 5; Z +2.
- **15 → 9 (+H₂O)**: a second water binds to regenerate the six-coordinate resting complex (15 is therefore a distinct intermediate, not 9 itself).

Overall inventory: 2 e⁻ enter (9→10, 11→12); 2 H⁺ enter (11→12, 13→14); CO₂ enters (10→11); CO leaves (14→15); the five H₂O steps — three releases (9→10, 10→11, 13→14) and two uptakes (12→13, 15→9) — sum to one H₂O released. Net: CO₂ + 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻ → CO + H₂O, identical to 8.1 ✓.

### 8.5 Catalyst density N_cat (5 pts)

Basis: 1.000 g of loaded composite, ω_cat = 0.038 → m_cat = 0.0380 g, m(C₃N₄) = 0.962 g.

Catalyst molecules on this basis:
N(1) = (0.0380 g / 557.21 g mol⁻¹) × 6.022×10²³ mol⁻¹
     = 6.820×10⁻⁵ mol × 6.022×10²³ mol⁻¹ = 4.107×10¹⁹.

Support area on this basis (the specific surface area is quoted per gram of C₃N₄):
A = 0.962 g × 17.8 m² g⁻¹ = 17.12 m² = 17.12×10¹⁸ nm² = 1.712×10¹⁹ nm²
(1 m² = 10¹⁸ nm²).

N_cat = 4.107×10¹⁹ / 1.712×10¹⁹ nm² = 2.40 → **N_cat ≈ 2.4 molecules nm⁻²**.

(If the full 1 g of composite is approximated as support, A = 17.8 m² and N_cat = 2.31 ≈ 2.3 nm⁻²; the difference is only the 3.8 % catalyst-mass correction. Either value implies a mean site spacing of roughly 1/√2.4 ≈ 0.6 nm, consistent with isolated molecular sites.)

### 8.6 Quantum yield for CO (10 pts)

Catalyst in 10.0 mg of loaded material:
n_cat = 0.0100 g × 0.038 / 557.21 g mol⁻¹ = 6.820×10⁻⁷ mol
N_cat = 6.820×10⁻⁷ mol × 6.022×10²³ mol⁻¹ = 4.107×10¹⁷ molecules.

CO formed in one hour at TOF = 8 h⁻¹: N(CO) = 8 × 4.107×10¹⁷ = 3.285×10¹⁸.
Reacted electrons (2 e⁻ per CO, from 8.1): N(e⁻) = 2 × 3.285×10¹⁸ = 6.571×10¹⁸ per hour.

Photon energy at 390 nm:
E_ph = hc/λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s × 2.998×10⁸ m s⁻¹) / (3.90×10⁻⁷ m) = 5.094×10⁻¹⁹ J.

Incident photons in one hour: E = 0.050 J s⁻¹ × 3600 s = 180 J →
N_ph = 180 J / 5.094×10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.534×10²⁰.

φ = (N(e⁻)/N_ph) × 100 % = (6.571×10¹⁸ / 3.534×10²⁰) × 100 % = 1.86 % ≈ **1.9 %**
(TOF given to ~1–2 sig figs, so 2-significant-figure answer).

### 8.7 Rate per gram vs ω_cat (2 pts)

Per gram of loaded material, catalyst molecules ∝ ω_cat, so the overall CO rate satisfies
rate ∝ ω_cat × TOF.

The printed TOF diagram shows TOF falling several-fold across the range (read-offs ≈ 60, 55, 37, 29, 15, 11 h⁻¹ at ω_cat ≈ 0.1, 0.3, 0.6, 1.0, 2.0, 2.9 %), while ω_cat itself grows 29-fold. The products ω_cat × TOF ≈ 6, 17, 22, 29, 30, 32 (common arbitrary units) increase monotonically over 0.1 ≤ ω_cat ≤ 2.9 (normalising per gram of support instead would only add the increasing factor 1/(1−ω_cat)). Hence the CO evolution rate per gram **increases → tick (a)**.

### 8.8 Assigning a–d to N/R/G/B (4 pts)

- The printed bar chart contains no H₂ and no CO bars at all in panel **a** — the only all-zero entry. With no irradiation there is no photochemistry, so the unique zero panel is the dark control: **a = N**.
- The remaining panels b, c, d show progressively less H₂: χ(H₂) decreases b → c → d (with χ(CO) rising correspondingly, since χ(H₂) + χ(CO) = 100 % of the gaseous products).
- LED photon energies from E = hc/λ: E(B) > E(G) > E(R).
- The ΔG diagram on p.3 shows reduction of additional protons to H₂ is the more energy-demanding route, so it is progressively shut down as photon energy drops, while CO₂→CO stays accessible.

Therefore the H₂ fraction tracks photon energy: most with blue, least with red. With χ(H₂) decreasing b→c→d this gives **b = B, c = G, d = R**, i.e. **a = N, b = B, c = G, d = R**.

### 8.9 Quenching percentages of S₁ and T₁ (12 pts)

With k_ISC and k_RISC negligible, each excited state independently competes between intrinsic decay (kept rate 1/τ₀) and pseudo-first-order reductive quenching (rate k_q[Red]). The quenched fraction is:

η_q = k_q[Red] / (1/τ₀ + k_q[Red]) = k_qτ₀[Red] / (1 + k_qτ₀[Red]).

**S₁:** k_Sτ₀(S₁)[Red] = (2.7×10⁹ M⁻¹ s⁻¹)(2.9×10⁻⁹ s)(0.1 M) = 7.83 × 0.1 = 0.783.
η_q(S₁) = 0.783 / 1.783 = 0.439 = **43.9 % (≈ 44 %)**.

**T₁:** τ₀(T₁) = 84 µs = 84×10⁻⁶ s; k_Tτ₀(T₁)[Red] = (1.5×10⁸ M⁻¹ s⁻¹)(84×10⁻⁶ s)(0.1 M) = 1.26×10⁴ × 0.1 = 1260.
η_q(T₁) = 1260 / 1261 = 0.9992 = **99.9 % (≈ 100 %)**.

Consistency check: both fractions lie between 0 and 1; the long-lived 84 µs triplet is quenched almost quantitatively, whereas the 2.9 ns singlet escapes quenching ~56 % of the time.

### 8.10 Effect of [Red] on the emission lifetimes (2 pts)

τ([Red]) = 1 / (1/τ₀ + k_q[Red]) = τ₀ / (1 + k_qτ₀[Red]).

Since k_q > 0, increasing [Red] increases the denominator and therefore **decreases** the emission lifetimes of both S₁ and T₁ → tick **(b)**. Numerically, at [Red] = 0.1 M (the 8.9 conditions): τ(S₁) = 2.9 ns/1.783 ≈ 1.63 ns and τ(T₁) = 84 µs/1261 ≈ 66.6 ns, both well below their unquenched τ₀ values.
