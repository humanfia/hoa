## Detailed solution

### Part 1. The blue elixir

**1.1 Identify X and Y.**

Four entries of the table share the formula C10H18O: compounds 2, 3, 6 and 10. The structure drawn for compound 3 — repeated under Zingiber, Chamomilla and Artemisia — is the bicyclic ether 1,8-cineole (eucalyptol), C10H18O. Its skeleton (two bridgehead carbons joined by two –CH2–CH2– bridges and one –O–C(CH3)2– bridge) has a mirror plane containing both bridgeheads, the ether oxygen and the gem-dimethyl carbon (bisecting its two methyl groups) and reflecting the two ethylene bridges into each other; both bridgeheads bear two identical substituent arms, so the molecule is achiral. Hence **Y = 3, 1,8-cineole**.

X must then be one of the remaining drawn C10H18O entries, 2, 6 or 10. Only compound 6 is drawn with the acyclic linalyl skeleton — a tertiary allylic alcohol CH2=CH–C(OH)(CH3)– head joined by a –CH2–CH2– tether to a second double bond CH=C(CH3)2 — i.e. linalool (3,7-dimethylocta-1,6-dien-3-ol). It is chiral: C-3 carries four different groups (OH, CH3, vinyl, 4-methylpent-3-en-1-yl), so X has no plane of symmetry. In acid it undergoes cationic cycloisomerization into Y:

(i) protonation and ionization of the allylic 3-OH gives the resonance-stabilized linalyl cation (+CH2–CH=C(CH3)– ↔ CH2=CH–C+(CH3)–);
(ii) the distal C6=C7 double bond attacks the allylic terminus C1 (6-exo-trig closure), forming the C1–C6 bond and hence a cyclohexane ring; the charge lands on C7, the tertiary gem-dimethyl carbon — the α-terpinyl (p-menthane) cation;
(iii) capture by water regenerates a tertiary alcohol, α-terpineol, merely an intermediate of the cascade (it is not one of the table entries);
(iv) protonation of the remaining ring double bond places the charge on the tertiary ring carbon bearing the methyl group;
(v) the alcohol oxygen closes onto this cation, forming the six-membered 1,8-epoxy bridge;
(vi) deprotonation gives achiral 1,8-cineole and regenerates H+.

Overall: C10H18O --H+ (catalytic)--> C10H18O — no atoms gained or lost, a genuine isomerization X (6) → Y (3), exactly as stated.

**1.2 Identify Z; structures of A and B.**

The blue colour produced on distilling camomile is characteristic of azulene-type sesquiterpenes.
- A = azulene, C10H8: two fused rings, a five-membered and a seven-membered ring, with a delocalized 10-π-electron periphery; planar, C2v (structure: `c1ccc2cccc-2cc1` fused 5–7 system). Its mirror planes are (a) the molecular plane and (b) the plane perpendicular to it which bisects the fused framework through the C2 axis — precisely "two perpendicular planes of symmetry".
- On strong heating azulene rearranges into its more stable aromatic isomer B = naphthalene, C10H8, two fused benzene rings (D2h): the molecular plane plus the two planes perpendicular to it along the long and short axes are "three mutually perpendicular planes of symmetry".
- Z is a derivative of azulene and gives the MS isotope pair at m/z 218/220 in a 3:1 ratio on chlorination. That ratio is the signature of exactly one Cl atom (35Cl : 37Cl = 75.8 : 24.2 ≈ 3 : 1), and monochlorination replaces one H by Cl:

M(Z) = 218 − 35 + 1 = 184 g/mol; nominal mass of C14H16 = 14(12) + 16(1) = 184 → C14H15Cl gives 218 (^35Cl) and 220 (^37Cl). ✓

The only C14H16 entry of the table is compound 7: **Z = chamazulene**, the azulene skeleton bearing CH3 at positions 1 and 4 and CH2CH3 at position 7 (C10H5 core + 2 CH3 + C2H5 = C14H16 ✓).

**1.3 Carbon count and identity of W.**

With only 12C (98.9%) and 13C (1.1%), and all other elements monoisotopic, the M peak requires all n carbons to be 12C, while M+1 requires exactly one 13C anywhere in the molecule:

I(M) ∝ (0.989)^n, I(M + 1) ∝ n(0.011)(0.989)^(n−1)

I(M + 1)/I(M) = n·(0.011/0.989) = 0.01112·n

The measured ratio is 1/9 = 0.1111, so:

n = (1/9)·(0.989/0.011) = 0.989/0.099 = 9.99 ≈ 10 (n must be an integer)

So W has **10 carbons**. The colour change with aqueous Fe3+ is the classical phenol test (violet Fe3+–phenolate complex). Among the ten-carbon entries (2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10) only compound 5 (C10H12O2) is drawn as a phenol; compound 1 (zingerone, C11H14O3) is also phenolic but is excluded by n = 10. **W = 5, eugenol**: a benzene ring with –OH at C1, –OCH3 ortho (C2) and –CH2–CH=CH2 para (C4), SMILES `C=CCc1ccc(O)c(OC)c1`, M = 10(12.011) + 12(1.008) + 2(15.999) = 164.2 g/mol. Consistency check: predicted I(M+1)/I(M) = 10 × 0.01112 = 0.1112, i.e. 9.0 : 1 ✓.

### Part 2. The mysterious stone

**1.4 Identify Q, C·xH2O and D.**

D is built from Na, Q and F (from the NaF treatment). Convert the mass fractions to moles per 100 g of D:

n(Na) = 32.85/22.99 = 1.429 mol; n(Q) tentatively Al: 12.85/26.98 = 0.4763 mol; n(F) = (100 − 32.85 − 12.85)/19.00 = 54.30/19.00 = 2.858 mol

Ratio 1.429 : 0.4763 : 2.858 = 3.00 : 1.00 : 6.00 → **D = Na3AlF6** (cryolite, Na3[AlF6]).

Verification: M = 3(22.99) + 26.98 + 6(19.00) = 209.95 g/mol; w(Na) = 68.97/209.95 = 32.85% ✓; w(Al) = 26.98/209.95 = 12.85% ✓. Cryolite is precisely the melt used industrially (Hall–Héroult) to produce aluminium by electrolysis, so **Q = Al**.

Anhydrous C with excess NaF gives cryolite: AlF3 + 3 NaF → Na3[AlF6], so C = AlF3 (M = 26.98 + 3(19.00) = 83.98 g/mol). For the hydrate with 39.16% H2O by mass:

0.3916 = 18.015·x/(83.98 + 18.015·x) → x = (0.3916 × 83.98)/(0.6084 × 18.015) = 32.887/10.960 = 3.00

**C·3H2O = AlF3·3H2O** (M = 138.02 g/mol; check: 3(18.015)/138.02 = 39.16% H2O ✓), precipitated as Al3+ + 3 F− + 3 H2O → AlF3·3H2O↓ at pH ≈ 4.

**1.5 Structures of E, F and G.**

Let E = CxHy. Per 100 g: n(C) = 88.82/12.011 = 7.395 mol; n(H) = 11.18/1.008 = 11.09 mol → C : H = 1 : 1.50, i.e. empirical unit C2H3. A six-fold symmetry axis demands 6-fold repetition: E = C12H18 with a benzene core bearing six methyl groups — **E = hexamethylbenzene, C6(CH3)6** (SMILES `Cc1c(C)c(C)c(C)c(C)c1C`), planar, D6h, C6 axis ⊥ ring. Check: w(H) = 18(1.008)/[12(12.011) + 18(1.008)] = 18.144/162.276 = 11.18% ✓.

Every benzylic CH3 of E is oxidized to COOH by hot acidic KMnO4 without changing the substitution pattern, so **F = mellitic acid, C6(COOH)6 = C12H6O12** — a hexabasic, highly symmetrical acid with a C6 axis. Balanced oxidation:

5 C12H18 + 36 KMnO4 + 54 H2SO4 → 5 C12H6O12 + 18 K2SO4 + 36 MnSO4 + 84 H2O

(each CH3 → COOH releases 6 e−, 36 e− per E; 5 × 36 = 180 = 36 Mn(VII) × 5 e−; all atoms and charge balance ✓)

P2O5 is a dehydrating agent and G is binary (only C and O): remove 3 H2O from F:

C12H6O12 → C12O9 + 3 H2O (the water is absorbed by P2O5; atoms balance ✓)

**G = mellitic anhydride, C12O9**: the benzene core carries the six carboxyl carbons as three –C(=O)–O–C(=O)– anhydride bridges across alternating ring edges (carbon pairs 1,2; 3,4; 5,6); the alternating arrangement leaves a single three-fold axis perpendicular to the ring. Check: w(O) = 9(15.999)/[12(12.011) + 9(15.999)] = 143.99/288.12 = 49.98% ✓.

**1.6 Formulae of the stone and H from thermogravimetry.**

The stone is the salt of metal Q (Al3+, 1.4) with the anion of acid F (mellitate, C12O12^6−, 1.5): charge balance gives Al2(C12O12)·xH2O, with

M(Al2C12O12) = 2(26.98) + 12(12.011) + 12(15.999) = 390.08 g/mol.

First step (≈100–200 °C, loss of crystal water), 10.00 g → 5.75 g:

n(salt) = 5.75/390.08 = 1.474 × 10−2 mol
n(H2O) = (10.00 − 5.75)/18.015 = 4.25/18.015 = 0.2359 mol
x = 0.2359/0.01474 = 16.0 → **stone = Al2(C12O12)·16H2O**, M = 390.08 + 16(18.015) = 678.32 g/mol

Checks (4 s.f.): calculated water mass = 10.00 × (16 × 18.015)/678.32 = 4.249 g vs observed 4.25 g; calculated plateau = 10.00 × 390.08/678.32 = 5.751 g vs observed 5.75 g ✓.

Al2(C12O12)·16H2O → Al2(C12O12) + 16 H2O (≈100–200 °C)

Second step (400 °C, in open air the organic anion is burnt away and only aluminium remains in the residue):

M(H per mol of stone) = 1.50 g/0.014742 mol = 101.8 g/mol ≈ M(Al2O3) = 101.96 g/mol → **H = Al2O3** (predicted residue 0.014742 mol × 101.96 g/mol = 1.503 g vs observed 1.50 g ✓)

2 Al2(C12O12) + 15 O2 → 2 Al2O3 + 24 CO2 (≥400 °C; O balance: 24 + 30 = 6 + 48 = 54 ✓)

The stone is therefore aluminium mellitate hexadecahydrate — the honey-yellow mineral **mellite ("honeystone")**, which indeed occurs in (brown-)coal deposits and is the salt of the hexabasic, highly symmetric mellitic acid.
