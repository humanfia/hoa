## Detailed solution

### 7.1 Gases in M1 and M2; relation between x and y

Follow Fig. 1 stage by stage; every reaction is quantitative except NH₃ formation.

1. **Steam reformer**, feed x mol CH₄ + y mol H₂O, CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂ (H₂O limiting, so all y mol of water react). Outlet: (x−y) CH₄ + y CO + 3y H₂. Because the figure still labels CH₄ in this stream, x − y > 0, i.e. **x > y** already at this point.
2. **Secondary reformer**, feed 4 N₂ + 1 O₂ (air). Partial oxidation, 2 CH₄ + O₂ → 2 CO + 4H₂, is quantitative, so the 1 mol of O₂ must consume all remaining methane: x − y = 2. The outlet is **M1: 4 N₂ + (y+2) CO + (3y+4) H₂** — hence M1 contains N₂, CO, H₂ only (no CH₄, no O₂, no CO₂ yet, no H₂O, no NH₃).
3. **Shift converter**, CO + H₂O → CO₂ + H₂ (quantitative): 4 N₂ + (y+2) CO₂ + (4y+6) H₂.
4. **Scrubber Z** removes CO₂ quantitatively, leaving the synthesis feed 4 N₂ + (4y+6) H₂. The synthesis requires N₂ : H₂ = 1 : 3, so 4y + 6 = 12, giving y = 3/2 and x = y + 2 = 7/2 — confirming x > y.
5. **Synthesis reactor**, N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, the only non-quantitative step; therefore the reactor outlet **M2 (before the cooler CLR) contains N₂ + H₂ + NH₃**. CLR liquefies the NH₃ and the unreacted N₂/H₂ are recycled.

### 7.2 Annual mass of CH₄

Both methane-consuming reactions count: y = 3/2 mol CH₄ are steam-reformed and x − y = 2 mol CH₄ are burned by the O₂ of the 4 N₂ + 1 O₂ air feed (2 CH₄ + O₂ → 2 CO + 4 H₂). The overall quantitative balance (reforming + oxidation + shift + synthesis, per 4 mol N₂ + 1 mol O₂):

$$\mathrm{\tfrac72\,CH_4 + 5\,H_2O + 4\,N_2 + O_2 \longrightarrow \tfrac72\,CO_2 + 8\,NH_3}$$

Atom check: C 3.5 = 3.5; H 14 + 10 = 24 = 24; N 8 = 8; O 5 + 2 = 7 = 7 ✓. Hence

n(CH₄)/n(NH₃) = (7/2)/8 = 7/16 = 0.4375.

Molar masses (booklet table): M(NH₃) = 14.01 + 3(1.008) = 17.034 g mol⁻¹; M(CH₄) = 12.01 + 4(1.008) = 16.042 g mol⁻¹.

The plant delivers 660 000 t NH₃ per year at a 97.0 % overall yield, so the feed must supply the stoichiometric equivalent of 660 000/0.970 t of NH₃:

$$m(\mathrm{CH_4}) = \frac{660\,000\ \mathrm{t}}{0.970}\times\frac{7}{16}\times\frac{16.042}{17.034}
= 680\,412\ \mathrm{t}\times 0.4375 \times 0.94176
\approx 280\,345\ \mathrm{t\,yr^{-1}}$$

i.e. **m(CH₄) ≈ 2.80 × 10⁵ tons of CH₄ per year** (3 s.f., limited by 97.0 %). The ton units cancel against grams because the stoichiometric factor is a pure mole ratio.

### 7.3 Cyclic reactor model

Each fresh portion of n₀ = 4 mol of the 1 : 3 mixture contains a = n₀/4 = 1.00 mol of N₂. In every cycle a fraction η = 0.150 of the N₂ present is converted to NH₃ (removed by liquefaction), so the fraction q = 1 − η = 0.850 remains in the system.

**a)** After cycle 58 the freshest portion contributes a q, the previous one a q², …, the first one a q⁵⁸:

$$n_{58} = a\sum_{k=1}^{58} q^k = a\,\frac{q(1-q^{58})}{1-q}
= 1.00\times\frac{0.850\,(1-0.850^{58})}{0.150}
= \frac{0.850}{0.150}\,(1-8.06\times10^{-5}) = 5.66621\ \mathrm{mol}$$

(geometric-series formula from the general booklet) → **n(N₂) = 5.6662 mol** (4 d.p.).

**b)** After n cycles the total N₂ fed is na and the unreacted inventory is Uₙ = a q(1−qⁿ)/(1−q), so the overall yield is

$$Y_n = \frac{na - U_n}{na} = 1 - \frac{q(1-q^n)}{n(1-q)}.$$

Check: Y₁ = 1 − q = 0.150 ✓. Since q(1−qⁿ)/(1−q) → 0.850/0.150 = 5.6667 mol rapidly (0.85¹⁸⁸ ≈ 5×10⁻¹⁴), test the integers around n = 5.6667/0.030 = 188.9:

- n = 188: Y = 1 − 5.66667/188 = 0.969858 < 0.9700 ✗
- n = 189: Y = 1 − 5.66667/189 = 0.970018 ≥ 0.9700 ✓

**189 cycles** are needed (the first cycle already provides 15.0 %, so 188 further cycles).

### 7.4 CO₂ scrubbing with 3

The structure drawn for 3 is N-methyldiethanolamine, (HOCH₂CH₂)₂NCH₃, a **tertiary** amine: it has no N–H bond and therefore cannot form a carbamate by the primary/secondary-amine pathway. It acts as a Brønsted base that promotes bicarbonate formation:

$$\mathrm{(HOCH_2CH_2)_2NCH_3 + CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons [(HOCH_2CH_2)_2NHCH_3]^+ + HCO_3^-}$$

Balance: LHS = C₆H₁₅NO₅ (C₅H₁₃NO₂ + CO₂ + H₂O); RHS [C₅H₁₄NO₂]⁺ + [CHO₃]⁻ = C₆H₁₅NO₅ ✓; charges +1 + (−1) = 0 ✓. On heating, equilibrium shifts left and the solution is regenerated, as in industrial MDEA carbon capture.

### 7.5 Structure of the dinitrogen product 5

Moles of N₂ taken up by 1.00 g of 4 (ideal gas; 1 bar = 10⁵ Pa per the booklet):

$$n(\mathrm{N_2})=\frac{PV}{RT}=\frac{(1.0\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{Pa})(94.97\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^3})}{(8.314\ \mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}})(273.15\ \mathrm{K})}
= \frac{9.497}{2270.97} = 4.182\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol}$$

Moles of 4, using the printed M(4) = 597.824 g mol⁻¹ for the Mo–PNP pincer complex (PNP = 2,6-bis[(di-tert-butylphosphino)methyl]pyridine, κ³-P,N,P):

$$n(\mathbf{4}) = \frac{1.00}{597.824} = 1.6727\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol},\qquad
\frac{n(\mathrm{N_2})}{n(\mathbf{4})} = 2.500 \approx \frac{5}{2}$$

So five N₂ molecules are fixed per two Mo centres — a multinuclear product, not a monomer (a single Mo carrying five N₂ would be 8-coordinate and is unreasonable). Reduction of 4 removes its three chlorides (Mo(III) → Mo(0)), freeing three coordination sites on each PNP-bound Mo. The 5 : 2 uptake is then matched uniquely by the symmetric dimer

$$\mathrm{5 = [(PNP)Mo(N_2)_2]_2(\mu\!-\!N_2)}$$

**Drawing instruction (simplified ligand scheme):** draw each Mo gripped on one face by the simplified PNP pincer (a P⌢N⌢P bracket), place two linear, terminal, end-on Mo–N≡N units on each Mo, and connect the two Mo atoms through one end-on bridging N₂, (N₂)₂(PNP)Mo–N≡N–Mo(PNP)(N₂)₂ (μ-η¹:η¹). Count per 2 Mo: 4 terminal + 1 bridging N₂ = 5 ✓. Each Mo is 6-coordinate Mo(0) (d⁶) with an 18-electron count (6e from PNP + 4e from two terminal N₂ + 2e from the bridge + d⁶), and all N₂ ligands are formally neutral; back-donation into π*(N₂) weakens the N≡N bonds, which is the activation exploited in 7.6.

### 7.6 Ranking of the Red/Ad combinations A–D

The reference combinations of the printed calibration table isolate three effects:

1. **Reductant strength is a prerequisite.** With the same acid additive, raising E° of the reductant from −0.88 V to ≈ −1.15 V changes the yield from 0 to 11.8 mol NH₃; a reductant that is too weak cannot inject electrons into the Mo–N₂ units of 5.
2. **Counterion.** With the same reductant and the same acid cation, replacing Cl⁻ by triflate raises the yield from 0.7 to 11.8 mol — coordinating Cl⁻ poisons the Mo centre.
3. **Acid strength matching.** With the same reductant and triflate counterion, the additive with the **higher** conjugate-acid pKₐ gives more NH₃ (11.8 vs 9.1 mol): a too-strong acid (low pKₐ) is discharged directly by the reductant to H₂, diverting equivalents away from N₂ reduction.

All four combinations A–D use triflate additives, so effect 2 is constant. B, C, D share a strong reductant (E° = −1.10 V, comfortably above the ∼−0.9 V threshold seen in effect 1), so their yields follow pKₐ by effect 3: B (pKₐ 15.0) > C (13.7) > D (10.6). A also has pKₐ 13.7, but its reductant E° = −0.09 V is far weaker even than the zero-yield reference (−0.88 V), so its N₂ reduction never starts and A gives the smallest yield.

$$\mathrm{B > C > D > A}$$

### 7.7 Binary nitrides 7–9 and compound 10

i.e. 2.00 : 1.20 : 1.40 : 1.00 → multiply by 5 → **LaN : Ca₃N₂ : Si₃N₄ : SiO₂ = 10 : 6 : 7 : 5**.

**Step 3 — compose 10.** Total atom inventory: La₁₀Ca₁₈Si₂₆N₅₀O₁₀ = 2 × [La₅Ca₉Si₁₃N₂₅O₅]. Per formula unit the stipulated framework [Si₁₂N₂₄] absorbs 12 Si + 24 N; the remainder is La₅Ca₉ + Si₁N₁O₅. T must be a tetrahedral anion containing the spare Si: **T = [SiO₃N]⁵⁻**, an SiO₃N tetrahedron with charge (+4) + 3(−2) + (−3) = −5. The two oxygens left over are the monoatomic anions: **S₂ = 2 O²⁻**. (The alternative T = [SiO₄]⁴⁻ would leave “NO” unmatched by any monoatomic S — rejected.)

**Step 4 — Q, R, α, β and neutrality.** Highest oxidation states: Q = La³⁺, R = Ca²⁺, so 10 = **La₅Ca₉O₂[SiO₃N][Si₁₂N₂₄]** with α = 5, β = 9 (ungrouped empirical formula La₅Ca₉Si₁₃N₂₅O₅, already minimal). Charge balance: 5(+3) + 9(+2) = +33 vs 2(−2) + (−5) + [12(+4) + 24(−3)] = −4 − 5 − 24 = −33 ✓.

**Step 5 — single-product synthesis equation:**

$$\mathrm{10\,LaN + 6\,Ca_3N_2 + 7\,Si_3N_4 + 5\,SiO_2 \longrightarrow 2\,La_5Ca_9O_2[SiO_3N][Si_{12}N_{24}]}$$

Atom check: La 10 = 10; Ca 18 = 18; Si 21 + 5 = 26 = 2×13; N 10 + 12 + 28 = 50 = 2×25; O 10 = 2×5 ✓ — every atom ends up in the single red oxonitridosilicate, as stated in the problem.
