## Detailed solution

### 3.1 Empirical formula and carbon content of COF-1

The two monomers in the COF-1 drawing are the linear diboronic acid A2 (benzene-1,4-diboronic acid, C₆H₈B₂O₄) and the trigonal hexahydroxy node B3 (2,3,6,7,10,11-hexahydroxytriphenylene, C₁₈H₁₂O₆). Each boronic acid condenses with one catechol pair:

Ar–B(OH)₂ + HO–Ar′–OH → Ar–B(O–Ar′–O) + 2 H₂O

One B3 node (three catechol sites) consumes three B(OH)₂ groups, but each A2 linker is shared between two B3 nodes, so one topological repeat contains 1 B3 + 3/2 A2, and there are 3 boronate rings per repeat:

C₁₈H₁₂O₆ + (3/2) C₆H₈B₂O₄ − 6 H₂O → C₂₇H₁₂B₃O₆ = 3 (C₉H₄BO₂)

Atom balance: C: 18 + 9 = 27 ✔; H: 12 + 12 − 12 = 12 ✔; B: 3 ✔; O: 6 + 6 − 6 = 6 ✔.

Hence the empirical formula is **C₉H₄BO₂**, with

M(C₉H₄BO₂) = 9(12.01) + 4(1.008) + 10.81 + 2(16.00) = 108.09 + 4.032 + 10.81 + 32.00 = 154.932 g mol⁻¹

w(C) = (108.09 / 154.932) × 100 % = 69.766 % ≈ **69.77 %**

### 3.2 Internal diameter of the COF-2 honeycomb

COF-2 is the boroxine COF: adjacent boronic acids of one monomer trimerise into B₃O₃ rings. The vertices of the ideal topological hexagon are the boroxine ring centres. For a regular B₃O₃ ring the centre-to-B radius equals its side, the B–O bond length; likewise the centre of a regular benzene ring lies one arene C–C bond length from a substituted carbon. One straight side from boroxine centre to boroxine centre therefore contains, at each end, one (B–O) radius + one C–B bond + one arene radius:

a = 2 × (r(B–O) + r(C–B) + r(C–C)) = 2 × (1.38 + 1.56 + 1.39) Å = 2 × 4.33 = 8.66 Å

Using the given inscribed-circle relation:

d = √3 · a = 1.7321 × 8.66 Å = 14.9996 Å ≈ **15.0 Å**

All three supplied bond lengths are used exactly once per half-side.

### 3.4 Repeat units of COFs 3–6

All four networks are Hexagonal-1 constructions: one black trigonal core joined through three red linear linkers. The repeat boundary is drawn exactly as for COF-1 — the dashed line passes through the centre of each shared red phenylene, so each repeat contains one black core + three half-linkers (= three linkages).

**COF-3 (o-hydroxy imine honeycomb).** Black core = benzene-1,3,5-tricarbaldehyde `T`; red linker = 1,4-diamino-2,5-dihydroxybenzene. Both ends of each linker condense:

`T–CH=N–C₆H₂(OH)₂–N=CH–T`, red-ring sequence C1(N), C2(OH), C3(H), C4(N), C5(OH), C6(H).

Each phenolic OH is ortho to an imine nitrogen and closes an intramolecular O–H···N hydrogen bond, which shields the C=N from acid/base hydrolysis; the IR spectrum retains an imine stretch ✔. Balance per repeat:

C₉H₆O₃ + (3/2) C₆H₈N₂O₂ − 3 H₂O → C₁₈H₁₂N₃O₃ (C: 9+9 = 18 ✔; H: 6+12−6 = 12 ✔; N: 3 ✔; O: 3+3−3 = 3 ✔).

**COF-4 (bis-benzoxazole honeycomb).** Each o-hydroxy imine unit is oxidatively closed: the phenolic O on the red ring attacks the adjacent imine carbon, a C–O bond forms, and aromatisation expels the imine C–H and the phenolic O–H. In atom-explicit terms, with `X` the aldehyde-derived carbon (bearing the black-core aryl `T`) and C1–C2 adjacent red-ring carbons:

`T–X`, `X=N1–C1(red)`, `X–O2–C2(red)`, C1–C2 bond retained → five-membered ring `X–N1–C1–C2–O2`, i.e. a 2-substituted benzoxazole. The identical second closure occurs at `X′`, `N4`, `O5` on red-ring carbons C4–C5 at the other end of the linker.

This closure is geometrically possible only with the OH on the **red (amine-derived)** ring, where O and imine N are ortho on the same ring (5-membered closure); a salicylidene-type OH on the aldehyde ring would place O and N on different rings and could not form a benzoxazole — consistent with "loss of six additional hydrogens as the only change". Balance: each of the three linkages per repeat loses H₂, so

C₁₈H₁₂N₃O₃ − 3 H₂ → **C₁₈H₆N₃O₃** (loss of 6 H ✔).

The residual C=N bonds are the endocyclic oxazole O–C=N bonds — a different type of C=N, so the imine IR stretch disappears ✔.

**COF-5 (enol–imine honeycomb).** Black core = 2,4,6-triformylphloroglucinol `P` (still aromatic, enol form, C₉H₆O₆); red linker = p-phenylenediamine. Three imine condensations per repeat give the conjugated edge

`P–CH=N–p-C₆H₄–N=CH–P`

with O–H···N contacts along each arm; an imine stretch is present in the IR. Balance per repeat:

C₉H₆O₆ + (3/2) C₆H₈N₂ − 3 H₂O → C₁₈H₁₂N₃O₃ ✔.

**COF-6 (β-keto-enamine honeycomb).** Irreversible proton-shift tautomerisation of COF-5: at each arm the phenolic O–H proton moves to the imine N while the π bonds shift; the former ring C–OH positions become ring C=O, each former C=N becomes C–NH, and the aldehyde-derived carbon forms a C=C bond to the core. The black core is then a cyclohexane-1,3,5-trione bearing exocyclic `=CH–NH–` groups at its 2,4,6 positions, and a complete edge is

`Kᵢ=C(H)–NH–p-C₆H₄–NH–C(H)=Kⱼ`

with intramolecular N–H···O=C hydrogen bonds. No C=N bond remains → imine stretch absent ✔. Tautomerisation moves only H atoms and π bonds, so COF-6 keeps the COF-5 formula C₁₈H₁₂N₃O₃; the extended keto-enamine conjugation makes COF-5 → COF-6 irreversible ✔.

### 3.5 Structure of macrocycle X

COF-7 is the Kagome framework of the printed combination **C2 + D4**: a bent planar 4-connected amine node C2 containing one internal stilbene bond Ar–CH=CH–Ar, imine-linked through the linear dialdehyde D4 (tetrafluoroterephthalaldehyde).

**Decoding the printed post-synthetic sequence.** The first step oxidises every imine to an amide (chlorite/Pinnick-type oxidation):

Ar–N=CH–C₆F₄–CH=N–Ar → Ar–NH–C(O)–C₆F₄–C(O)–NH–Ar

The degradation step is ozonolysis (O₃, then Me₂S), which cleaves only the internal alkene of each C2 vertex:

Ar–CH=CH–Ar → 2 Ar–CHO

so each C2 node is split into two identical, 120°-angled, aldehyde-terminated corner pieces

V = OHC–C₆H₃[–p-C₆H₄–NH–]₂ (1,3,5-substitution around its central ring).

Around a Kagome hexagonal pore the 120° turn is made inside each such corner; cleaving every C2 vertex therefore releases the boundary hexagon of the pore, in which six corners V alternate with the six oxidised edges

L = –C(O)–C₆F₄–C(O)–.

The exact connectivity is `Nᵢ(arm)–C(O)–C₆F₄–C(O)–Nᵢ₊₁(arm)` for i = 1…6 cyclically, each L joining a terminal amide N of Vᵢ to one of Vᵢ₊₁. Hence

**X = cyclo[V–L]₆,** with the six aldehydes produced by ozonolysis pointing outward.

**Smallest repeat unit** (one sixth of the ring): one V–L pair. Atom count: V = C₆H₃(CHO)(C₆H₄)₂(NH)₂ = C₁₉H₁₄N₂O; L = C(O)–C₆F₄–C(O) = C₈F₄O₂; so V–L = **C₂₇H₁₄F₄N₂O₃** (C: 19+8 = 27; H: 14; F: 4; N: 2; O: 1+2 = 3 ✔).

### 3.6 π–π stacking energies of COF-8

COF-8 comes from the printed Hexagonal-2 combination E1 + D2 (trigonal + trigonal). Reading the printed monomers: E1 (1,3,5-tris(cyanomethyl)benzene, C₁₂H₉N₃) supplies one benzene node; D2 (2,4,6-tris(4-formylphenyl)-1,3,5-triazine, C₂₄H₁₅N₃O₃) supplies one triazine core plus three peripheral benzene rings. One repeat unit therefore presents, face-to-face between adjacent layers,

4 benzene rings + 1 triazine ring.

**AA (eclipsed, like-over-like):** all five ring pairs stack in the first geometry column of the table:

E(AA) = 4 E(b–b) + E(t–t) = 4(−7.9) + (−6.7) = −31.6 − 6.7 = **−38.3 kJ mol⁻¹**

**AA′ check (slipped, like-over-like):** using the second (slipped) column,

E(AA′) = 4(−12.6) + (−16.7) = −50.4 − 16.7 = −67.1 kJ mol⁻¹ ✔ — exactly the printed bilayer result, which confirms the 4 b + 1 t contact inventory.

**AB and AB′:** as labelled beside the printed stacking diagrams, shifting the honeycomb by one sublattice puts the single triazine of one layer over a single benzene of the other; all remaining aromatic rings lie over pore openings and contribute nothing (π–π stacking only between aromatic units, as stipulated). There is therefore **one** benzene–triazine contact per repeat unit, taken straight from the b–t row of the table:

E(AB) = E(b–t) = **−49.8 kJ mol⁻¹** (first overlap geometry)
E(AB′) = E′(b–t) = **−55.6 kJ mol⁻¹** (second, slightly slipped geometry)

Summing five contacts for AB/AB′ (e.g. −49.8 − 4×7.9) is invalid: once the sublattices are interchanged the benzenes cannot simultaneously remain eclipsed over benzenes.

### 3.7 Uranyl uptake of COF-9

**Equilibrium absorption capacity.** All uranium is present as UO₂²⁺; volume 200.0 mL = 0.2000 dm³.

m(adsorbed) = (C₀ − Cₑ)·V = (19.90 − 9.225) mg dm⁻³ × 0.2000 dm³ = 10.675 × 0.2000 = 2.135 mg

qₑ = m(adsorbed)/m(COF) = 2.135 mg / 0.005000 g = **427.0 mg g⁻¹** (4 s.f.)

**Ions per pore.** From the printed structures: E1 = C₁₂H₉N₃, D2 = C₂₄H₁₅N₃O₃. One E1 + one D2 form three C=C linkages, eliminating 3 H₂O, so the formula associated with one pore is

C₁₂H₉N₃ + C₂₄H₁₅N₃O₃ − 3 H₂O = C₃₆H₁₈N₆
(C: 36 ✔; H: 9+15−6 = 18 ✔; N: 6 ✔; O: 3−3 = 0 ✔)

carrying three cyanostilbene nitrile groups per pore. In COF-9 each nitrile is converted to an amidoxime, –C≡N + NH₂OH → –C(=N–OH)–NH₂, adding N H₃ O per group:

C₃₆H₁₈N₆ + 3(NH₃O) = **C₃₆H₂₇N₉O₃**,  M = 36(12.01) + 27(1.008) + 9(14.01) + 3(16.00) = 432.36 + 27.216 + 126.09 + 48.00 = 633.67 g mol⁻¹ per pore.

Pore-sharing check (honeycomb): six vertices border a pore and each vertex is shared by three pores, giving 6 × 1/3 = 2 effective vertices per pore = one E1 + one D2 — exactly the formula unit above.

Number of moles of each species:

n(pores) = 5.000 × 10⁻³ g / 633.67 g mol⁻¹ = 7.891 × 10⁻⁶ mol
n(UO₂²⁺) = 2.135 × 10⁻³ g / (238.03 + 2 × 16.00) g mol⁻¹ = 2.135 × 10⁻³ / 270.03 = 7.907 × 10⁻⁶ mol

Avogadro's constant cancels in the ratio:

N(UO₂²⁺)/N(pores) = 7.907 × 10⁻⁶ / 7.891 × 10⁻⁶ = 1.002 ≈ **1.00 UO₂²⁺ ion per pore**

Note on the calculation: 270.03 g mol⁻¹ is correctly the molar mass of *one uranyl ion* and must be used for the ion count; the framework side must be evaluated with the actual COF-9 pore formula derived above (M = 633.67 g mol⁻¹), not with an assumed repeat mass.
