## Detailed solution

### 6.1 Aromatic π systems of the cyclocarbons

Every carbon of a cyclo[*n*]carbon is sp‑hybridised and contributes two electrons — one to each of two mutually perpendicular, fully conjugated loops around the ring: an in‑plane loop and an out‑of‑plane loop. Hückel’s rule is applied separately to each loop; only closed‑shell loops are classified (4k + 2 electrons = aromatic, A; 4k electrons = anti‑aromatic, AA).

- **C18**: each loop holds 18 electrons, 18 = 4×4 + 2 → both loops aromatic: A = 2, AA = 0 (“doubly aromatic”).
- **C16**: each loop holds 16 electrons, 16 = 4×4 → both loops anti‑aromatic: A = 0, AA = 2.
- **³C13 (S = 1)**: the two unpaired electrons have parallel spins and must occupy *different* perpendicular loops, so each loop holds 13 electrons (one unpaired electron per loop; 13 + 13 = 26). Thirteen is neither a 4k + 2 nor a 4k closed‑shell count, and an open (radical) loop is not classified by the ground‑state Hückel rule — no invented “odd‑electron” rules are needed: A = 0, AA = 0.
- **¹C13 (S = 0)**: a singlet has no unpaired electrons, so each loop must contain an even number of electrons. The 26 π electrons redistribute by one pair into a 14 + 12 split (26 conserved): 14 = 4×3 + 2 → one aromatic loop; 12 = 4×3 → one anti‑aromatic loop: A = 1, AA = 1.

Every molecule contains exactly two π‑systems, which is consistent with the A + AA totals in each row (2, 2, 0 and 2 respectively: in ³C13 both loops are open and simply unclassified).

### 6.2 Intermediates B–D in the AFM synthesis of C14

The scheme starts from decachloroanthracene: Cl on the ten perimeter CH positions C1, C2, C4, C6, C7, C8, C9, C11, C13, C14, with quaternary fusion carbons C3, C5, C10, C12 and fusion bonds C3–C12 and C5–C10. Two elementary events govern the surface chemistry: (i) voltage pulses strip chlorine atoms; removing an odd total number of Cl atoms leaves one unpaired electron (hence the note that the boxes may contain unpaired electrons); (ii) once the two para chlorines of one six‑ring are gone, the resulting 1,4‑didehydro diradical undergoes retro‑Bergman opening, which deletes that ring’s fusion bond.

- **A (given example)**: loss of Cl from C2 and C13 (the two para chlorines of the left ring), then retro‑Bergman opening of fusion bond C3–C12 → C14Cl8.
- **A → B**: five further chlorines are removed (those at C1, C4, C8, C11, C14), so **B = C14Cl₃•** with Cl remaining at C6, C7, C9. The right fusion bond C5–C10 is still present. The printed Lewis form puts the radical on C8; bond orders (3,1,3,1,2,1,2,1,2,1,3,1,3,1) — triple bonds C1≡C2, C3≡C4, C11≡C12, C13≡C14; double bonds C5=C6, C7=C8, C9=C10.
- **B → C**: removal of the right ring’s remaining para pair (Cl at C6 and C9) triggers the second retro‑Bergman opening (C5–C10 broken), so **C** is the fully monocyclic **C₁₄Cl•**: Cl at C7, radical at C8, bond orders (3,1,3,1,3,1,2,1,3,1,3,1,3,1).
- **C → C14 (shown end product, not a lettered box)**: loss of the last Cl• at C7 converts the C7=C8 double bond into a triple bond, leaving the alternating single/triple‑bond form of cyclo[14]carbon.
- **A → D (branch)**: the chlorines removed here are at C6, C9 (again opening C5–C10) and C11; **D = monocyclic C₁₄Cl₅•** with Cl at C1, C4, C7, C8, C14 and the radical localised at C11; bond orders (2,2,2,1,3,1,2,1,3,1,2,2,2,1), i.e., two cumulene runs (C1=C2=C3=C4 and C11=C12=C13=C14), alkynes C5≡C6 and C9≡C10, and the double bond C7=C8.

Chlorine budgets check against the precursor C14Cl10: B keeps 10 − (2 + 5) = 3 Cl, C keeps 3 − 2 = 1 Cl, D keeps 10 − (2 + 3) = 5 Cl; B, C and D each carry the single unpaired electron required by their odd number of removed chlorines. All three bond‑order lists satisfy carbon valence: each chlorinated carbon has C–C bond‑order sum 3 plus its C–Cl bond; each closed unchlorinated carbon has sum 4; each indicated radical carbon has sum 3 plus one unpaired electron. (Each list can be shifted to resonance‑equivalent forms; the dot may be drawn on the equivalent conjugated position.)

### 6.3 Which halogen can be cleaved at 2.5 V?

One electron accelerated through U = 2.5 V gains e·U; per mole of electrons, using the booklet Faraday constant:

E = F·U = 96 485 C mol⁻¹ × 2.5 V = 241 212.5 J mol⁻¹ = 241.2125 kJ mol⁻¹ ≈ 241.2 kJ mol⁻¹

(241 212.5 J mol⁻¹ is the exact product; the 2.5 V datum carries two significant figures, so the result may be quoted as 2.4×10² kJ mol⁻¹, with the exact value retained for the bond‑energy comparison.) Cross‑check with the electronvolt constants: 2.5 eV × (1.602×10⁻¹⁹ J eV⁻¹) × (6.022×10²³ mol⁻¹) = 241 181 J mol⁻¹ ≈ 2.412×10⁵ J mol⁻¹ — the same value to three significant figures (the small difference comes from rounding of e and N_A in the booklet).

A bond can be broken only if its dissociation energy is not larger than the available ~241 kJ mol⁻¹:

| Bond | BDE / kJ mol⁻¹ | cleaved at 2.5 V? |
|---|---:|---|
| C–F | 467 | no |
| C–Cl | 346 | no |
| C–Br | 290 | no |
| C–I | 228 | **yes** |

228 < 241.2 < 290, so the halogenated precursor for the retro‑Bergman synthesis of C18 may contain **iodine only**.

### 6.4 Ions in the electrospray mass spectrum

Integer atomic masses (C = 12, H = 1, N = 14, O = 16):

M(E) = 40×12 + 34×1 + 2×14 + 3×16 = 480 + 34 + 28 + 48 = **590 Da**; M(C48) = 48×12 = **576 Da**.

Catenation adds atoms and loses none, so the neutral assemblies are E alone (590), C48 threaded by two E (576 + 1180 = 1756) and by three E (576 + 1770 = 2346). In positive mode the nitrogen atoms are protonated, giving [M + zH]ᶻ⁺ ions with m/z = (M + z)/z; no fragmentation occurs. The four exact checks:

- m/z 591 (given example): (590 + 1)/1 = 591 → **[E + H]⁺**, singly protonated free macrocycle.
- m/z 783: (576 + 3×590 + 3)/3 = 2349/3 = 783 → **[C48·3E + 3H]³⁺** (triply protonated catenane with three threaded rings).
- m/z 879: (576 + 2×590 + 2)/2 = 1758/2 = 879 → **[C48·2E + 2H]²⁺** (doubly protonated catenane with two threaded rings).
- m/z 1174: (576 + 3×590 + 2)/2 = 2348/2 = 1174 → **[C48·3E + 2H]²⁺** (the second charge state of the three‑ring catenane).

All four peaks are exact integer fits with no leftover mass, and the two three‑ring peaks are mutually consistent: 3×783 − 3 = 2×1174 − 2 = 2346 Da. No other simple [C48·mE + zH]ᶻ⁺ combination (m = 0–3, z = 1–3) reproduces the observed set.

### 6.5 Synthesis of the [5]CPP precursor — structures F–L

The scheme is the modular “masked‑aromatic” route: each bent 1,4‑diarylcyclohexa‑2,5‑diene unit D serves as a masked benzene ring that kinks the chain sharply enough to close a five‑ring hoop. The starting material drawn in the booklet is the para‑quinol 4‑hydroxy‑4‑(4′‑TBSO‑biphenyl‑4‑yl)cyclohexa‑2,5‑dien‑1‑one.

1. **F**: NaH deprotonates the quinol OH, and p‑bromophenyllithium adds 1,2 to its ketone; protonation gives the 1,4‑diaryl‑1,4‑diol F, i.e., p‑Br–Ph–D_H–p‑Ph–p‑Ph–OTBS (C¹ of D bears OH and the 4‑bromophenyl arm, C⁴ bears OH and the TBSO‑biphenyl arm). C₃₀H₃₃BrO₃Si.
2. **G**: two equivalents of TESCl protect both alcohols (each O–H → O–SiEt₃, net +C₆H₁₄Si per site): C₃₀H₃₃BrO₃Si + C₁₂H₂₈Si₂ = **C₄₂H₆₁BrO₃Si₃**.
3. **H**: LiOH cleaves only the remote TBS aryl ether (the two TES ethers survive), releasing the terminal phenol: G − C₆H₁₄Si = **C₃₆H₄₇BrO₃Si₂** (matches the formula printed beside H).
4. **I**: PhI(OAc)₂/H₂O — the stated two‑electron oxidant — dearomatises that phenol into the new terminal para‑quinol. Balance: ArOH + H₂O → quinol + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻; PhI(OAc)₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → PhI + 2 AcOH (I(III) → I(I)). One oxygen is added and the hydrogen count is unchanged: H(C₃₆H₄₇BrO₃Si₂) → I(**C₃₆H₄₇BrO₄Si₂**). The phenolic oxygen stays on its original carbon and becomes the C1 carbonyl oxygen of the quinol, while water supplies the oxygen of the newly installed para C4–OH group.
5. **J**: the NaH/aryllithium sequence is repeated on the new carbonyl, adding a terminal p‑bromophenyl group and generating the second 1,4‑diol unit D_H: I + C₆H₅Br = **C₄₂H₅₂Br₂O₄Si₂**, i.e., p‑Br–Ph–D_TES–p‑Ph–D_H–p‑Ph–Br.
6. **K**: TES protection of the second diol: +C₁₂H₂₈Si₂ → **C₅₄H₈₀Br₂O₄Si₄**.
7. **L**: intramolecular nickel(0) reductive (Yamamoto) coupling of the two terminal aryl bromides forms the closing biaryl bond and ejects both bromines: C₅₄H₈₀Br₂O₄Si₄ → **C₅₄H₈₀O₄Si₄**, exactly the formula printed beside L. The product is the five‑ring belt cyclo[p‑Ph–D_TES–p‑Ph–D_TES–p‑Ph] — three aromatic rings plus two masked cyclohexadienylene rings.

L is therefore the *protected precursor* of [5]CPP (C₃₀H₂₀): subsequent fluoride removal of all four TES groups and reductive aromatisation of the two D rings delivers the five para‑phenylenes of the nanohoop; L itself is drawn as the silyl‑protected macrocycle.

### 6.6 Template synthesis building blocks M–R

The boxes follow the printed arrows in order, which fixes the identity of every letter; the letters run M, N, O, Q, R, with P reserved for the nanoring product P6 discussed in 6.7.

- **M**: Friedel–Crafts tert‑butylation of toluene is reversible; under thermodynamic control the two bulky t‑Bu groups avoid each other and the methyl group, giving the 1,3,5‑pattern, i.e., 3,5‑di‑tert‑butyltoluene, C₁₅H₂₄. The hint checks out: it has exactly **four proton environments** — the two equivalent t‑Bu groups (18 H), the CH₃ (3 H), the equivalent aryl H‑2/H‑6 pair (2 H), and aryl H‑4 (1 H). Any other regioisomer is more hindered and shows more signals.
- **N**: NBS/benzoyl peroxide brominates the benzylic CH₃ to ArCH₂Br, and the Sommelet sequence (HMTA, then acidic aqueous workup) converts it into 3,5‑di‑tert‑butylbenzaldehyde, C₁₅H₂₂O.
- **O**: the N → O arrow bundles the porphyrin assembly: two molecules of N and two of dipyrromethane condense under acid, DDQ oxidises the porphyrinogen, and Zn(OAc)₂ inserts Zn(II). The product is the trans‑A₂ zinc porphyrin — zinc(II) 5,15‑bis(3,5‑di‑tert‑butylphenyl)porphyrin with meso‑H at 10 and 20 — **C₄₈H₅₂N₄Zn** (an Ar group is C₁₄H₂₁; the zinc porphyrin core carrying two meso H is C₂₀H₁₀N₄Zn, so 20 + 28 = 48 C and 10 + 42 = 52 H).
- **Q**: NBS brominates both remaining meso positions (10 and 20) of the zinc porphyrin: C₄₈H₅₂N₄Zn − 2H + 2Br = **C₄₈H₅₀Br₂N₄Zn**.
- **R**: double Sonogashira coupling with trihexylsilylacetylene installs –C≡C–Si(n‑Hex)₃ at 10 and 20, and n‑Bu₄NF strips both silyl groups, replacing each Br by –C≡CH: C₄₈H₅₀Br₂N₄Zn − 2Br + 2(C₂H) = **C₅₂H₅₂N₄Zn**. Oxidative (Glaser–Hay) homocoupling of these terminal alkynes on the template then furnishes the meso–C≡C–C≡C–meso butadiyne links of the nanobelt P6.

### 6.7 Global aromaticity of oxidised P6

The booklet’s counting convention: only the π bonds lying on the continuous bold circuit are counted, and a –C≡C– unit contributes only its *in‑plane* (conjugating) π bond (2 e⁻), not the perpendicular one. P6 consists of six porphyrin units linked by six butadiyne bridges (meso–C≡C–C≡C–meso):

- Each porphyrin segment between its two linked meso carbons spans ten bonds of the macrocycle perimeter; in the alternating Kekulé structure exactly five of them are double bonds → **10 π electrons per porphyrin**.
- Each butadiyne (two triple bonds) contributes one conjugating π bond per triple bond → 2 × 2 e⁻ = **4 π electrons per bridge**.

Neutral P6: n₀(π) = 6 × (10 + 4) = **84 π electrons = 4×21** — a 4n count.

Only closed‑shell (even‑electron) circuits are classified by Hückel’s rule, exactly as for the loops in 6.1. The accessible oxidation ladder of the closed loop is:

- 84 e⁻ (neutral P6) = 4×21 — a 4n count;
- 82 e⁻ (P6²⁺, two electrons removed) = 4×20 + 2 — Hückel **aromatic** ✓;
- 80 e⁻ (P6⁴⁺, four electrons removed) = 4×20 — globally **anti‑aromatic** (the even 4n oxidised state alluded to in the prompt);
- 78 e⁻ (P6⁶⁺) = 4×19 + 2 — aromatic again.

Removing a single electron would give an open‑shell 83‑electron radical cation, which — like the open 13‑electron loops of ³C13 in 6.1 — belongs to neither Hückel class (it is neither a 4k nor a 4k + 2 closed shell). Because the neutral count is 84 ≡ 0 (mod 4) and a 4k + 2 count is even, electrons must be removed in pairs, and the minimum removal landing on 4k + 2 is two electrons: 84 − 2 = 82 = 4×20 + 2.

Hence **n(e) = 2** electrons removed and **n(t) = 82 π electrons** in the globally aromatic system.
