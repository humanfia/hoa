## Detailed solution

### 7.1 Process streams and the relation between x and y

In the steam reformer, all $y$ mol of water react. Because the stream leaving
that reactor is labelled $\mathrm{CH_4,CO,H_2}$ and contains no water, the
amounts leaving it are

$$
\mathrm{(x-y)CH_4+yCO+3yH_2},
$$

so already $x>y$.

The second reactor receives $\mathrm{4N_2+O_2}$. Quantitative partial oxidation
of the remaining methane,

$$
\mathrm{2CH_4+O_2\longrightarrow2CO+4H_2},
$$

requires $x-y=2$. Its outlet M1 therefore contains

$$
\mathrm{M1:}\qquad
4\mathrm{N_2}+(y+2)\mathrm{CO}+(3y+4)\mathrm{H_2}.
$$

There is no methane or oxygen left. Quantitative water-gas shift converts every
mole of CO into one mole each of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and additional $\mathrm{H_2}$:

$$
4\mathrm{N_2}+(y+2)\mathrm{CO_2}+(4y+6)\mathrm{H_2}.
$$

After the scrubber removes $\mathrm{CO_2}$, the synthesis feed must have the
stoichiometric ratio $\mathrm{N_2:H_2}=1:3$. Hence

$$
4y+6=3(4)=12,
\qquad y=\frac32,
\qquad x=y+2=\frac72.
$$

This confirms $x>y$. Since ammonia formation is the only nonquantitative
reaction, the synthesis-reactor outlet M2 contains unreacted $\mathrm{N_2}$ and
$\mathrm{H_2}$ together with the $\mathrm{NH_3}$ product.

### 7.2 Annual methane requirement

For the amounts just found, $\frac72$ mol of methane generate the
$4\mathrm{N_2}+12\mathrm{H_2}$ feed that can form 8 mol of ammonia. The full
quantitative material balance before applying the ammonia yield can be written

$$
\mathrm{\frac72CH_4+5H_2O+4N_2+O_2
\longrightarrow\frac72CO_2+8NH_3}.
$$

This equation balances C (3.5), H (24), N (8), and O (7) on both sides. Using
the booklet atomic masses,

$$
M(\mathrm{CH_4})=12.01+4(1.008)=16.042\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}},
$$

$$
M(\mathrm{NH_3})=14.01+3(1.008)=17.034\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

The stated 97.0% overall yield means that the methane feed must correspond to
$660\,000/0.970$ tons of theoretical ammonia. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
m(\mathrm{CH_4})
&=660\,000\ \mathrm{t\ NH_3}
  \left(\frac{1}{0.970}\right)
  \left(\frac{3.5\ \mathrm{mol\ CH_4}}{8\ \mathrm{mol\ NH_3}}\right)
  \left(\frac{16.042}{17.034}\right)\\
&=280\,344.56\ \mathrm{t\ CH_4\,year^{-1}}\\
&\approx 2.80\times10^5\ \mathrm{t\ CH_4\,year^{-1}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### 7.3 Cyclic reactor

Let $q=1-\eta=0.850$ be the fraction of a reagent left after one cycle. Each
fresh $n_0=4$ mol stoichiometric portion contains

$$
a=n_0\left(\frac{1}{1+3}\right)=1.00\ \mathrm{mol\ N_2}.
$$

#### 7.3(a)

At the end of cycle 58, the portion just added has left $aq$ mol of nitrogen,
the preceding portion has left $aq^2$ mol, and the first portion has left
$aq^{58}$ mol. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{N_2},58}
 &=a\sum_{k=1}^{58}q^k
 =a\frac{q(1-q^{58})}{1-q}\\
 &=1.00\ \mathrm{mol}\,
   \frac{0.850(1-0.850^{58})}{1-0.850}\\
 &=5.66620997\ \mathrm{mol}
 =\boxed{5.6662\ \mathrm{mol}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

#### 7.3(b)

After $n$ cycles the total fresh nitrogen supplied is $na$, whereas the
unreacted nitrogen still in the system is

$$
U_n=a\frac{q(1-q^n)}{1-q}.
$$

Because product ammonia is removed but all unreacted gas remains in the system,
the overall yield relative to all fresh feed is

$$
Y_n=1-\frac{U_n}{na}
   =1-\frac{q(1-q^n)}{n(1-q)}.
$$

This expression gives $Y_1=1-q=0.150$, as required. Testing the two integers
around the 97.0% threshold gives

$$
Y_{188}=0.9698582<0.970,
\qquad
Y_{189}=0.9700176>0.970.
$$

Hence the first qualifying cycle is cycle 189.

### 7.4 Capture of carbon dioxide by 3

Compound 3 is the tertiary amine
$\mathrm{(HOCH_2CH_2)_2NCH_3}$ (N-methyldiethanolamine). A tertiary amine has
no N-H bond and therefore cannot give a neutral carbamic acid or carbamate by
the usual primary/secondary-amine route. Instead it accepts a proton while
water and carbon dioxide form bicarbonate:

$$
\mathrm{(HOCH_2CH_2)_2NCH_3+CO_2+H_2O
\rightleftharpoons[(HOCH_2CH_2)_2NHCH_3]^++HCO_3^-}.
$$

The equation is atom-balanced and its two ionic products have zero net charge.

### 7.5 Structure of the dinitrogen complex 5

The amount of dinitrogen taken up is obtained from the ideal-gas law:

$$
\begin{aligned}
n(\mathrm{N_2})
 &=\frac{PV}{RT}\\
 &=\frac{(1.00\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa})
          (94.97\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^3})}
         {(8.314\ \mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}})(273.15\ \mathrm{K})}\\
 &=4.18192\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Meanwhile,

$$
n(\mathbf 4)=\frac{1.00\ \mathrm{g}}
                    {597.824\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}}
             =1.67273\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\frac{n(\mathrm{N_2})}{n(\mathbf 4)}=2.50005\approx\frac52.
$$

Here PNP is 2,6-bis[(di-tert-butylphosphino)methyl]pyridine, coordinated through
both P atoms and the pyridine N. Two Mo precursors therefore incorporate five
dinitrogen molecules. After the three chlorides on each precursor have been
removed by the three equivalents of reductant, the three available sites at
each PNP-bound Mo are occupied by two terminal $\mathrm{N_2}$ ligands and one
end of a shared $\mathrm{N_2}$ ligand. Thus

$$
\mathrm{5=\big[(PNP)Mo(N_2)_2\big]_2(\mu\!\!-N_2)},
$$

with a $\mu$-$\eta^1:\eta^1$ end-on bridge. This structure has four terminal
plus one bridging $\mathrm{N_2}$ per two Mo centers, exactly the measured
uptake.

### 7.6 Reductant/additive ranking

The supplied reference combinations isolate three trends:

1. Combinations 1 and 4 use the same 2,6-lutidinium triflate additive, but the
   more negative reductant potential in 4 ($-1.15$ rather than $-0.88$ V)
   changes the yield from 0 to 11.8 mol. A sufficiently strong reductant is
   essential.
2. Combinations 2 and 4 use the same reductant and the same acid cation, but
   replacing chloride by triflate changes the yield from 0.7 to 11.8 mol.
   Chloride strongly suppresses the yield.
3. Combinations 3 and 4 use the same reductant and triflate counterion. The
   higher-$pK_a$ additive gives the larger yield (11.8 rather than 9.1 mol).

All four new additives have triflate counterions. B, C, and D also have the
same reductant potential, $E^\circ=-1.10$ V, so their yields follow their
$pK_a$ values:

$$
\mathrm{B}\ (15.0)>\mathrm{C}\ (13.7)>\mathrm{D}\ (10.6).
$$

A has the same additive $pK_a$ as C but a much less reducing reductant
($E^\circ=-0.09$ V), even weaker than the zero-yield reference combination 1.
It is therefore last, giving $\mathrm{B>C>D>A}$.

### 7.7 Binary nitrides and compound 10

Using the booklet atomic masses, the three proposed binary nitrides reproduce
the given nitrogen mass fractions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_{\mathrm N}(\mathrm{LaN})
 &=\frac{14.01}{138.9+14.01}=0.09162=9.162\%,\\
w_{\mathrm N}(\mathrm{Ca_3N_2})
 &=\frac{2(14.01)}{3(40.08)+2(14.01)}=0.18899=18.899\%,\\
w_{\mathrm N}(\mathrm{Si_3N_4})
 &=\frac{4(14.01)}{3(28.09)+4(14.01)}=0.39940=39.940\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $\mathbf 7=\mathrm{LaN}$, $\mathbf 8=\mathrm{Ca_3N_2}$, and
$\mathbf 9=\mathrm{Si_3N_4}$. Convert the stated reagent masses to relative
amounts:

| Reagent | $M$ ($\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$) | amount in the stated ratio (mol) | ratio to $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $\mathrm{LaN}$ | 152.91 | $5.09/152.91=0.0332876$ | 2.000 |
| $\mathrm{Ca_3N_2}$ | 148.26 | $2.96/148.26=0.0199649$ | 1.200 |
| $\mathrm{Si_3N_4}$ | 140.31 | $3.27/140.31=0.0233055$ | 1.400 |
| $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ | 60.09 | $1.00/60.09=0.0166417$ | 1.000 |

Multiplication by 5 gives the integer reagent ratio
$10:6:7:5$. Those amounts contain

$$
\mathrm{La_{10}Ca_{18}Si_{26}O_{10}N_{50}}.
$$

Two units of the stipulated $\mathrm{[Si_{12}N_{24}]}$ framework account for
$\mathrm{Si_{24}N_{48}}$. Per product formula unit, the remaining atoms are
$\mathrm{SiO_5N}$. The two monoatomic anions $S$ are therefore two oxide ions,
and the remaining $\mathrm{SiO_3N}$ forms the requested tetrahedral anion:

$$
S=\mathrm{O^{2-}},
\qquad
T=\mathrm{[SiO_3N]^{5-}}.
$$

The complete quantitative reaction is consequently

$$
\mathrm{10LaN+6Ca_3N_2+7Si_3N_4+5SiO_2
\longrightarrow2La_5Ca_9O_2[SiO_3N][Si_{12}N_{24}]}.
$$

It balances La (10), Ca (18), Si (26), O (10), and N (50). Finally, one formula
unit is electrically neutral:

$$
\underbrace{5(+3)+9(+2)}_{+33}
+\underbrace{2(-2)}_{\mathrm{O_2}:-4}
+\underbrace{\bigl[+4+3(-2)+(-3)\bigr]}_{\mathrm{[SiO_3N]^{5-}}:-5}
+\underbrace{\bigl[12(+4)+24(-3)\bigr]}_{\mathrm{[Si_{12}N_{24}]^{24-}}:-24}
=0.
$$

This independently fixes $Q=\mathrm{La}$, $R=\mathrm{Ca}$,
$\alpha=5$, and $\beta=9$.
