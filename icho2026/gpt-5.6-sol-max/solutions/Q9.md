## Detailed solution

### 9.1 Molar mass of beta-CD

Seven glucose molecules contain a total mass of \(7M(\text{glucose})\).
A cyclic seven-unit CD has seven alpha-1,4-glycosidic bonds, so its formation
eliminates seven water molecules, not six.  From the supplied periodic table,

\[
M(\mathrm{H_2O})=2(1.008)+16.00=18.016
\simeq18.02\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
M(\beta\text{-CD})
 &=7M(\text{glucose})-7M(\mathrm{H_2O})\\
 &=7(180.16)-7(18.02)\\
 &=1261.12-126.14\\
 &=1134.98\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}
 \simeq1.1350\times10^3\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is also \(7\) times the anhydroglucose repeat mass,
\(M(\mathrm{C_6H_{10}O_5})=162.14\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}\).

### 9.2 Structure and conformation of K

With seven equivalents of TsCl, the seven exposed primary C6-OH groups
are converted to C6-OTs.  Hydroxide then deprotonates O3.  In each residue,
O3 attacks the primary C6 carbon intramolecularly and displaces tosylate:

\[
\mathrm{C3-O^- + C6-OTs\longrightarrow C3-O-C6 + TsO^-}.
\]

Thus every glucose unit loses the elements of water overall and acquires a
3,6-anhydro ether; O2-H is the one OH bond left in that unit.  As an atom and
functional-group check,

\[
\mathrm{C_{42}H_{70}O_{35}}-7\mathrm{H_2O}
=\mathrm{C_{42}H_{56}O_{28}},
\]

which has one OH and one new ether bridge per residue.

The 3,6 bridge brings the two up substituents at C3 and C5/C6 close together.
They are diaxial in the favorable flipped \(^{1}C_4\) chair, whereas they
would point apart in the diequatorial arrangement.  No carbon stereocentre
is inverted by substitution at primary C6.  An unambiguous per-residue
stereochemical specification is therefore:

- C1-O-glycosidic: down, equatorial (alpha);
- C2-OH: down, axial;
- C3-O-C6 and C5-C6: up, axial, joined to one another;
- C4-O-glycosidic: down, axial.

Repeating this residue seven times around the beta-CD ring gives \(K\).

### 9.3 Ring size and stereocentres of X

Periodate cleaves the vicinal C2--C3 bond of every glucopyranoside.  The two
new carbonyl termini are reduced by sodium borohydride and then acetylated;
both C2 and C3 therefore become achiral `CH2OAc` side chains.

The macrocycle itself remains connected through the other side of every
former pyranose ring.  One residue contributes the following five atoms to
the unique surviving large-ring path:

\[
\mathrm{C1-O_{gly}-C4-C5-O5}.
\]

There are seven residues, so

\[
r_s=7(5)=35.
\]

The original stereocentres at C1, C4, and C5 survive.  C2 and C3 cease to be
stereocentres on becoming methylene groups, and reduction/acetylation creates
no new one.  Hence

\[
s_c=7(3)=21.
\]

### 9.4 Structure of Y

The six equivalents of TBSCl protect the six primary C6-OH groups.  After
the twelve secondary alcohols are deprotonated, one O2 group per residue is
tosylated.  The neighboring O3 alkoxide attacks C2 by intramolecular
\(\mathrm{S_N2}\) displacement.  C2 is inverted, while the C3-O bond is
retained, so the single epoxide oxygen has an up bond to both C2 and C3.
Every residue of \(Y\) consequently has:

```text
          O
         / \
   C2(up)---C3(up)       C6-O-TBS(up)
```

with all alpha-1,4-glycosidic stereochemistry unchanged.  This is the
sixfold 2,3-anhydro intermediate; hot aqueous opening after TBS removal
inverts C3 and furnishes the displayed altro configuration.

The printed molecular formula independently checks the assignment.  Alpha-CD
is \(\mathrm{C_{36}H_{60}O_{30}}\).  Replacing six O-H hydrogens by six
TBS groups adds \(6\mathrm{C_6H_{14}Si}\), and forming six epoxides removes
\(6\mathrm{H_2O}\):

\[
\mathrm{C_{36}H_{60}O_{30}}
+6\mathrm{C_6H_{14}Si}-6\mathrm{H_2O}
=\mathrm{C_{72}H_{132}O_{24}Si_6},
\]

exactly the formula stated for \(Y\).

### 9.5 Structure of L

Beta-CD has \(7(3)=21\) OH groups.  The excess NaH/BnCl benzylates all of
them.  During treatment with two equivalents of DIBAL-H, the first primary
O-benzyl cleavage defines unit 1 and creates C6-OH there.  That protic OH
directs the next primary debenzylation to unit 4.  All five other primary
groups and all fourteen secondary groups remain benzyl ethers.  Therefore

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
\text{unit}&1&2&3&4&5&6&7\\ \hline
\text{C6 group}&\mathrm{CH_2OH}&\mathrm{CH_2OBn}&\mathrm{CH_2OBn}&
\mathrm{CH_2OH}&\mathrm{CH_2OBn}&\mathrm{CH_2OBn}&\mathrm{CH_2OBn}
\end{array}
\]

and every C2-O and C3-O group is OBn.

### 9.6 Number of beta-CD dimer isomers

The two free primary alcohol sites of \(L\), denoted A (unit 1) and B
(unit 4), are constitutionally nonequivalent on the oriented alpha-1,4-linked
seven-membered ring.  Mono-pentenylation can therefore occur at A or B.
Joining two such monomers by metathesis and hydrogenation gives the unordered
site pairs

\[
(A,A),\quad(A,B),\quad(B,B).
\]

Interchanging the two identical CD halves does not create another isomer, and
hydrogenation changes no attachment position.  The number is therefore 3.

### 9.7 Sodium-adduct masses of the degradation products

The two degradation sites are units 1 and 4.  Cutting the seven-unit cycle at
those sites partitions the intact residues into the two arcs

\[
\{2,3\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{5,6,7\}.
\]

The scheme supplies the additive formulas

\[
R=\mathrm{C_{27}H_{28}O_5}
\quad\text{(one intact perbenzylated residue)},
\]

and

\[
D=\mathrm{C_{22}H_{25}O_4}
\quad\text{(one degraded terminal fragment)}.
\]

The other end drawn in the scheme is capped as `AcO`; that complete cap
contributes \(\mathrm{C_2H_3O_2}\).  Thus the neutral products are

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_2D(\mathrm{OAc})
 &: 2\mathrm{C_{27}H_{28}O_5}
   +\mathrm{C_{22}H_{25}O_4}+\mathrm{C_2H_3O_2}
   =\mathrm{C_{78}H_{84}O_{16}},\\
R_3D(\mathrm{OAc})
 &: 3\mathrm{C_{27}H_{28}O_5}
   +\mathrm{C_{22}H_{25}O_4}+\mathrm{C_2H_3O_2}
   =\mathrm{C_{105}H_{112}O_{21}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The even hydrogen counts also check that both assembled formulas are closed,
neutral fragments.  With the required integer masses
\(\mathrm C=12\), \(\mathrm H=1\), \(\mathrm O=16\), and
\(\mathrm{Na}=23\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
m/z\left[\mathrm{C_{78}H_{84}O_{16}+Na}\right]^+
 &=78(12)+84+16(16)+23=\boxed{1299},\\
m/z\left[\mathrm{C_{105}H_{112}O_{21}+Na}\right]^+
 &=105(12)+112+21(16)+23=\boxed{1731}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Both ions have \(z=1\), so no further division is required.

### 9.8 Structures of O-S

Only the primary-rim groups change; the twelve secondary O-benzyl groups stay
as `(OBn)12` throughout.

**N to O.**  Swern oxidation converts unit-1 `CH2OH` to an aldehyde, and the
methylenation converts it to the vinyl group `CH=CH2`.  Units 2-6 remain
`CH2OBn`.

**O to P.**  The unit-1 alkene directs one DIBAL-H debenzylation to its
clockwise 1,2-neighbor, unit 2.  Mesylation followed by primary
\(\mathrm{S_N2}\) displacement with azide changes unit-2 `CH2OH` into
`CH2N3`.

**P to Q.**  One DIBAL-H equivalent reduces unit-2 `CH2N3` to `CH2NH2`.
The resulting protic amine directs the next equivalent to the 1,4-related
unit 5, converting its `CH2OBn` to `CH2OH`.  The two nucleophiles then replace
the two chlorides of \(\mathrm{ClCH_2C(=CH_2)CH_2Cl}\), making the single
bridge

```text
(unit 2)-CH2-NH-CH2-C(=CH2)-CH2-O-CH2-(unit 5).
```

The nitrogen still has one H and is therefore still a protic director.

**Q to R.**  The unit-2 NH director would first select the 1,4-related unit
5, but that primary position is already used in the bridge.  The stated
fallback is therefore the 1,3 position; counting counterclockwise from unit 2
selects unit 6.  DIBAL-H exposes unit-6 `CH2OH`.  Boc protection removes the
directing N-H, and MsCl/NaN3 converts unit 6 to `CH2N3`.  The bridge nitrogen
is now `N(Boc)`.

**R to S.**  Trifluoroacetic acid removes Boc, and NaH/BnI converts the bridge
nitrogen to the nonprotic tertiary `N(Bn)` group.  During the final two-
equivalent DIBAL-H treatment, unit-6 azide is first reduced to `CH2NH2`.
That new protic group is a stronger director than either alkene and directs
debenzylation to the opposite (1,4-related) unit 3.  Unit 3 therefore becomes
`CH2OH`; unit 4 is the one primary `CH2OBn` group left.  These transformations
give exactly the O-S table in the final-answer section and six different
primary-rim environments in \(S\).

### 9.9 Arrangements on a hexadifferentiated alpha-CD

With six different primary-rim functional groups, there are initially \(6!\)
linear orders.  The six rotations of alpha-CD describe the same arrangement,
so division by six fixes one chosen group as the reference position:

\[
N=\frac{6!}{6}=5!=120.
\]

The clockwise and counterclockwise sequences are not identified: a ring of
chiral D-glucopyranosides joined in the directed alpha-(1-to-4) sense has
rotational symmetry but no reflection operation that reverses that sequence.
Hence there is no additional factor of two.
