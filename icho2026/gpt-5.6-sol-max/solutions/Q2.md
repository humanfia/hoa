## Detailed solution

Malonic acid (MA) is \(\mathrm{HOOC{-}CH_2{-}COOH}\), or
\(\mathrm{CH_2(COOH)_2}\). Bromomalonic acid (BMA) in the mechanism is
\(\mathrm{HOOC{-}CHBr{-}COOH}\), or \(\mathrm{CHBr(COOH)_2}\).

### 2.1 Overall reaction

Write the oxidation and reduction half-reactions in acidic solution. Oxidation
of all three carbon atoms of one MA molecule to carbon dioxide gives

\[
\mathrm{CH_2(COOH)_2}+2\,\mathrm{H_2O}
\longrightarrow 3\,\mathrm{CO_2}+8\,\mathrm{H^+}+8\,e^- .
\]

Reduction of bromate to bromide gives

\[
\mathrm{BrO_3^-}+6\,\mathrm{H^+}+6\,e^-
\longrightarrow \mathrm{Br^-}+3\,\mathrm{H_2O}.
\]

Multiplying the first half-reaction by 3 and the second by 4 cancels 24
electrons and 24 protons, giving the boxed equation in the final answers.
The check on that equation is

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc|c}
 & \mathrm C & \mathrm H & \mathrm O & \mathrm{Br} & \text{charge}\\ \hline
\text{left}  &9&12&24&4&-4\\
\text{right} &9&12&24&4&-4
\end{array}
\]

so atoms and charge are both balanced. Cerium is a catalyst and does not
appear in the net equation; potassium and sulfate are spectator ions.

### 2.2 Stationary concentrations of \(\mathrm{HBrO_2}\)

Let

\[
x=[\mathrm{HBrO_2}],\quad
a=[\mathrm{BrO_3^-}],\quad
y=[\mathrm{Br^-}],\quad
h=[\mathrm{H^+}].
\]

#### Process A

For elementary steps (1)-(3), define

\[
v_1=k_1xah,\qquad
v_2=k_2[\mathrm{BrO_2^{\bullet}}]
             [\mathrm{Ce^{3+}}]h,\qquad
v_3=k_3x^2.
\]

The factors of 2 in the material balances follow directly from the displayed
stoichiometry: step (1) forms two \(\mathrm{BrO_2^{\bullet}}\), and
step (3) consumes two \(\mathrm{HBrO_2}\). The steady-state equations are

\[
0=\frac{d[\mathrm{BrO_2^{\bullet}}]}{dt}=2v_1-v_2,
\]

\[
0=\frac{dx}{dt}=-v_1+v_2-2v_3.
\]

The first equation gives \(v_2=2v_1\). Substitution into the second gives
\(v_1=2v_3\), so the nonzero stationary concentration is

\[
x_A=\frac{k_1ah}{2k_3}.
\]

Numerically,

\[
x_A=
\frac{(1.0\times10^4\ \mathrm{M^{-2}s^{-1}})
      (0.060\ \mathrm M)(0.80\ \mathrm M)}
     {2(4.0\times10^7\ \mathrm{M^{-1}s^{-1}})}
=6.0\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm M.
\]

#### Process B

The two elementary rates that respectively consume and form
\(\mathrm{HBrO_2}\) are

\[
v_4=k_4xyh,
\qquad
v_5=k_5ayh^2.
\]

Step (6) contains \(\mathrm{HBrO}\), not \(\mathrm{HBrO_2}\), and therefore
does not enter this material balance. The steady-state condition is

\[
0=\frac{dx}{dt}=v_5-v_4.
\]

For nonzero \(y\), cancellation of \(y h\) gives

\[
x_B=\frac{k_5ah}{k_4}.
\]

Therefore

\[
x_B=
\frac{(2.1\ \mathrm{M^{-3}s^{-1}})
      (0.060\ \mathrm M)(0.80\ \mathrm M)}
     {2.0\times10^9\ \mathrm{M^{-2}s^{-1}}}
=5.04\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm M
\approx5.0\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm M.
\]

Both expressions have units of concentration: the numerator of the Process A
expression has units \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), and that of the Process B expression
has units \(\mathrm{M^{-1}s^{-1}}\), which become molarity after division by
the respective rate constant.

### 2.3 Critical bromide concentration

At the switching threshold, steps (1) and (4) have equal rates:

\[
k_1xah=k_4xy_{\mathrm{critical}}h.
\]

The instantaneous values of \(x\) and \(h\) are common to the two competing
rates, so they cancel. Hence

\[
y_{\mathrm{critical}}
=\frac{k_1a}{k_4}
=\frac{(1.0\times10^4\ \mathrm{M^{-2}s^{-1}})(0.060\ \mathrm M)}
       {2.0\times10^9\ \mathrm{M^{-2}s^{-1}}}
=3.0\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm M.
\]

For \(y>y_{\mathrm{critical}}\), \(v_4>v_1\) and Process B is selected; for
\(y<y_{\mathrm{critical}}\), \(v_1>v_4\) and Process A is selected.

### 2.4 Direction around the phase portrait

On the lower horizontal segment, Process B is active and
\([\mathrm{HBrO_2}]=x_B\). Steps (4) and (5) consume bromide, so the system
moves from the lower-right vertex at \([\mathrm{Br^-}]_{\max}\) to the left,
toward \([\mathrm{Br^-}]_{\mathrm{critical}}\). It then switches from B to A
and moves rapidly to the upper branch. On the upper branch Process A is active
and Process C supplies bromide, so the trajectory moves to the right until A
switches back to B. Thus the bottom edge is traversed right-to-left and the
top edge left-to-right: the complete loop is **clockwise**.

### 2.5 Oscillation period

The problem states that the return to \([\mathrm{Br^-}]_{\max}\) is almost
immediate. The period is consequently the time spent on the slow Process B
leg, during which \(x=x_B\) and bromide falls from
\(y_{\max}=7.0\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm M\) to
\(y_{\mathrm{critical}}\). In this colourless phase cerium is predominantly
\(\mathrm{Ce^{3+}}\), so the bromide-forming step (7), which requires
\(\mathrm{Ce^{4+}}\), is negligible on the slow leg. Its appreciable action is
part of the fast return whose duration the question tells us to neglect.

Each occurrence of step (4) or step (5) consumes one bromide ion. Thus

\[
-\frac{dy}{dt}=v_4+v_5
=\left(k_4x_Bh+k_5ah^2\right)y
=k_{\mathrm{obs}}y.
\]

Using the unrounded value \(x_B=5.04\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm M\), the two
contributions are

\[
k_4x_Bh
=(2.0\times10^9)(5.04\times10^{-11})(0.80)\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}
=0.08064\ \mathrm{s^{-1}},
\]

\[
k_5ah^2
=(2.1)(0.060)(0.80)^2\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}
=0.08064\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}.
\]

The equality of these terms is also the Process B steady-state result
\(v_4=v_5\). Therefore

\[
k_{\mathrm{obs}}=0.16128\ \mathrm{s^{-1}},
\qquad
y(t)=y_{\max}e^{-k_{\mathrm{obs}}t}.
\]

At \(t=\tau\), \(y=y_{\mathrm{critical}}\), so

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau
&=\frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{obs}}}
  \ln\!\left(\frac{y_{\max}}{y_{\mathrm{critical}}}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{0.16128\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}}
  \ln\!\left(\frac{7.0\times10^{-4}}{3.0\times10^{-7}}\right)\\
&=48.084\ \mathrm s\approx48\ \mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
\]

### 2.6 Perturbations of an open BZ system

1. **Add \(\mathrm{Ce^{4+}}\) during Process A: c.** Step (7) consumes the
   added \(\mathrm{Ce^{4+}}\) and produces \(\mathrm{Br^-}\). Raising bromide
   above its critical value makes step (4) dominate step (1), so A switches to
   B.
2. **Add \(\mathrm{Ag^+}\) during Process B: d.** Silver ion removes bromide
   as \(\mathrm{AgBr(s)}\). Once bromide falls below the critical value,
   Process B switches to Process A.
3. **Add \(\mathrm{Br^-}\) during Process B: b.** Bromide must decay through a
   larger interval before reaching the critical concentration, so Process B is
   prolonged.
4. **Add \(\mathrm{Br^-}\) continuously: e.** The feed maintains the bromide
   inhibition of Process A and prevents the threshold crossing needed for
   alternation. The system remains in Process B and the oscillations stop.

### 2.7 Gibbs-energy rate in a closed system

For a spontaneous reaction in a closed system at constant temperature and
pressure,

\[
\frac{dG}{dt}=-T\frac{d_iS}{dt}\leq0,
\]

because internal entropy production \(d_iS/dt\) is nonnegative. Chemical
oscillation can modulate the rate of dissipation, so \(dG/dt\) can oscillate
between values of different negative magnitude, but it cannot cross above
zero. A closed system has finite reactants and ultimately approaches
equilibrium; its oscillations damp out and \(dG/dt\to0^-\). This identifies
the graph described in the final answer. For example, a curve of the form

\[
\frac{dG}{dt}=-A e^{-t/t_d}\bigl(1+\varepsilon\cos\omega t\bigr),
\qquad A>0,\quad t_d>0,\quad 0<\varepsilon<1,
\]

has the required qualitative features; this is only a representative sketch,
not an additional kinetic model for the reaction.
