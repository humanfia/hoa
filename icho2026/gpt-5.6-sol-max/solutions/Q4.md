## Detailed solution

### 4.1

The periodic table gives the mean atomic mass of natural uranium as
\(238.03\). Let \(x\) be the atomic fraction of \(^{235}\mathrm{U}\); then
\(1-x\) is the fraction of \(^{238}\mathrm{U}\). The isotope-weighted mean is

\[
238.03=x(235.04)+(1-x)(238.05).
\]

Therefore

\[
x=\frac{238.05-238.03}{238.05-235.04}
  =6.6445\times10^{-3},
\]

so the atomic percentage is

\[
100x=0.66445\%\simeq\boxed{0.664\%}.
\]

### 4.2

Conservation of mass number \(A\) and atomic number \(Z\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{(a)}\quad
{}^{11}_{5}\mathrm{B}+{}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}
&\rightarrow{}^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N}+{}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n},\\
\text{(b)}\quad
\gamma+{}^{2}_{1}\mathrm{D}
&\rightarrow{}^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H}+{}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n},\\
\text{(c)}\quad
{}^{9}_{4}\mathrm{Be}+{}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}
&\rightarrow{}^{12}_{6}\mathrm{C}+{}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For (a), the two sides have \((A,Z)=(15,7)\); for (b), they have
\((2,1)\); and for (c), they have \((13,6)\). Thus every equation is
balanced in both nucleon number and charge.

### 4.3

The two maxima in the plotted mass-yield distribution occur at
\(A=93\) and \(A=140\). Three neutrons are emitted, and indeed

\[
93+140+3=236=235+1.
\]

The two fission-product elements must be in the same periodic-table group and
their atomic numbers must sum to 92. Rubidium and caesium are both in group 1,
and \(37+55=92\). Hence the main fission channel is

\[
\boxed{{}^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}+{}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n}\rightarrow
{}^{93}_{37}\mathrm{Rb}+{}^{140}_{55}\mathrm{Cs}
+3\,{}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n}}.
\]

This equation conserves both mass number (236 on each side) and atomic number
(92 on each side).

### 4.4

The incident neutron and the three outgoing free neutrons have no binding
energy in this calculation. The initial bound nucleus therefore contains 235
nucleons, whereas the two product nuclei contain \(93+140=233\) bound
nucleons. The increase in total binding energy is the energy released:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E
&=B_{\mathrm{products}}-B_{\mathrm{initial}}\\
&=(93+140)(8.45\ \mathrm{MeV\,nucleon^{-1}})
  -235(7.59\ \mathrm{MeV\,nucleon^{-1}})\\
&=1968.85\ \mathrm{MeV}-1783.65\ \mathrm{MeV}\\
&=\boxed{185.2\ \mathrm{MeV}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(185.2\ \mathrm{MeV}\) is released; equivalently, the energy of the
nuclear system changes by \(-185.2\ \mathrm{MeV}\).

### 4.5

Successive logarithmic decrements add, so after \(n_c\) collisions

\[
\ln\!\left(\frac{E_0}{E_f}\right)=n_c\xi.
\]

Using a common energy unit, \(2\ \mathrm{MeV}=2.0\times10^6\ \mathrm{eV}\).
Thus

\[
n_c=\frac{\ln[(2.0\times10^6\ \mathrm{eV})/(0.012\ \mathrm{eV})]}
{0.948}
=19.9699\simeq\boxed{20\ \text{collisions}}.
\]

### 4.6

Because every species is gaseous, the balanced combustion reaction is

\[
\mathrm{CH_4(g)+2O_2(g)\rightarrow CO_2(g)+2H_2O(g)}.
\]

Using \(\Delta_fH_{298}^{\circ}(\mathrm{O_2,g})=0\) for elemental oxygen in
its standard state,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_rH_{298}^{\circ}
&=\sum\nu\Delta_fH_{298}^{\circ}(\text{products})
  -\sum\nu\Delta_fH_{298}^{\circ}(\text{reactants})\\
&=\left[-393.5+2(-241.8)\right]
  -\left[-74.8+2(0)\right]\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\\
&=\boxed{-802.3\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The equation has one C, four H, and four O atoms on each side.

### 4.7

For the combustion reaction, the reaction heat-capacity change is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_rC_P
&=\left[C_P(\mathrm{CO_2})+2C_P(\mathrm{H_2O})\right]
 -\left[C_P(\mathrm{CH_4})+2C_P(\mathrm{O_2})\right]\\
&=[37+2(34)]-[35+2(29)]\\
&=12\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Taking these heat capacities as constant and using the supplied temperature
correction,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_rH_{2000}
&=\Delta_rH_{298}^{\circ}+\Delta_rC_P(2000-298)\\
&=-802.3\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}
 +(0.012\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}})(1702\ \mathrm{K})\\
&=-781.876\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\\
&\simeq\boxed{-782\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The positive \(\Delta_rC_P\) makes the combustion enthalpy less negative at
the higher temperature, as the result shows.

### 4.8

The gas is ideal. Its amount flowing per day, evaluated from the stated
pre-combustion pressure, volume, and temperature, is

\[
\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{day}}
&=\frac{PV}{RT}\\
&=\frac{(101325\ \mathrm{Pa})(2.2\times10^5\ \mathrm{m^3\,day^{-1}})}
{(8.314\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}})(298\ \mathrm{K})}\\
&=8.9973\times10^6\ \mathrm{mol\,day^{-1}},
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(1\ \mathrm{Pa\,m^3}=1\ \mathrm{J}\). Complete combustion consumes
one mole of methane per mole of reaction. Using the magnitude of the
combustion enthalpy at \(2000\ \mathrm{K}\), the released energy is

\[
\begin{aligned}
E
&=n_{\mathrm{day}}\left|\Delta_rH_{2000}\right|\\
&=(8.9973\times10^6\ \mathrm{mol\,day^{-1}})
  (781.876\times10^3\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}})\\
&=7.0348\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{J\,day^{-1}}\\
&\simeq\boxed{7.0\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{J\,day^{-1}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The quoted \(E\) is the positive amount released; the corresponding enthalpy
change of the reacting stream is negative.

### 4.9

The TNT-equivalent energy of the explosion is

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{explosion}}
&=(30\ \mathrm{kiloton})
  \left(10^3\ \frac{\mathrm{ton}}{\mathrm{kiloton}}\right)
  \left(4.184\times10^9\ \frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{ton}}\right)\\
&=1.2552\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
\]

From 4.4, the energy per fission in joules is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fission}}
&=(185.2\ \mathrm{MeV})
  \left(10^6\ \frac{\mathrm{eV}}{\mathrm{MeV}}\right)
  \left(1.602\times10^{-19}\ \frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{eV}}\right)\\
&=2.966904\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Assuming the stated TNT-equivalent energy all comes from these fissions,

\[
TN=\frac{E_{\mathrm{explosion}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fission}}}
=\frac{1.2552\times10^{14}}{2.966904\times10^{-11}}
=4.2307\times10^{24}
\simeq\boxed{4.2\times10^{24}\ \text{fissions}}.
\]

Only 33% of the initial \(^{235}\mathrm{U}\) atoms fissioned. The initial mass
of \(^{235}\mathrm{U}\), followed by the correction for 90% enrichment, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
m
&=\frac{1}{0.90}
  \left[\frac{TN}{0.33N_A}
  (235.04\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}})
  \left(\frac{1\ \mathrm{kg}}{1000\ \mathrm{g}}\right)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{0.90}
  \left[\frac{4.2307\times10^{24}}
  {(0.33)(6.022\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{mol^{-1}})}
  (235.04\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}})
  \left(\frac{1\ \mathrm{kg}}{1000\ \mathrm{g}}\right)\right]\\
&=5.5597\ \mathrm{kg}
\simeq\boxed{5.6\ \mathrm{kg}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the fissioned \(^{235}\mathrm{U}\) itself has mass \(1.651\ \mathrm{kg}\);
dividing first by \(0.33\) gives \(5.004\ \mathrm{kg}\) of initial
\(^{235}\mathrm{U}\), and dividing by \(0.90\) gives the total enriched-
uranium mass above.
