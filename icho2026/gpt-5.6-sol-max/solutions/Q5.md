## Detailed solution

### 5.1 Counting fragment connections

A type a fragment has one free connection, b has two, c has three, and d has
one. The stated collection therefore has

    n(1) + 2(2) + 3(3) + 4(1) = n + 17

free connections. Every bond made between fragments consumes two connections,
so n + 17 must be even. Thus n is odd and choice **(b)** is correct.

There is a stronger check. There are n + 9 fragments. A connected acyclic
molecule made from them has n + 8 interfragment bonds, while pairing all free
connections gives (n + 17)/2 bonds. Hence

    (n + 17)/2 = n + 8
    n = 1

That one hydrogen terminates the unesterified oxygen on the central glycerol.

### 5.2 PL1 and its stabilized monoanion

The four d fragments acylate four glycerol oxygens. Each b fragment connects
an outer, diacylated glycerol to one terminal oxygen of the third glycerol.
The full connectivity is

    RCOOCH2-C*_L(H)(OCOR)-CH2-O-P(=O)(OH)-O-
        CH2-CH(OH)-CH2-O-P(=O)(OH)-O-
        CH2-C*_R(H)(OCOR)-CH2-OCOR

Here OCOR means O-C(=O)-R, not a direct carbon-carbon bond to R. At each
starred carbon the four groups are H, O-C(=O)-R, CH2-O-C(=O)-R, and
CH2-O-P(...), so it is stereogenic. Taking both centres as R gives the R,R
enantiomer; reflection gives S,S. The two arms at the middle CH(OH) are
identical in the R,R structure, so that middle carbon is not an additional
stereocentre.

After the first acid dissociation, one phosphate is anionic and the other
still bears its acidic proton. Folding the central headgroup lets the neutral
phosphate OH donate a hydrogen bond to the central glycerol oxygen, while the
central OH donates a charge-assisted hydrogen bond to the anionic phosphate
oxygen:

    P_L-O-H ... O_c-H ... O(-)-P_R

The corresponding form with L and R exchanged has the same energy, so the
proton and charge are stabilized over two equivalent arrangements. A second
deprotonation both breaks this cooperative proton trap and places two negative
phosphate groups close together. It is therefore much less favourable, which
accounts for pKa2 being much greater than pKa1.

### 5.3 Formula of the fatty acid

Let the fatty acid have x carbon atoms and k carbon-carbon double bonds. It is
an acyclic monocarboxylic acid, so its formula is

    Cx H(2x - 2k) O2

Reductive ozonolysis of a linear chain with k double bonds cuts it into k + 1
pieces. The carboxyl-end and methyl-end products each occur once and are
necessarily different. Since the three different products are equimolar,
there is one internal product as well. Therefore k + 1 = 3, so k = 2.

The balanced hydrolysis in the question gives the non-ionised PL1 formula in
terms of one fatty acid:

    PL1 + 8 H2O ->
        4 Cx H(2x - 2k) O2 + 2 H3PO4 + 3 C3H8O3

    PL1 = C(4x + 9) H(8x - 8k + 14) O17 P2

PL1 is connected and acyclic. It contains

    N = (4x + 9) + (8x - 8k + 14) + 17 + 2
      = 12x - 8k + 42 atoms

and hence N - 1 = 12x - 8k + 41 sigma bonds. Its pi bonds are the 4k
fatty-chain C=C bonds, four ester C=O bonds, and two P=O bonds:

    N(pi) = 4k + 4 + 2 = 4k + 6

Thus the given total bond count is

    (12x - 8k + 41) + (4k + 6) = 12x - 4k + 47 = 255

With k = 2,

    12x - 8 + 47 = 255
    x = 18
    2x - 2k = 32

Therefore

    RCOOH = C18H32O2

As a direct check, PL1 is C81H142O17P2. Its 242 atoms give 241 sigma
bonds, and 8 + 4 + 2 = 14 pi bonds, for 241 + 14 = 255.

### 5.4 Formula of X

Using the atomic masses in the booklet,

    M(C18H32O2)
      = 18(12.01) + 32(1.008) + 2(16.00)
      = 280.436 g mol^-1

The observed iodine uptake is

    n(acid) = 100 g / (280.436 g mol^-1)
            = 0.356588 mol

    n(I2) = 181.0 g / (253.8 g mol^-1)
          = 0.713160 mol
          = 2.0000 n(acid)

This independently confirms two alkene addition sites. The analogous reagent
X therefore adds twice per fatty-acid molecule. With one iodine atom in each
molecule of the iodine-containing addition reagent, the adduct contains
2(126.9) = 253.8 g mol^-1 of iodine. Its molar mass is

    M(adduct) = 253.8 / 0.3657
              = 694.011 g mol^-1

so

    M(X) = (694.011 - 280.436)/2
         = 206.788 g mol^-1

After subtracting iodine,

    206.788 - 126.9 = 79.888 g mol^-1

which matches bromine, 79.90 g mol^-1. Hence

    X = IBr

Check:

    iodine mass fraction
      = 2(126.9) / [280.436 + 2(126.9 + 79.90)]
      = 0.365687
      = 36.5687%, which rounds to 36.57%.

### 5.5 Lipid phase after protonation

Protonation removes the two headgroup charges. It therefore decreases
electrostatic repulsion and hydration at the headgroups, reducing their
effective cross-sectional area while leaving tail volume and length nearly
unchanged. The lipid packing parameter consequently increases. The molecules
become effectively inverted-cone-shaped and favour negative interfacial
curvature. The resulting phase is **(d), inverse hexagonal**.

### 5.6 Structures of PL2 and PL3

#### PL2

The PL1 and PL2 hydrolyses use the same amount of water and give the same four
fatty acids and two phosphoric acids. PL2 gives two glycerols plus Z, whereas
PL1 gives three glycerols. Thus the structural change is replacement of the
central glycerol linker by Z.

Glycerol has total bond order

    B(C3H8O3) = [3(4) + 8(1) + 3(2)]/2 = 13

Since PL2 has one fewer sigma-plus-pi bond than PL1, B(Z) = 13 - 1 = 12.
Hydrolysis must regenerate two OH groups on Z, one from each phosphate ester,
so Z is a diol. It does not hydrogenate, so use the saturated acyclic diol
formula Cq H(2q + 2) O2. Then

    B(Z) = [4q + (2q + 2) + 2(2)]/2
         = 3q + 3
         = 12

    q = 3

Thus Z is C3H8O2. Of its diol connectivities, propane-1,3-diol has equivalent
ends and produces the stated increase in symmetry:

    Z = HO-CH2-CH2-CH2-OH

Connecting one identically configured 1,2-diacylglycerol arm through each
phosphate gives the PL2 structure in the final answer. Choosing both outer
centres R gives a chiral R,R molecule with a symmetric central linker; its
mirror image is S,S. As a formula and bond-count check, neutral PL2 is

    C81H142O16P2

    total bond order = [81(4) + 142 + 16(2) + 2(5)]/2
                     = 254

sigma-plus-pi bonds, exactly as stated.

#### PL3

The hydrolysis products require a glycerol bearing two fatty-acid esters and a
phosphate diester whose other organic group is ethyl. At physiological pH the
one non-esterified phosphate OH has dissociated, giving

    RCOOCH2-C*(H)(OCOR)-CH2-O-P(=O)(O-)-O-CH2CH3

The acyl groups must occupy adjacent glycerol positions, leaving a terminal
glycerol oxygen for phosphate. The starred carbon then has four different
substituents and may be R or S; these are the stated enantiomeric pair. Putting
the phosphate at the middle carbon and equal acyl groups at both ends would
instead make the molecule achiral.

There are two carboxyl-ester and two phosphate-ester hydrolyses, so m = 4. A
balance check gives neutral PL3 as C41H73O8P. Loss of its phosphate proton at
physiological pH gives C41H72O8P(-), consistent with the required charge.
