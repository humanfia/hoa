## Detailed solution

### 1.1 Identification of X and Y

Compounds 6 and 3 both have formula $\mathrm{C_{10}H_{18}O}$. Compound 6 is
the acyclic dienol linalool. In acid it can undergo cationic intramolecular
cyclization: its two carbon-carbon double bonds are converted into the two
rings of the bicyclic ether 1,8-cineole. Thus the net reaction is an
isomerization, with neither loss nor gain of atoms:

$$
\underset{X\;(6),\ \text{linalool}}{\mathrm{C_{10}H_{18}O}}
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{H^+}}
\underset{Y\;(3),\ \text{1,8-cineole}}{\mathrm{C_{10}H_{18}O}}.
$$

The structure drawn for 1,8-cineole has a mirror plane passing through the
ether oxygen and the two bridgehead positions, interchanging the two sides of
the carbon framework. This supplies the stated plane of symmetry.

### 1.2 Identification of Z, A, and B

Azulene is the familiar blue, non-alternant aromatic system made by fusing a
five-membered ring to a seven-membered ring. Its molecular plane is one mirror
plane; a second mirror plane is perpendicular to it and bisects the fused
framework. On heating, azulene rearranges to the more stable isomer
naphthalene. Naphthalene has three mutually perpendicular mirror planes: its
molecular plane and two planes perpendicular to that plane.

The blue candidate in the table derived from the azulene skeleton is compound
7, chamazulene. Its table formula also gives an independent mass check. The
nominal mass of $\mathrm{C_{14}H_{16}}$ is

$$
14(12)+16(1)=184.
$$

Monochlorination replaces one H by chlorine, so the ions containing
$^{35}\mathrm{Cl}$ and $^{37}\mathrm{Cl}$ have nominal masses

$$
184-1+35=218,\qquad 184-1+37=220.
$$

The approximately 3:1 natural abundance ratio of these two chlorine isotopes
therefore gives exactly the reported 218/220 pair. Hence

$$
Z=\text{chamazulene},\qquad A=\text{azulene},\qquad
B=\text{naphthalene}.
$$

Structurally, $Z$ is the azulene 5-7 fused ring system bearing methyl groups
at positions 1 and 4 and an ethyl group at position 7; $B$ is the 6-6 fused
ring system stated in the final answers.

### 1.3 Carbon count and identification of W

Let $p_{12}=0.989$ and $p_{13}=0.011$. With $n$ carbon atoms and the stated
monoisotopic treatment of all other elements, the molecular-ion peak contains
only $^{12}\mathrm C$, whereas the $M+1$ peak contains exactly one
$^{13}\mathrm C$:

$$
I_M\mathrel{\propto}(p_{12})^n,
\qquad
I_{M+1}\mathrel{\propto}n p_{13}(p_{12})^{n-1}.
$$

Using $I_M:I_{M+1}=9:1$,

$$
9=\frac{I_M}{I_{M+1}}
=\frac{0.989}{n(0.011)},
\qquad
n=\frac{0.989}{9(0.011)}=9.9899\simeq\boxed{10}.
$$

The colour test with aqueous $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ points to a phenolic OH
group. Of the phenolic entries, compound 1 has 11 carbon atoms but compound 5
has 10. Therefore $W$ is compound 5, eugenol, $\mathrm{C_{10}H_{12}O_2}$.

### 1.4 Identification of Q, C, and D

The fluoride used industrially in aluminium production is cryolite,
$\mathrm{Na_3AlF_6}$. The supplied percentages quantitatively verify both the
formula and the identity of $Q$. Using the booklet atomic masses,

$$
M(\mathrm{Na_3AlF_6})
=3(22.99)+26.98+6(19.00)
=209.95\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
w(\mathrm{Na})=\frac{3(22.99)}{209.95}(100\%)=32.85\%,
$$

$$
w(\mathrm{Al})=\frac{26.98}{209.95}(100\%)=12.85\%.
$$

Thus $Q=\mathrm{Al}$ and $D=\mathrm{Na_3AlF_6}$. The anhydrous precursor is
$C=\mathrm{AlF_3}$, consistent with the balanced fluoride-complex formation

$$
\mathrm{AlF_3+3NaF\longrightarrow Na_3AlF_6}.
$$

Now $M(\mathrm{AlF_3})=26.98+3(19.00)=83.98\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}$ and
$M(\mathrm{H_2O})=18.016\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}$. If the water mass fraction
is 0.3916, then

$$
0.3916=\frac{x(18.016)}{83.98+x(18.016)},
$$

$$
x=\frac{0.3916(83.98)}{(1-0.3916)(18.016)}=3.0003\simeq 3.
$$

The precipitate is therefore $\mathrm{AlF_3\mathbin{\cdot}3H_2O}$; its
calculated water content is 39.157%, which rounds to the stated 39.16%.

### 1.5 Structures of E, F, and G

A sixfold-symmetric hydrocarbon consistent with the hydrogen percentage is
hexamethylbenzene, $E=\mathrm{C_6(CH_3)_6}=\mathrm{C_{12}H_{18}}$. Its
hydrogen mass fraction is

$$
\frac{18(1.008)}{12(12.01)+18(1.008)}(100\%)
=11.1818\%\simeq 11.18\%.
$$

Strong oxidation converts every benzylic methyl group into a carboxyl group,
without changing the sixfold substitution pattern. Hence

$$
\underset{E}{\mathrm{C_6(CH_3)_6}}
\xrightarrow[\mathrm{HNO_3}]{\mathrm{KMnO_4}}
\underset{F}{\mathrm{C_6(COOH)_6}},
$$

where $F$ is mellitic acid. Its conjugate anion in the stone is the
hexaanion $\mathrm{[C_6(COO)_6]^{6-}}$.

$\mathrm{P_2O_5}$ removes one water molecule from each of three adjacent
carboxyl-group pairs. Thus

$$
\underset{F}{\mathrm{C_{12}H_6O_{12}}}
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{P_2O_5}}
\underset{G}{\mathrm{C_{12}O_9}}+3\mathrm{H_2O}.
$$

The three identical cyclic anhydrides are arranged at 120 degrees around the
central ring, so $G$ has a threefold axis. It is binary (C and O only), and its
oxygen percentage checks the formula:

$$
w(\mathrm O)=
\frac{9(16.00)}{12(12.01)+9(16.00)}(100\%)
=49.979\%\simeq49.98\%.
$$

### 1.6 Formulae of the stone and H

Charge balance between $\mathrm{Al^{3+}}$ from 1.4 and the mellitate
hexaanion from 1.5 requires two aluminium ions per mellitate ion. The
anhydrous salt is therefore

$$
\mathrm{Al_2[C_6(COO)_6]}=\mathrm{Al_2C_{12}O_{12}},
$$

with molar mass

$$
M_{\rm anhydrous}=2(26.98)+12(12.01)+12(16.00)
=390.08\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}.
$$

The first TGA step loses $10.00-5.75=4.25\ \mathrm g$ of water and leaves
$5.75\ \mathrm g$ of anhydrous aluminium mellitate. Therefore the hydration
number is

$$
x=\frac{4.25\ \mathrm g/(18.016\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}})}
{5.75\ \mathrm g/(390.08\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}})}
=16.00.
$$

Thus the stone is
$\mathrm{Al_2[C_6(COO)_6]\mathbin{\cdot}16H_2O}$. Its calculated water mass
fraction is

$$
\frac{16(18.016)}{390.08+16(18.016)}(100\%)=42.495\%,
$$

matching the observed loss of 42.50%.

In open air the organic mellitate carbon is oxidized to $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and
the nonvolatile aluminium residue is $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$. The atom-balanced
anhydrous decomposition can be written

$$
2\mathrm{Al_2C_{12}O_{12}}+15\mathrm{O_2}
\longrightarrow 2\mathrm{Al_2O_3}+24\mathrm{CO_2}.
$$

Since $M(\mathrm{Al_2O_3})=101.96\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}$, the predicted
residue from the 5.75 g anhydrous plateau is

$$
m(H)=5.75\ \mathrm g\left(\frac{101.96}{390.08}\right)
=1.5028\ \mathrm g\simeq1.50\ \mathrm g,
$$

in agreement with the final plateau. Hence $H=\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$.
