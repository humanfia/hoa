### 3.4

The repeat-unit boundary convention used below is the one in the question:
each repeat unit contains one black trigonal core and one half of each of its
three red linear linkers. A dashed boundary passes through the center of each
shared red phenylene ring, giving three dashed edges per repeat unit.

- **COF-3:** imine-linked. A complete edge between two black
  1,3,5-benzenetriyl cores, `T`, is
  `T-CH=N-C6H2(OH)2-N=CH-T`; on the red ring the N substituents are
  at positions 1 and 4 and the OH groups at positions 2 and 5.
- **COF-4:** the two ortho-hydroxy imines on that complete red linker
  are oxidatively closed into two benzoxazole rings. In an atom-explicit
  description, the aldehyde-derived carbon `X_L` is bonded to `T`, double
  bonded to `N1`, and single bonded to `O2`; `N1` and `O2` are bonded to
  adjacent red-ring carbons C1 and C2. The other end is identical with
  `X_R`, `N4`, `O5`, C4, and C5. Thus each five-membered ring is
  `X-N-C(red)-C(red)-O`, with `X-T` at its 2-position.
- **COF-5:** enol-imine-linked. A complete edge is
  `P-CH=N-p-C6H4-N=CH-P`, where `P` is the
  1,3,5-triformylphloroglucinol-derived aromatic core and retains its three
  phenolic OH groups.
- **COF-6:** the keto-enamine tautomer. The core is a
  cyclohexane-1,3,5-trione unit; its positions 2, 4, and 6 each have an
  exocyclic `=CH-NH-` group. A complete edge is therefore
  `K_i=C(H)-NH-p-C6H4-NH-C(H)=K_j`, where `K_i` and `K_j` are ring
  carbons of neighboring triketone cores. It has C=C and C-NH bonds, but
  no C=N bond.

The corresponding formula checks per topological repeat are
`C18H12N3O3` (COF-3), `C18H6N3O3` (COF-4), and
`C18H12N3O3` for both COF-5 and COF-6.

### 3.5

Oxidation changes every imine into an amide, and ozonolysis of every internal

\[

`C27H14F4N2O3`; the full macrocycle has six outward aldehyde groups.

## Detailed solution

### 3.1 Empirical formula and carbon content of COF-1

The two monomers in the COF-1 drawing are A2 and B3. Their molecular
formulae are

\[
\mathrm{A2=C_6H_8B_2O_4},\qquad
\mathrm{B3=C_{18}H_{12}O_6}.
\]

One B3 node has three catechol sites. It is attached to three A2 linkers,
but every A2 linker is shared by two B3 nodes. Thus one topological repeat
contains

\[
1\mathrm{\ B3}+\frac32\mathrm{\ A2}.
\]

Each of the three boronate-ester rings eliminates two water molecules, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{C_{18}H_{12}O_6}
+\frac32\mathrm{C_6H_8B_2O_4}
-6\mathrm{H_2O}
&=\mathrm{C_{27}H_{12}B_3O_6} \\
&=3(\mathrm{C_9H_4BO_2}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence the empirical formula is `C9H4BO2`. Using the atomic masses supplied
in the general booklet,

\[
M(\mathrm{C_9H_4BO_2})
=9(12.01)+4(1.008)+10.81+2(16.00)
=154.932\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}.
\]

Therefore

\[
w(\mathrm C)=
\frac{9(12.01)}{154.932}(100\%)
=69.766\%\approx\boxed{69.77\%}.
\]

### 3.2 Internal diameter of COF-2

The vertices of the ideal topological hexagon are the centers of boroxine
rings. Along one straight side, the distance from one boroxine center to the
next contains, in order:

- one boroxine-center-to-B radius, one B-C bond, and one arene radius;
- the same three distances in reverse at the other end.

For a regular `B3O3` ring its center-to-vertex radius equals its side,
the B-O bond length. Likewise, the distance from the center of a regular
benzene ring to a substituted carbon is one arene C-C bond length. Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
a
&=2\left(r_{\mathrm{B-O}}+r_{\mathrm{B-C}}+r_{\mathrm{C-C}}\right)\\
&=2(1.38+1.56+1.39)\ \mathrm{\mathring A}\\
&=8.66\ \mathrm{\mathring A}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The stated hexagon relation gives

\[
d=\sqrt3a=\sqrt3(8.66)=14.9996\ \mathrm{\mathring A}
\approx\boxed{15.0\ \mathrm{\mathring A}}.
\]

### 3.3 Monomer combinations and topology

First classify the monomers by the geometry of their reactive sites:

| Geometry and connectivity | Monomers |
|---|---|
| linear, 2-connected | A2, B2, C1, D4 |
| trigonal planar, 3-connected | A1, B3, D2, E1 |
| square planar, 4-connected | B1, C3 |
| bent/rhombic planar, 4-connected | A3, C2 |
| tetrahedral, 4-connected | A4, D3 |
| trigonal-planar, 6-connected | D1 |

Only complementary functional classes can react under the conditions in the
question: A with B (boronate ester), C with D (imine), or E with D (C=C).
The topology requirements are then:

| Topology | Required node pair |
|---|---|
| Tetragonal 1 | square-planar 4-connected + linear 2-connected |
| Tetragonal 2 | square-planar 4-connected + tetrahedral 4-connected |
| Hexagonal 1 | trigonal 3-connected + linear 2-connected |
| Hexagonal 2 | trigonal 3-connected + trigonal 3-connected |
| Trigonal | 6-connected + linear 2-connected |
| Kagome | bent/rhombic 4-connected + linear 2-connected |
| Tetrahedral | tetrahedral 4-connected + linear 2-connected |

Applying both filters gives the two answer rows printed in the Final answers.
For Hexagonal 1 there are actually three unused possibilities after the given
`A2 + B3`: `A1 + B2`, `C1 + D2`, and `E1 + D4`. The table has only two
blank cells in that column, so any two of these three are sufficient. For
Hexagonal 2, Trigonal, and Kagome only one additional allowed combination
exists; the remaining cell must consequently be `XXX`.

### 3.4 Repeat units of COFs 3-6

#### Boundary placement

All four networks have the Hexagonal 1 construction: one black trigonal core
is joined through three red linear linkers. Center a repeat on one black core
and draw a dashed line through the inversion center of each of the three red
phenylene linkers. Each red linker is thereby shared equally between two
neighboring repeats. This is exactly the three-boundary convention shown for
COF-1 in the booklet.

#### COF-3

The black monomer is 1,3,5-benzenetricarbaldehyde. The red monomer is a
1,4-diaminobenzene bearing OH groups ortho to the two amines. Condensation at
both ends of a red linker gives

\[
\mathrm{T-CH=N-C_6H_2(OH)_2-N=CH-T}.
\]

Here the red-ring sequence is C1(N), C2(OH), C3(H), C4(N), C5(OH),
C6(H). The dashed repeat boundary passes through the center of this ring.

The formula per repeat is a useful atom-balance check:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{C_9H_6O_3}
+\frac32\mathrm{C_6H_8N_2O_2}
-3\mathrm{H_2O}\\
&\hspace{35mm}=\mathrm{C_{18}H_{12}N_3O_3}.
\end{aligned}
\]

#### COF-4

Every COF-3 imine has an ortho phenolic OH. Oxidative cyclization removes the
H on that OH and the H on the adjacent imine carbon, and closes a C-O bond.
For one end let `X` be the aldehyde-derived carbon and let C1-C2 be adjacent
carbons of the red ring. The connectivity is

\[
\mathrm{T-X,\quad X=N-C1(red),\quad X-O-C2(red)},
\]

where C1-C2 is also a bond. The closed five-membered ring is therefore
`X-N-C1-C2-O`, i.e. a 2-substituted benzoxazole. The same closure at red-ring
positions 4 and 5 makes the second benzoxazole at the other end of the linker.

There are three such closures per topological repeat. Each loses `H2`, so

\[
\mathrm{C_{18}H_{12}N_3O_3}-3\mathrm{H_2}
=\mathrm{C_{18}H_6N_3O_3},
\]

which is exactly the stated loss of six H atoms. The remaining C=N bonds are
the endocyclic benzoxazole bonds, not imines.

#### COF-5

Direct condensation of the triformylphloroglucinol derivative with
`p`-phenylenediamine first gives the enol-imine edge

\[
\mathrm{P-CH=N-p\!\!-C_6H_4-N=CH-P}.
\]

The black core `P` is still aromatic and bears its three alternating OH
groups. As before, a dashed boundary bisects each shared red `p`-phenylene.
The repeat formula is

\[
\mathrm{C_9H_6O_6}
+\frac32\mathrm{C_6H_8N_2}
-3\mathrm{H_2O}
=\mathrm{C_{18}H_{12}N_3O_3}.
\]

#### COF-6

At each arm of COF-5, an OH proton transfers to the imine N while the double
bonds shift. Each former C-OH becomes C=O and each former C=N becomes
C-NH; the aldehyde-derived carbon forms a C=C bond to the core. After all
three transfers, the black core is a cyclohexane-1,3,5-trione whose C2, C4,
and C6 atoms carry exocyclic `=CH-NH-` groups. A complete red edge is

\[
\mathrm{K_i=C(H)-NH-p\!\!-C_6H_4-NH-C(H)=K_j}.
\]

This keto-enamine structure contains no imine stretch. Tautomerization moves
only H atoms and pi bonds, so COF-6 retains the COF-5 formula
`C18H12N3O3`.

### 3.5 Structure of macrocycle X

In COF-7, each D4-derived imine is oxidized by chlorite to an amide:

\[
\mathrm{Ar-N=CH-C_6F_4-CH=N-Ar}
\longrightarrow
\mathrm{Ar-NH-C(O)-C_6F_4-C(O)-NH-Ar}.
\]

C2 contains an internal stilbene bond. Reductive ozonolysis cleaves it as

\[
\mathrm{Ar-CH=CH-Ar\ \xrightarrow[Me_2S]{O_3}\ 2\ Ar-CHO}.
\]

Thus each C2 is split into two identical 120-degree units

\[
V=\mathrm{OHC-C_6H_3[-p\!\!-C_6H_4-NH-]_2}.
\]

At a Kagome vertex the two arms within one such half make the 120-degree
turn around a hexagonal pore. Cleaving every C2 vertex therefore releases the
hexagon, not one of the triangular pores. Six `V` corners alternate with six
tetrafluoroterephthaloyl edges

\[
L=\mathrm{-C(O)-C_6F_4-C(O)-}.
\]

Label the corners cyclically. The exact connectivity is

\[
\mathrm{N_i^{(R)}-C(O)-C_6F_4-C(O)-N_{i+1}^{(L)}},
\quad i=1,\ldots,6,
\]

where `N_i^(L)` and `N_i^(R)` are the terminal N atoms on the two
`p-C6H4-NH-` arms already included in `V_i`, and `V_7 = V_1`. Hence `X`
is `cyclo-[V-L]6`; its smallest repeat is one `V-L` pair. Atom counting gives
`V-L = C27H14F4N2O3`, while the complete ring contains six aldehydes
produced by ozonolysis.

### 3.6 Stacking energies of COF-8

One pore/repeat of the alternating trigonal network contains one E1-derived
benzene node and one D2-derived node. D2 contributes one triazine and three
peripheral benzene rings. Thus one repeat has

\[
4\ \text{benzene rings}+1\ \text{triazine ring}.
\]

In AA stacking all like rings are directly eclipsed, so the first energy
column applies:

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{AA}}
&=4E_{\mathrm{b-b}}+E_{\mathrm{t-t}}\\
&=4(-7.9)+(-6.7)\\
&=\boxed{-38.3\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

As a check, using the shifted-pair column for AA' gives

\[
4(-12.6)+(-16.7)=-67.1\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}},
\]

exactly the value supplied in the question.

In AB stacking, one sublattice of the upper honeycomb lies over the other
sublattice of the lower honeycomb. Consequently there is one benzene-triazine
overlap per repeat; the other aromatic units lie over pores. For direct AB,

\[
E_{\mathrm{AB}}=E_{\mathrm{b-t}}
=\boxed{-49.8\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}}.
\]

For AB', that benzene-triazine pair has the second, slightly slipped geometry
shown in the energy table:

\[
E_{\mathrm{AB'}}=E'_{\mathrm{b-t}}
=\boxed{-55.6\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}}.
\]

### 3.7 Uranyl uptake by COF-9

#### Equilibrium absorption capacity

The solution volume is `200.0 mL = 0.2000 dm3`. The mass removed from
solution is

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_{\mathrm{ads}}
&=(c_0-c_e)V\\
&=(19.90-9.225)\ \mathrm{mg\ dm^{-3}}
  (0.2000\ \mathrm{dm^3})\\
&=2.135\ \mathrm{mg}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The COF mass is `5.000 mg = 0.005000 g`, hence

\[
q_e=\frac{m_{\mathrm{ads}}}{m_{\mathrm{COF}}}
=\frac{2.135\ \mathrm{mg}}{0.005000\ \mathrm g}
=\boxed{427.0\ \mathrm{mg\ g^{-1}}}.
\]

#### Ions per pore

The E1 and D2 molecular formulae are

\[
\mathrm{E1=C_{12}H_9N_3},\qquad
\mathrm{D2=C_{24}H_{15}N_3O_3}.
\]

One E1 and one D2 make three C=C links and lose three waters, so one
COF-8 pore has formula

\[
\mathrm{C_{12}H_9N_3+C_{24}H_{15}N_3O_3-3H_2O
=C_{36}H_{18}N_6}.
\]

Each pore has three nitriles. Addition of hydroxylamine changes every
`-C#N` to amidoxime, `-C(=N-OH)-NH2`, adding one N, three H, and one O per
nitrile. Therefore the COF-9 formula per pore is

\[
\mathrm{C_{36}H_{18}N_6+3(NH_3O)
=C_{36}H_{27}N_9O_3}.
\]

In the honeycomb boundary, three E1 and three D2 vertices surround a pore,
but each vertex is shared by three pores. The effective content is therefore
one E1 plus one D2 per pore, as used above.

Its molar mass is

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{\mathrm{COF-9,pore}}
&=36(12.01)+27(1.008)+9(14.01)+3(16.00)\\
&=633.666\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

From the supplied periodic table,

\[
M(\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}})=238.0+2(16.00)=270.0\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}.
\]

Avogadro's constant cancels when the number of ions is divided by the number
of pores:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{N(\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}})}{N(\text{pores})}
&=\frac{(0.002135\ \mathrm g)/(270.0\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}})}
{(0.005000\ \mathrm g)/(633.666\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}})}\\
&=1.00213\\
&\approx\boxed{1.00\ \mathrm{ion\ per\ pore}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
